# ADR-0030 — A Deleted Comment Leaves a Tombstone

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-09-02

**Decision date:** — · **Deciders:** Sprint 13 item 4's author, at construction — **author-verified** ([sprint plan](../../../projects/planning/01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md) §2), so this record stays `Draft` until a second reader has it · **Supersedes:** — · **Superseded by:** — · **Related:** `D-51`, [ADR-0029](0029.%20A%20Comment%20Thread%20Is%20One%20Level%20Deep.md), [ADR-0011](0011.%20A%20Derived%20Surface%20Requires%20the%20Permission%20of%20What%20It%20Derives%20From.md), FR-CMT-004, issue [#253](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/253)

## 1. Context

**Written when the decision was taken** (standard §9).

[SRS](../../requirements/srs.md) §4.11 FR-CMT-004 says *the system shall support comment delete if permitted*, `Should`. [#253](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/253) AC2 requires that **what happens to the replies be decided rather than defaulted**, and AC4 that the delete be soft and *the thread's shape survive it*.

The default was already available and already wrong. Every table in this schema carries `deleted_at`, and every read filters `deleted_at IS NULL`. Applied here, that hides the deleted comment and leaves its replies in the list with a `parentCommentId` pointing at nothing: the screen either drops them too — deleting other people's words on one person's action — or draws answers to a question no reader can see.

Two things bound the choice. [ADR-0029](0029.%20A%20Comment%20Thread%20Is%20One%20Level%20Deep.md) fixes the thread at one level, so *what hangs from this comment* is a single `EXISTS` rather than a subtree walk. And [ADR-0011](0011.%20A%20Derived%20Surface%20Requires%20the%20Permission%20of%20What%20It%20Derives%20From.md) — the rule this project has now answered five times — establishes that a surface withholds what belongs to another surface **rather than hiding the row**, because a reader who is shown less must still be able to tell that something is there.

The comment is behind `comment:read`; a deleted comment's text is behind nothing at all, because nobody should read it. Those are different absences and the payload has to say which.

## 2. Decision

**Kelir deletes a comment softly and leaves its replies untouched. A deleted comment that still has an undeleted reply is served as a tombstone — its author, its time, `body: null` and `deletedAt` set — and one that nobody replied to is not served at all.** The row keeps its text; the withholding happens at the read boundary, in `repository::list_for_document`, which every reader comes through. Replies are never cascaded, never re-parented, and never hidden. This decides what a delete leaves behind, not who may delete — that is authorship plus `comment:delete`, decided in the same item.

## 3. Options Considered

| Option | Summary | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Tombstone while replies live** | The shape survives; the words do not | **Chosen** |
| B. Cascade to the replies | One rule, nothing left dangling | Rejected — destroys other people's words on one person's action |
| C. Re-parent the replies | Keep them, lose the parent | Rejected — with one level, that promotes answers to top-level comments; an answer with no question reads as agreement or as nonsense |
| D. Hide it and leave the replies | The schema's default filter | Rejected — the orphan case above, which is what AC4 exists to prevent |
| E. Tombstone always | Never stop serving a deleted comment | Rejected — a tombstone with nothing under it holds no shape, and the list fills with rows saying only that somebody changed their mind |

### A. Tombstone while replies live — chosen

**A thread is a shape, and the shape belongs to everybody in it.** The deleted comment's own words are the deleter's to take back; the position they occupy is what makes the answers legible, and that is not theirs alone. Serving author and time with no body keeps the second and gives up the first.

**Two rows in the list, decided by one predicate**, which is why the rule lives in the statement rather than in a caller: `deleted_at IS NULL OR EXISTS (an undeleted reply)`. `count_for_document` repeats it verbatim, because a count that did not would report a total the page exceeds — [#279](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/279)'s defect, one module over, and the version of it that reached this file was **seen red** before this shipped.

**`body: null` rather than an empty string.** An empty body is a comment of nothing, which `domain::normalize_body` refuses to store; serving one would make a valid-looking row out of a withheld one. Null with `deletedAt` set says which absence this is — [ADR-0011](0011.%20A%20Derived%20Surface%20Requires%20the%20Permission%20of%20What%20It%20Derives%20From.md)'s `valuesWithheld` in the smallest possible form.

**What it does not solve:** nothing here is an undo. The row keeps its text, so a later release could offer one; this one does not, and a person who deletes by mistake has to say so again.

### B. Cascade to the replies

One rule, no dangling references, and the tidiest schema. It is rejected on the merits rather than the mechanics: a reply is written by somebody else, and a delete that removed it would let the author of a question end a conversation they only started. Soft-deleting the replies too would be recoverable in the database and indistinguishable from B in the product.

### C. Re-parent the replies to the root

Attractive with deep threads and meaningless with one level: the root *is* the top, so re-parenting promotes every answer into a fresh top-level comment. "Yes, they are approved" as a standalone remark on a purchase requisition is worse than either the tombstone or nothing.

### D. Hide it and leave the replies

The path of least code — no predicate, no tombstone, the schema's own default. It produces exactly the orphan AC4 names, and the screen has no honest way to draw it.

### E. Tombstone always

Consistency, at the price of a conversation that accumulates rows saying only that somebody deleted something. Nobody replied to it; there is no shape to preserve and no reader to help.

## 4. Consequences

**Positive** — a delete is safe to offer, because it cannot destroy anybody else's contribution; the screen's confirmation can say so, and does. The audit trail's recorded length stays meaningful, because the text is still on the row. The `activity_events` link written by `Comment.Deleted` still resolves.

**Negative, accepted** — a deleted comment's text remains in the database indefinitely. That is the point of a soft delete, and it is a data-retention question this project has not yet answered for any table; when it does, `comments.body` is one of the columns that must be in the answer. Named here rather than discovered there. A person deleting a comment is told the replies stay, but not that the words are retained — no screen in this product says that about any table today.

**Neutral** — `Comment` gains two nullable fields (`body`, `deletedAt`) and every client must handle a null body. One vocabulary word — *tombstone* — is now load-bearing in three files.

## 5. Compliance

Three tests, all in `kelir-backend/tests/document_comments.rs`: `deleting_a_comment_that_has_replies_leaves_a_tombstone_and_keeps_them` (the shape survives, and the count agrees with the page), `deleting_a_comment_nobody_answered_takes_it_out_of_the_conversation` (the other half, plus the row keeping its text), and `a_deleted_comment_cannot_be_edited`. `says a deleted comment was deleted rather than showing an empty one` holds the screen to it.

The count predicate was **seen red on 2026-09-01** with the tombstone clause dropped from `count_for_document` alone: the page kept two rows and the total became one.

A reviewer adding a read over `comments` checks that it goes through `list_for_document` or repeats its predicate; a reviewer adding a delete anywhere in this product now has a worked answer to *what does this leave behind*.

## 6. Revisit When

A retention policy lands and names `comments.body`; or an undo is asked for, which this decision leaves possible and does not build; or FR-CMT-005 (resolve) arrives and makes *resolved* a second reason to serve a comment differently, at which point the single predicate is the place both rules meet.
