# ADR-0041 — Every Workflow Transition Writes an Outbox Event, and After-Hooks Are Its First Consumer

**Status:** Adopted · **Last updated:** 2026-09-24

**Decision date:** 2026-09-24 · **Deciders:** Product owner · **Supersedes:** [ADR-0036](0036.%20The%20Hook%20Chain%20Ships%20Its%20Before%20Half%20First.md) in part: its after-hook clauses (standard 06 §6.3) · **Superseded by:** — · **Related:** [ADR-0034](0034.%20One%20Delivery%20Attempt,%20Recorded%20Rather%20Than%20Retried.md), [ADR-0036](0036.%20The%20Hook%20Chain%20Ships%20Its%20Before%20Half%20First.md), [ADR-0037](0037.%20ERP%20Posting%20Runs%20Inside%20the%20Document%20Transaction.md), `D-79`, `D-81`, FR-INT-005, FR-INT-007, issue [#519](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/519)

## 1. Context

**The hook chain ships its before half only, and the reason given was the missing outbox** ([ADR-0036](0036.%20The%20Hook%20Chain%20Ships%20Its%20Before%20Half%20First.md) §2, §6). Architectures/01 §12.5 delivers an after-hook *published to the outbox in the same transaction, executed by the worker*, with idempotency, at-least-once delivery, a retry schedule and a circuit breaker. JWSS `actions` register `after_workflow_transition` entries. `hook::service::registration_errors` validates them at publish, and nothing runs them. ADR-0036 §6 names the outbox's arrival as the trigger that reopens this.

**D-79 put the general outbox at the head of Phase 9, and D-81 moved it one sprint to Sprint 20.** [Construction plan 16](../../../projects/planning/16.%20Sprint%2020%20Phase%209%20Outbox%20Construction%20Plan.md) row 3 builds it with its first reader. Plan 16 §8 gives it its own migration, `outbox_events` alone, and requires this record. FR-INT-005, which reads the outbox for webhooks, is `Could`, and no FR names the outbox itself.

What already exists, and bounds the choice:

- **`outbox_events` is specified and unbuilt** ([Database Schema](../../design/02.%20Database%20Schema.md) §12.8). It has one row per event, a five-value status (`PENDING`, `PROCESSING`, `PROCESSED`, `FAILED`, `DEAD_LETTER`), `attempt_count`, `next_attempt_at` and `last_error`. Its `payload_json` is a full [Event Envelope Schema](../../schema/Event%20Envelope%20Schema.md) envelope.
- **A worker shape exists.** `notification::worker` and `attachment::worker` are each a loop over their own table, and each exposes a `pass` a test can drive. [ADR-0034](0034.%20One%20Delivery%20Attempt,%20Recorded%20Rather%20Than%20Retried.md) chose one recorded attempt for notifications, with no retry.
- **A transition is one transaction.** `engine::fire` (decided) and `engine::advance_automatically` (a service task's `AUTO` edge) each write a `workflow_history` row. [#514](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/514) takes `FOR KEY SHARE` on the role inside that transaction.
- **The three `core:` handlers are before-kind.** `continue_always`, `set_form_field` and `reject_when` return `CONTINUE`, `MODIFY` and `REJECT`. `actions` accepts all three at publish today, though `MODIFY` and `REJECT` mean nothing once the transition has committed.

**The #519 acceptance criteria name five questions no document answers:**

1. Which transitions write a row.
2. Where a workflow-sourced handler's failures are counted for the circuit breaker. Database Schema §13 counts them in `plugin_error_logs`, which is a plugin table.
3. How an after-hook's *success or failure* (LHCS §5.2) maps onto `document_hook_executions.result`, whose CHECK allows only `CONTINUE`, `MODIFY`, `REJECT` and `ERROR`.
4. What `aggregateType` and `eventType` the envelope carries.
5. Whether a row is the event or its delivery. EES §5.1 says exhausted retries *dead-letter the delivery, never the event*, while §12.8 lets the event row itself become `DEAD_LETTER`.

**The circuit breaker's N is undefined** (architectures/01 §12.5), and **no document gives a retry schedule for the outbox worker.**

## 2. Decision

**Kelir writes one `Workflow.Transitioned` event to `outbox_events` in the transaction of every committed workflow transition, decided or automatic, and a new worker delivers each event to the `after_workflow_transition` chain.**

- **The event.**
  - `aggregateType` is `WORKFLOW_INSTANCE` and `aggregateId` is the instance.
  - `sequence` is the transition's position in that instance's `workflow_history`.
  - The payload is the `Workflow.*` profile, with its own `action` set, plus `fromState` and `toState`. It carries no form data (EES §4.2).
- **The chain.** The worker resolves the chain **at delivery**. It merges the transition's own `actions`, read from the definition revision the instance runs, with the registry's `after_workflow_transition` entries as they stand when the event is delivered. It invokes the chain with LHCS §4's payload, built from the document as it is at delivery.
- **Retries.** A delivery succeeds when every handler it runs succeeds. Any failure or timeout makes the whole delivery retryable, and each retry runs the whole chain again (LHCS §5.2 requires handlers to be idempotent).
  - Each handler has a 2-second budget and the chain has 5 seconds. These are the before-hook defaults architectures/01 §12.5 sets, applied to after-hooks, because it gives after-hooks none.
  - A failed attempt sets the row `FAILED`, with `next_attempt_at` 30 s × 2^(attempt − 1) away: 30 s, 1 min, 2 min, 4 min, 8 min.
  - **The sixth failed attempt makes the row `DEAD_LETTER`.**
  - A claimed row is `PROCESSING` under a five-minute lease held in `next_attempt_at`, and a row whose lease has expired is claimed again. That is how a crashed worker's work is redelivered rather than lost.
- **The execution log.** Every handler run is a `document_hook_executions` row. **Success is recorded as `CONTINUE`, and failure or timeout as `ERROR`** with `error_message`.
- **The circuit breaker is derived from that log**, not from `plugin_error_logs` and not from a new table.
  - It opens when a handler's last five executions for (tenant, `hook_name`, `handler_reference`) are all `ERROR`. Holders of `ROLE-ADMIN` in the tenant are notified once, by the execution that opened it.
  - While it is open, the handler is not run. Its event counts as a retryable failure, so the event is delayed, not dropped.
  - Ten minutes after the handler's last `ERROR`, one trial execution is allowed. A success closes the breaker. A failure is another `ERROR`, so the breaker stays open and the ten minutes start again.
- **Handlers declare their kind.** Each `core:` handler declares whether it serves before, after or both, and `actions` naming a before-only handler is refused at publish with `HANDLER_KIND_MISMATCH`.
  - `continue_always` serves both. `set_form_field` and `reject_when` are before-only.
  - A definition published before this rule that reaches a before-only handler at delivery gets an `ERROR` for it. That failure is permanent: it does not make the delivery retryable, and the rest of the chain runs.
- **A row is the event plus the state of its in-process dispatch.** `DEAD_LETTER` marks the dispatch as exhausted and keeps the envelope, so the event is never discarded. That is how this record reads EES §5.1.
- **Out of scope.**
  - Every other event family, webhooks and plugin dispatch.
  - Any screen.
  - `guards` on decided transitions, which stay as ADR-0036 left them.
  - The three boundaries in §4.

## 3. Options Considered

| Option | Summary | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A. One event per transition; the worker resolves the chain** | The outbox is an event stream, and after-hooks are its first consumer | **Chosen** |
| B. A row only when the after-chain is non-empty | The outbox is a queue of hook work | Rejected: FR-INT-005 would add a second write to the engine for the same fact |
| C. Resolve the chain at commit and store it in the row | The row carries its handler list | Rejected: puts non-envelope data in `payload_json`, which EES S4 makes the webhook body |
| D. A breaker in its own state table, reset by an administrator | The handler stays off until a person turns it on | Rejected: needs a route and a screen this row does not have |
| E. Count failures in `plugin_error_logs` | Database Schema §13 as written | Rejected: a plugin table, and there are no plugins |
| F. Run any handler in `actions` and ignore its result | Nothing refused | Rejected: a `REJECT` after commit looks like a veto and is not one |
| G. Retry only the handlers that failed | Per-handler delivery state | Rejected for now: needs a per-event column `document_hook_executions` does not have |

### A. One event per transition, resolved at delivery: chosen

**The fact the engine knows at commit is that a transition happened**, not which handlers will care. Writing that fact once, for every transition, is what makes the table the general outbox D-79 scheduled rather than a private queue with a general name. A webhook subscription (FR-INT-005) or a search indexer reads the same rows later without touching the engine again. ADR-0036 rejected its option E for exactly that reason: two queues to reconcile.

**Resolving at delivery follows from it.** The row carries only the envelope. The definition revision is pinned on the instance (architectures/01 §12.5, *Definition version*), so the transition's own `actions` are the ones that were in force. Registry entries are read as they stand when the event is delivered. A registry entry added between commit and delivery therefore runs, and one removed does not. That is stated as a property, not hidden.

**What it does not solve:** a handler sees the document as it is at delivery, not as it was at commit. The envelope may not carry form data (EES §4.2), and a snapshot would be a second copy of it.

### B. A row only when a chain exists

Fewer rows, and the table would hold only work. The cost is that the table stops being an outbox. The first consumer that is not a hook needs its own write in `fire` and `advance_automatically`, and the engine's transaction would then decide per consumer whether to write. The volume argument is weak: one row per `workflow_history` row, and a history row is already written.

### C. Resolve at commit

It would freeze the chain the transition saw, and make delivery independent of later registry edits. It needs the handler list inside `payload_json` or a second column. The first breaks EES S4: the envelope must be the bytes a webhook receives. The second is a schema addition for a guarantee nothing asks for.

### D. An administrator resets the breaker

This is the more conservative breaker: a handler that failed five times stays off until a person has looked. It needs state (a table or a column on a registry that `actions` do not live in) and a route to reset it. With no screen in this row, the only reset would be an API call nobody knows about. A cool-down with one trial is the standard shape and needs neither. And because an open breaker delays rather than drops, the cost of a false close is one more logged `ERROR`.

### E. `plugin_error_logs`

Database Schema §13 says consecutive failures are *derived from `plugin_error_logs` by `hook_name`*. That was written when every handler that could fail repeatedly was a plugin. `actions` are `WORKFLOW`-sourced `core:` handlers, `plugin_error_logs` requires a `plugin_id`, and no plugin exists. `document_hook_executions` already records *every execution, any source, any result* (LHCS §7), so it is the one log a breaker for all sources can read.

### F. Run anything, ignore the result

Nothing is refused and nothing stored breaks. But `reject_when` in an `actions` list reads as a veto and cannot be one, and `set_form_field` reads as a write and is not one. A handler that looks as if it does something and does not is [ADR-0016](0016.%20An%20Unenforceable%20Rule%20Refuses%20Rather%20Than%20Passes.md)'s failure. The kind is refused where the author is.

### G. Retry only what failed

Handlers that already succeeded would not run again, and a chain of three with one failing would not repeat two successes six times. It needs `document_hook_executions` to know which event an execution belonged to. It has no such column: EES §2 says `correlationId` is present there, and Database Schema §6.12 has none. LHCS §5.2 makes idempotency the handler's duty. So this is recorded as the revisit trigger it is (§6), not built.

## 4. Consequences

**Positive**
- JWSS `actions` run, and ADR-0036's main negative (*a definition author's `actions` are validated and never run*) expires.
- Every transition becomes an event another consumer can read without changing the engine.
- The retry schedule, dead letter and circuit breaker are properties of one worker, which is where §12.5 puts them, and not of each handler.
- The breaker needs no new table. It reads the log LHCS §7 already requires.

**Negative**
- **One outbox row per transition**, most of them for chains that turn out to be empty. The worker marks those `PROCESSED` with nothing run.
- **A retry reruns the whole chain.** A handler that is not idempotent despite LHCS §5.2 has its effect applied again.
- **A handler sees the document at delivery.** An after-hook cannot know what the form data was at the moment of the transition.
- **Registry edits made between commit and delivery apply** to events already written.
- **Dead letters have no screen.** `DEAD_LETTER` rows are visible by SQL only until FR-INT-006 or a later row gives them one.
- **A definition published before this rule** and naming `set_form_field` or `reject_when` in `actions` now logs an `ERROR` for that handler on every transition it covers, instead of silently doing nothing.
- **Two documents change meaning.** The breaker note in Database Schema §13 stops being about `plugin_error_logs` alone, and EES §5.1 is read, not amended. If a later reader finds that reading wrong, the correction is a superseding record.

**Neutral**
- A new module, `modules::outbox`: the writer, the worker, and a dispatch table from `eventType` to consumer.
- A new event type, `Workflow.Transitioned`. EES §3 permits new types in a minor platform version.
- A kind on each core handler, and `idx_document_hook_executions_breaker` on (tenant, `hook_name`, `handler_reference`, `executed_at`), so the breaker's last-five lookup is not a scan.
- `FAILED` joins `idx_outbox_events_pending`'s predicate, so a row waiting for its retry is found by the same index.

**Three boundaries**, stated as plan 16 §8 requires:
- **Notifications stay on their own table**, delivered by `notification::worker` under ADR-0034. The breaker's report is an in-app notification written through `notification::service::notify`, not an outbox event.
- **ERP posting does not move onto the outbox** (ADR-0037 §6): it runs inside the document transaction.
- **FR-INT-007's per-integration `external_systems.retry_policy_json` is not this schedule.** It governs calls to one external system. This schedule governs in-process dispatch.

## 5. Compliance

`tests/outbox.rs` and `tests/workflow_system_tasks.rs` hold the decision. Each guard is seen red under a reintroduced defect ([coding standard](../../standards/01.%20Coding%20Standard.md) §2.9). The tests assert that:

- A decided and an automatic transition each leave one `Workflow.Transitioned` row whose `payload_json` validates against the EES meta-schema, and a rolled-back transition leaves none.
- A handler that fails is retried on the schedule and dead-lettered on the sixth failure. A `PROCESSING` row past its lease is delivered again.
- Five consecutive `ERROR`s open the breaker and notify the tenant's administrators once. A trial after the cool-down that succeeds closes it.
- `actions` naming `core:set_form_field` is refused at publish with `HANDLER_KIND_MISMATCH`.

A reviewer checks one thing no test can: **a new event is written through `outbox::write`, inside the business transaction**, and not by a consumer calling another consumer.

## 6. Revisit When

- **A second consumer reads `outbox_events`** (FR-INT-005's webhooks, or a plugin runtime). A row's status then has to mean the fan-out, with per-consumer delivery state where EES §5.1 puts it.
- **A handler turns out not to be idempotent in practice**, or chains grow long enough that whole-chain retries matter. Per-handler retry (option G) then earns a column.
