# ADR-0033 — A Governed Record Parks at Pending Approval

**Status:** Adopted · **Last updated:** 2026-09-03

**Decision date:** — · **Deciders:** Sprint 13 item 6's author, with the product owner deciding §3's question at construction — **author-verified** ([sprint plan](../../../projects/planning/01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md) §2) until 2026-09-03, when the [Sprint 13 independent pass](../../../projects/verifications/13.%20Sprint%2013%20Independent%20Pass.md) read it and the code under it. **Adopted on that reading**, which is what this line said it was waiting for — and the reading found §4 incomplete rather than wrong, so the correction is in §4 and the decision itself stands · **Supersedes:** — · **Superseded by:** — · **Related:** `D-55`, `D-43`, [ADR-0020](0020.%20The%20Workflow%20Owns%20the%20Document%20Status.md), [ADR-0028](0028.%20A%20Definition%20Is%20Refused%20at%20Save%20Rather%20Than%20at%20Render.md), FR-MDM-010, issue [#255](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/255)

## 1. Context

**Written when the decision was taken** (standard §9).

[SRS](../../requirements/srs.md) §4.4 FR-MDM-010 says *the system shall route master-data changes through the document workflow*, `Should`. It has been the MDM epic's last unbuilt row since Phase 2, re-targeted twice — to Sprint 11, cut, then to Sprint 13 by **D-43** on the grounds that it needed the whole workflow phase to exist first.

By the time it was built, three things existed that decide its shape:

* **`document_types.target_entity_type`**, created by `0015_document.sql` in Sprint 4 with the comment *set for master-data change document types*, and **read by nothing** for nine sprints.
* **`documents.entity_type` / `entity_id`** (FR-DOC-011, [#170](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/170)) — a document already names the master-data record it is about, with a write-time existence check that holds the row.
* **`mdm_*.record_status`**, and specifically `PENDING_APPROVAL`. `master_data::domain::record_status` — written for [#99](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/99), before any workflow existed — reserved that value in as many words: *it is the workflow's state (FR-MDM-010, Phase 5+) and nothing today can approve anything*, and it named `RecordStatus::may_move_to` as **where that would change when the workflow landed**.

[#255](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/255) AC2 turns that reservation into a question that must be answered rather than inherited: *the record's `record_status` and the document's status are not two records of one fact* — and if that sentence changes, it changes deliberately.

And AC6 draws the boundary the answer must not cross: **nothing in the workflow engine learns about master data.** A `match` on entity type inside the engine is [#178](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/178) AC4's failure repeated.

## 2. Decision

**Kelir parks a governed master-data record at `PENDING_APPROVAL` for the life of its change document.** Submitting the change moves the record there in the submit's own transaction; the record is not written until the process closes; a direct `PUT` on a parked record is refused; approving applies the change and moves the record to `ACTIVE`, and refusing puts it back exactly where it was, in the transaction that closed the process.

`may_move_to` gains the workflow's path, and **the transition surface refuses `PENDING_APPROVAL` at either end**: parking is a process's move, not a person's.

The dependency runs **engine → document → master data**. The engine projects a status onto a document, as it does for every document; the document module — which has known about master data since FR-DOC-011 — asks `master_data` to settle whatever change that document carried. The `match` on entity type exists only at the far end.

## 3. Options Considered

| Option | Summary | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The record parks at `PENDING_APPROVAL`** | The reserved value, used for what it was reserved for | **Chosen** |
| B. The record does not move; the lock is derived from the open document | One fact, in one place | Rejected — a cross-module query on every master-data write, and a record that cannot say *change in flight* without a join |
| C. A `locked_by_document_id` column on each record | Explicit, and no status change | Rejected — a second mechanism for a question `record_status` already answers, on every governed table |
| D. The engine applies the change itself | Fewest hops | Rejected — AC6 exactly: a `match` on entity type inside the workflow engine |

### A. Parking — chosen

**The value exists for this**, and using it is what makes `record_status` a lifecycle rather than a column with an unreachable state. The refusal of direct edits then costs nothing: `update_party` already reads the record it is about, so the guard is one comparison on a value already in hand — no new permission, no query into another module, and the same rule holds for any future route that parks a record.

**It is not a second copy of the document's status**, which is AC2's requirement and worth stating precisely: the document's status answers *how far has this change got*, and the record's answers *is this record editable*. They move together at two moments and mean different things throughout — a rejected change leaves the document at `REJECTED` for ever and the record back at `ACTIVE`, which is exactly the two facts diverging.

**What makes them safe to move together** is that both writes happen in the transaction that caused them. A record parked by a submit that then rolled back would be a record nobody can edit and no approver can find a document for.

### B. Deriving the lock from the open document

Attractive because it keeps one fact in one place. It costs a cross-module read on **every** master-data update — including the overwhelming majority of records that are not governed at all — and it makes *does this record have a change in flight* a question the master-data surface cannot answer without joining `documents`. The record's own list, its own screen and its own API would all have to reach into another module to render a state.

### C. A dedicated lock column

Honest, and duplicative: `record_status` is already the column that answers *what state is this record in*, and a second one would leave two answers to keep in step on four tables — the two-meanings-one-column failure this schema has refused before, inverted into one-meaning-two-columns.

### D. The engine applying the change

The shortest path and the one AC6 names. The engine would have to know that a document can be *about* a party, which table a party is in, and what a party's fields are. `modules::workflow` currently knows about documents and nothing else, and this decision keeps that true.

## 4. Consequences

**Positive** — the governance is enforceable rather than advisory: a governed record cannot be edited around its own approval, and the refusal is under the permission that already governs the write. The configuration is a column that has existed since Sprint 4 (AC3). The change and its outcome are one row in `mdm_change_requests`, which is also the record's own history of what has been asked of it (AC4).

**Negative, accepted** — **a governed change carries the record's scalar fields only.** `statusId`, `externalId`, `description` and `additionalAttributes` for a party; `name`, `facilityType` and `additionalAttributes` for a facility. The sub-aggregates — contacts, identifications, relationships, classifications, a facility's address — are written by multi-statement service logic that this process does not reproduce inside a workflow's closing transaction, and a change naming one is **refused at raise** with the field named. Accepting them and applying the scalars would approve a change to a supplier's contacts and then not make it.

**Negative** — **the transition surface's answer for `PENDING_APPROVAL` changed from 422 to 409.** It used to be an illegal transition; it is now a legal transition refused by the surface. Any client that branched on the code sees a different one. It is a `Should`-priority surface with no known client, and the message says what to do instead.

**Negative** — a record parked by a change whose document is then deleted would stay parked. `documents` cascade-deletes `mdm_change_requests`, so the row goes and the record's status does not. Nothing in the product deletes a submitted document — the discard path refuses anything but a draft ([#278](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/278)) — so this is reachable only by hand, and it is named here rather than left to be discovered.

> **This paragraph looked at one end of the relationship, and the other end was the defect** (**D-60**, 2026-09-03). *Document deleted, record stranded* was considered above and correctly found unreachable. ***Record* deleted, *document* stranded** was not considered, and was reachable by `master-data:party:delete` — an ordinary grant. The delete answered 204, and the approver's decision then failed with a 500 on **both** `APPROVE` and `REJECT` for ever, because `settle`'s `move_record_status_in` carries `deleted_at IS NULL` and matched nothing; the instance stayed `RUNNING` and the task `ASSIGNED`, which nobody including an administrator could move.
>
> Found by the [Sprint 13 independent pass](../../../projects/verifications/13.%20Sprint%2013%20Independent%20Pass.md), finding 1, and fixed the same day: `delete_party` and `delete_facility` now take `refuse_if_awaiting_approval` under `lock_record_status`'s `FOR UPDATE`, which is the read `raise` already takes, so the delete and the submit serialise rather than merely checking.
>
> **The lesson is the shape of the reasoning, not the missing line.** Citing #278 to dismiss one direction made the other direction look considered. A relationship between two records has two ends, and a consequence written about one of them should say which.

**Neutral** — `PENDING_APPROVAL` becomes reachable, which means every screen rendering `record_status` now has a sixth value to render. The vocabulary has been in the `CHECK` since `0008`.

## 5. Compliance

`tests/master_data_governance.rs` — nine tests: the record parks and is not written; a direct edit is refused; one change at a time; an approval applies it and activates the record; a refusal leaves an `ACTIVE` record active and a `DRAFT` record draft; an ordinary document raises nothing; a type naming an ungovernable entity is refused at save; a governed document naming no record is refused at submit.

**Four mutations seen red** on 2026-09-02, including the one that matters most — `governance::raise` never called, which reddens six of the nine.

AC6 is held by a fact a reviewer can check in one command:

```text
grep -rn "modules::master_data" kelir-backend/src/modules/workflow/
```

returns nothing — no import, no call, no type. (`grep master_data` alone returns one line, in a doc comment in `workflow::domain::task` that cites a rule from that module's prose. A sentence is not a dependency, and the narrower grep is the one that answers the question.)

## 6. Revisit When

A sub-aggregate change has to go through approval — a supplier's bank details are the obvious first — which needs the update path factored so it can run inside a caller's transaction, and is a bigger change than this one; or a third governed entity arrives, which is a migration and a match arm in one module and is the shape this decision was built to make cheap.
