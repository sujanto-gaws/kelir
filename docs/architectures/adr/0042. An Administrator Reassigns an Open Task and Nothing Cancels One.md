# ADR-0042 — An Administrator Reassigns an Open Task, and Nothing Cancels One

**Status:** Adopted · **Last updated:** 2026-10-01 (the lock order, [#619](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/619))

**Decision date:** 2026-09-26 · **Deciders:** Product owner (the decidability check: 2026-09-26) · **Supersedes:** — · **Superseded by:** — · **Related:** `D-89`, `D-91`, FR-WF-017, FR-WF-009, FR-TASK-008, issues [#512](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/512), [#532](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/532), [#508](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/508), [#529](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/529), [#619](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/619)

## 1. Context

**A role delete is refused while an open task needs the role** (`D-89`, [#487](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/487)). `identity::service::delete_role` locks the role `FOR UPDATE`, counts the open tasks that need it, and answers 409 while the count is not zero. A task needs a role when it is offered to that role and unclaimed, or when an edge out of its instance's current state names the role in `allowedBy`, claimed or not ([#529](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/529)). Until this change the 409 told the administrator to have the tasks decided, and nothing else could clear it. No route changes who holds a task the caller does not hold.

**`D-91` (2026-09-18) answered what an administrator may do instead.** They reassign an open task to a live role or user, under a permission of its own, audited and recorded. **No route cancels a single task**: that would be an administrator deciding a workflow outcome, which is `D-89`'s rejected option B one layer down. `D-91` makes the route's shape an ADR, because it constrains the workflow engine. On 2026-09-25 the product owner added **FR-WF-017 (`Should`)** to the SRS and put the record in `workflow_task_history` rather than `workflow_history`. Construction plan 17 §8 named the permission `workflow:task:reassign`.

**What the engine already has:**

- **One answer to *who is this task for*.** `workflow::service::assignment` resolves a JWSS rule in three steps: `normalize` turns the rule into a principal against the document, `direct` checks the principal against this tenant's tables, and `redirect` applies an open delegation window. `ResolvedAssignment` has two constructors, a user or a role, so no resolution names both. The module header warns that a second answer would be a second routing rule.
- **One lock discipline against the delete.** `direct` reads a role `FOR KEY SHARE`, and `hold_deciding_roles` does the same for every role an edge names, so a transition and a delete wait on each other (#513, #514, #528).
- **One precedent for moving a task without moving the process.** The hand-off (`POST /api/v1/workflow/tasks/{id}/delegation`, FR-WF-009, FR-TASK-008) ~~locks only the task~~ locked only the task until [#619](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/619), and now locks its instance first, writes a `workflow_task_history` row with `action = 'DELEGATE'`, and fires no transition. It is taken by the task's holder, under `workflow:task:execute`, and only to a user.

**A reassign has no document context.** `resolve` and `permits` take an `AssignmentContext` built from the document: the `OWNER` rule and the department scopes read it. The reassign names its target directly, so it has nothing to normalize.

## 2. Decision

**Kelir lets an administrator move an open task to one live user or one live role who could decide it, through the engine's own assignment checks, and has no route that cancels a task.**

- **The route is `POST /api/v1/workflow/tasks/{id}/reassign`**, guarded by **`workflow:task:reassign`** (`0048_workflow_task_reassign_permission.sql`). The body is `{ "userId" }` or `{ "roleCode" }`, with an optional `comment`. Naming both or neither is a 422, and a null target counts as none.
- **The target goes through `assignment::direct`, by a narrower entry point.** `assignment::reassign_to` maps the target to a principal and calls `direct`, with no `normalize` and no `redirect`. A role is named by its code, as a rule names it. A dead or unknown user or role is refused as `ASSIGNMENT_UNRESOLVED` on the field that named it. `direct`'s checks are unchanged. It now takes the caller's refusal wording, so a transition and a reassign raise the same code with words about their own situation.
- **The target must be able to decide the task** (the product owner's decision of 2026-09-26). The **decision edges** are the `APPROVE`, `REJECT` and `RETURN` transitions out of the instance's current state, read from the published definition the instance is pinned to, as `decide` reads it. The target must satisfy **at least one**; otherwise the reassign is a 422 `TARGET_CANNOT_DECIDE` on `userId` or `roleCode`, naming the state and what its decisions need.
  - **A user** is measured by `assignment::permits`, the check the decision itself makes, with a context built from the document as `decide` builds it (`created_by`, `requested_for_department_id`) and nobody on whose behalf. `USER`, `ROLE`, `OWNER` and `DEPARTMENT_ROLE`, department scope included, mean exactly what they will mean when the user decides.
  - **A role** passes an edge that **names it**: a `ROLE` rule for its code, or a `DEPARTMENT_ROLE` rule for its code whose department resolves, both judged by `assignment::names_role` through `normalize` and `direct`. **An `OWNER` or `USER` edge does not pass a role target**: holding a role makes nobody the document's creator or the named user, and a task offered to a role whose holders could only decide it by coincidence is the stranded task this check exists to refuse. For a `DEPARTMENT_ROLE` edge, which holders are in the department is a question about each holder, answered when one claims or decides; the check asks only that the edge is about this role in a department that exists.
  - **An edge with no `allowedBy`** admits anybody, as the engine checks nothing on it, so it passes every target. **An edge whose own rule no longer resolves** (a deleted role) counts as not passed, since the decision would refuse it too.
  - **A state with no decision edges is not judged**, and the reassign is allowed. A `RETURNED` state whose task is the owner's correction leaves only by `RESUBMIT`, taken on the document rather than decided on the task, so there is no decision to strand.
  - The instance and the definition are read after the task lock~~, and the instance is not locked: the engine takes the instance before the task, and nothing but a decision, which waits on the task this reassign holds, moves a state with an open task~~. **The instance is locked, before the task** (corrected 2026-10-01, [#619](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/619)), so the state the check reads cannot move under it: a decision, the only thing that moves a state with an open task, waits on the same instance lock.
- **Lock order: the instance, then the task, then the role** (corrected 2026-10-01, [#619](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/619)). The reassign locks the instance `FOR UPDATE` (`lock_instance`), then the task `FOR UPDATE` (`lock_task`), then resolves the target, which reads a role `FOR KEY SHARE` in the same transaction, then writes. **The instance is taken whether the path names it or not**: the `workflow_task_history` row's foreign key to `workflow_instances` takes `FOR KEY SHARE` on the instance, which conflicts with a decision's `FOR UPDATE`. A decision takes the instance, then the task, before `engine::fire` resolves an edge's role, so the order is the same. The claim and the hand-off take the same order. The instance id is read on the pool before the transaction, and a task whose instance under the lock is not that one is refused with a 409. **Since the instance lock serializes every task path first, the task's own `FOR UPDATE` is now a backstop**: removing it reddens no test, and that is accepted, not a gap (`tests/task_inbox.rs`'s mutation table). `delete_role` takes the role `FOR UPDATE` and never locks a task or an instance, so it sits at the end of the order and closes no cycle. A delete that commits first leaves the role unmatched, and the reassign is refused. A reassign that commits first is counted by the delete. Under the decidability check a role target is always a role the task's own edges name, so the delete count already holds it through the edge; the lock still orders the two, and the tests assert the wait in both orders.
  - ~~**Lock order: the task, then the role.** The reassign locks the task `FOR UPDATE` (`lock_task`), then resolves the target, which reads a role `FOR KEY SHARE` in the same transaction, then writes. A decision takes the task before `engine::fire` resolves an edge's role, so the order is the same.~~ This was the order until 2026-10-01. It listed only the explicit locks. The history row's foreign key took the instance after the task, so a reassign and a decision deadlocked, and the decision answered 500 (record 20, finding 2). The claim and the hand-off had the same shape.
- **The holder changes through the resolution, every column at once.** `assignee_user_id`, `candidate_role_id`, `candidate_department_id` and `delegated_from_user_id` are all written from the `ResolvedAssignment`. A reassign to a user clears the role. A reassign to a role clears the assignee, so a claimed task becomes unclaimed and offered to the role. `delegated_from_user_id` is cleared, because a reassigned task is nobody's work done on their behalf.
- **The status follows the holder, as at creation.** `ASSIGNED` with an assignee, `CREATED` without one: `insert_task`'s rule, which is also what `claim` requires of a task it takes.
- **Only an open task moves, and a closed one is refused before its target is looked at.** Straight after `lock_task`, a task whose status is not open is a 409 naming the status. The target is resolved and judged only after that: the decidability check reads the instance's *current* state, which a closed task of a multi-stage workflow has left, so judged first, a closed task was a 422 about a decision it would never take (found 2026-09-28). The write still carries `status IN ('CREATED', 'ASSIGNED', 'IN_PROGRESS')`, and zero rows is a 409, as with `claim`, `complete` and `delegate`; under the task lock it is the backstop.
- **The record is one `workflow_task_history` row**, `action = 'REASSIGN'`, with the old and new status and the comment. **No `workflow_history` row**, because the process did not move. The column has no `CHECK`, so the value needs no migration. It is a `TaskHistoryAction`, not a `TransitionAction`, because no definition can declare it.
- **Audited** as `Workflow.TaskReassigned`, after the commit, with the holder columns before and after and whether a comment was given. The comment itself stays on the history row, for the hand-off's reason (`D-12`, `D-32`).
- **Department scope is out.** A reassign to a role offers the task to every holder of the role, and clears any department the task carried.
- **A provisioned tenant's `ROLE-ADMIN` gets the permission.** Provisioning grants the catalogue except the withheld families (`D-18`), and this is not added to them. A tenant's administrator can then follow the 409 in their own tenant.
- **No route cancels a single task** (`D-91`), and none is added.

**Finding a task and moving it are two permissions.** The list of a role's open tasks, `GET /api/v1/identity/roles/{id}/open-tasks` ([#532](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/532), plan 17 row 8), is a read guarded by `identity:role:delete`. The reassign is a write guarded by `workflow:task:reassign`. Neither implies the other, and neither route checks the other's permission. **The reassign dialog opens from row 8's list for a caller who holds both.** A caller who holds only the list's permission sees the tasks and no reassign action. A caller who holds only `workflow:task:reassign` can still call the route with a task id. The dialog is built in a later commit on #512's branch.

## 3. Options Considered

| Option | Summary | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A. `direct` through a narrow entry point** | Map the target to a principal and call `direct`; no `normalize`, no `redirect` | **Chosen** |
| B. Reimplement the liveness checks in the route | Two `SELECT`s in the task service | Rejected — a second answer to *is this live*, which the module header forbids |
| C. Build a JWSS rule and call `resolve` | `{ "assigneeType": "USER", … }` from the request | Rejected — needs a document context the route does not have, and applies a delegation window |
| **D. Clear the other holder column** | Every holder column from the resolution | **Chosen** |
| E. Write only the named column | `UPDATE … SET assignee_user_id = $3` | Rejected — a task naming a user and a role |
| **F. `workflow_task_history`** | One `REASSIGN` row | **Chosen** (product owner, 2026-09-25) |
| G. `workflow_history` | A row with `from_state = to_state` | Rejected — that table records moves of the process |
| **H. Role-only** | No department scope | **Chosen** |
| I. Department-scoped reassign | A `departmentCode` beside `roleCode` | Rejected for now — no requirement asks for it |
| J. A cancel route | Close a single task | Rejected — `D-91`, `D-89` option B |
| **K. Check the target against the decision edges, exactly** | `permits` for a user; *names the role* for a role; at least one edge | **Chosen** (product owner, 2026-09-26) |
| L. No decidability check | Any live target | Rejected — a reassign could strand the task it was meant to free |
| M. Every decision edge | The target must satisfy all of them | Rejected — refuses a holder who can reject but not approve, whose decision still moves the task |
| N. A role passes `OWNER` and `USER` edges | Treat forms that name no role as open to any role | Rejected — the role's holders could not decide them |
| O. Refuse a state with no decision edges | Nothing to satisfy, so nothing passes | Rejected — nothing is stranded, and the owner's correction could never be moved |

### A. `direct` through a narrow entry point — chosen

`direct` already answers *is this user live in this tenant* and *is this role live, and hold it*, and its `FOR KEY SHARE` is the lock `D-89` depends on. `reassign_to` reaches it without the two steps that need a rule: there is no rule to normalize and no document to normalize against. `redirect` is left out on purpose. A delegation window redirects work a rule routes to a person on leave. A reassign is an administrator choosing a person, so a window standing in the way would override the choice. The refusal is passed in as a closure because `direct`'s old wording talked about a transition creating a task. The code, `ASSIGNMENT_UNRESOLVED`, is the same, so a client handles one refusal.

What it does not solve on its own: `direct` says a target is live, not that it can decide the task. Option K adds that.

### B. Reimplement the checks in the route

Two small queries, and no change to `assignment.rs`. It is exactly the second dialect the module header warns about: a change to what *live* means, such as `users.status`, would reach one path and not the other. The two copies would also each have to take the lock, and an unlocked read during a delete was `D-89`'s original bug.

### C. Build a rule and call `resolve`

It reuses every step, but it needs an `AssignmentContext`, which means reading the document for a route that has no use for it. It would also apply `redirect`, so a reassign to somebody on leave would land on their delegate, which is not what the administrator chose. `normalize`'s refusals, such as *this document has no creator*, cannot occur for a named target.

### D. Clear the other holder column — chosen

`ResolvedAssignment`'s invariant is that at most one of user and role is set, and the task row is written from it. Writing every holder column, including the ones it clears, keeps the invariant at the write and not only at construction.

### E. Write only the named column

A reassign to a role would leave the old assignee, so the task would stay claimed, `claim` would refuse its new role's holders, and the old assignee would keep deciding it. The role would not count it either, because a claimed task does not need its candidate role (#529).

### F. `workflow_task_history` — chosen

§7.7 is *what happened to this one task*, and it already records the hand-off with a comment. A reassign changes who holds a task and fires no transition, so this is the table. It needs no migration.

### G. `workflow_history`

`D-91`'s first wording. §7.11 holds one row per move of the process, written in the transition's transaction, and every reader of it, the document's history screen included, reads a row as a move. A reassign row there would need `from_state = to_state` and an action no definition declares.

### H. Role-only — chosen

`candidate_department_id` exists for a `DEPARTMENT_ROLE` rule, which resolves its department from the document. An administrator naming a role names the people who hold it. Clearing the column, rather than keeping a department the task carried, means the offer is exactly what the request said.

### I. Department-scoped reassign

It would narrow a reassign to one department's holders of a role. No requirement asks for it, and it would add a second lookup and a second field. It can be added beside `roleCode` without changing this route's other behaviour.

### J. A cancel route

It would close a task and leave the process with no open task, which is an administrator deciding the outcome of an approval. `D-89` rejected that for the delete, and `D-91` for the task. The reassign never accepts a null target for the same reason.

### K. The decision edges, exactly — chosen

A reassign exists to free a task somebody cannot act on, so accepting one that leaves the task held by somebody else who cannot act on it defeats it. Measuring a user with `permits` makes the reassign's answer and the decision's answer the same function, `DEPARTMENT_ROLE` scope included. A role cannot be measured with `permits`, which needs a person, so it is measured by whether an edge is about it, through the same `normalize` and `direct`. It costs two reads, the instance and the definition, and one per edge's role, each `FOR KEY SHARE` as a decision takes it.

### L. No decidability check

This record's first version: the route was cheaper and needed no document context, and the cost was stated as a negative. The product owner reversed it before the merge, on the ground in K.

### M. Every decision edge

It would refuse a user who may reject but not approve, although their rejection moves the task and frees the role. One decision is enough to un-strand a task.

### N. A role passes `OWNER` and `USER` edges

It would accept a reassign to any role on an owner's correction, leaving it offered to holders none of whom can decide it unless one happens to be the creator.

### O. Refuse a state with no decision edges

It would make the owner's correction task in `RETURNED` impossible to move, for a rule whose purpose is to stop decisions being stranded, when that task has no decision.

## 4. Consequences

**Positive**

- A 409 from a role delete has a way through that does not need the task decided. The message and the User Manual name it.
- A reassign never leaves a task with a decision nobody holding it can take, judged by the function the decision uses.
- The liveness check and its lock are shared with the engine, so a change to either reaches the reassign.
- The route writes no outbox event~~ and holds no instance lock, so it does not block decisions on other tasks of the same process~~. It does hold the instance lock since [#619](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/619); see the negative below.

**Negative**

- **The check reads the document, the instance and the definition**, which the route otherwise would not. Two of them are on the pool before the transaction, as `decide` reads them, and the definition and instance inside it.
- **A role target is refused for an `OWNER`- or `USER`-only state**, although one of its holders might be the creator. The administrator reassigns to that person by name instead.
- **A `DEPARTMENT_ROLE` edge passes a role target without looking at holders**, so a role whose holders are all in other departments can still receive the task. Its holders can claim it, and their decisions are refused. Refusing that would need a count of holders per department at reassign time, which changes as grants change.
- **The delete count now depends on this route's clearing.** Before it, nothing cleared an assignee once set, and `count_open_tasks_needing_role` said so. A reassign to a role makes the task count against that role again. The comment and a test say so.
- **Task paths serialize per instance** ([#619](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/619)). A reassign, a claim or a hand-off locks its task's instance, so it waits for, and blocks, a decision or another task path on any task of the same process. Each wait lasts one short transaction, and the alternative was a deadlock answering 500.
- One more route and one more permission to document and grant. A tenant provisioned by an older image does not have the permission until its administrator grants it.

**Neutral**

- `TaskHistoryAction` joins `TransitionAction` in the history column's vocabulary.
- `direct` takes its refusal wording from the caller.
- [#530](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/530) (plan 17 row 12) hides a claimed task from its assignee when every decision edge names a deleted role. A reassign changes no edges, so the two do not interact.

## 5. Compliance

- `tests/task_inbox.rs`, the `#512` section:
  - the reassign to a user and to a role, including the history row, the inboxes and a decision after;
  - both, neither, a null target and a dead or unknown user or role;
  - a decided task, the permission, and another tenant;
  - the decidability check: each `allowedBy` form for a user target, passing and not, `DEPARTMENT_ROLE` in and out of its department, mixed edges where one passes, a role target named and not, an `OWNER`-only state for a role, and a state with no decision edges;
  - the race against a role delete, in both orders, seen in `pg_blocking_pids`: the real route arriving during a held reassign, and a reassign arriving during a delete held by the test;
  - the lock order ([#619](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/619)): a decision and a claim arriving during a held reassign, each seen waiting in `lock_instance`; a claim, a hand-off and a reassign sent while the test holds the instance and then takes the task; none increases `pg_stat_database.deadlocks`; and `a_task_path_refuses_a_missing_task_or_instance_with_a_404` keeps the 404s.

  Each predicate was seen red under a mutation (the table in the file).
- `task_inbox.rs`'s published-contract test lists the path, and `router.rs`'s `every_annotated_route_reaches_the_document` holds the annotation.
- `workflow::domain::task`'s unit tests hold the one-target rule.
- A reviewer checks that any new writer of a task's holder columns goes through `ResolvedAssignment`, and that a new role-resolving path reads the role `FOR KEY SHARE` before it writes.

## 6. Revisit When

- A role target whose holders are all outside a `DEPARTMENT_ROLE` edge's department is reported stuck: the role-target rule then counts holders in the department.
- A definition gains a decision verb beyond `APPROVE`, `REJECT` and `RETURN`: the decision edges are that list, and it grows with it.
- A requirement asks for a department-scoped reassign, or for a cancel.
