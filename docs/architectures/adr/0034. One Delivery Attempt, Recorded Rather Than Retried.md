# ADR-0034 — One Delivery Attempt, Recorded Rather Than Retried

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-09-02

**Decision date:** — · **Deciders:** Sprint 13 item 8's author — **author-verified** ([sprint plan](../../../projects/planning/01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md) §2), so this record stays `Draft` until a second reader has it · **Supersedes:** — · **Superseded by:** — · **Related:** `D-56`, `D-35`, `D-48`, [ADR-0026](0026.%20ClamAV%20in%20the%20Stack,%20Scanning%20Asynchronously.md), FR-NTF-004, FR-NTF-005, issue [#257](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/257)

## 1. Context

**Written when the decision was taken** (standard §9).

[SRS](../../requirements/srs.md) §4.7 FR-NTF-004 says the system shall deliver notifications by email, and FR-NTF-005 that the message body comes from a configurable template. Both are `Should`. `0034_notification.sql` created `notification_templates`, `notification_channels` and `notification_logs` in Sprint 13 item 2 and **wrote none of them** — its own `COMMENT ON` says they belong to this issue.

Three facts already on disk decide most of the shape:

* **`notifications` is written inside the transaction of the thing it announces** ([#251](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/251) AC3). `modules::notification::service::notify` says so and says why: a notification that outlives a rolled-back approval is a lie somebody acts on. An SMTP call in that transaction would hold a database lock open across somebody else's network — the shape **D-35** already cost this project once.
* **`mail.rs` and Mailpit have existed since Sprint 4's password reset.** There is a mailer; what there is not is a caller that cares whether the send worked. `Mailer::send` returns `()` on purpose, because a password reset must answer identically whether or not the address exists.
* **`notification_logs` has an `attempt` column**, `NOT NULL DEFAULT 1`, and nothing has ever written a row into that table.

So the delivery loop itself is not the open question — `attachment::worker` established the poll-over-a-status-column shape in Sprint 12 ([ADR-0026](0026.%20ClamAV%20in%20the%20Stack,%20Scanning%20Asynchronously.md)), and following it is what standard §2 excludes from this folder. **The open question is what happens when a delivery fails**, because `attempt` is an invitation to retry and the answer here is that this release does not.

That answer has to be recorded rather than inferred from its absence. A reader who finds a column named `attempt` that is always `1`, and a `FAILED` row nobody ever revisits, will otherwise read a gap where there is a decision.

## 2. Decision

**A notification is delivered once, on every channel its tenant has enabled, and the outcome of each attempt is written to `notification_logs` and never retried.**

A failed send marks the notification `FAILED` and changes nothing else about it: the title, the body and `read_at` are exactly as they were, and the notification centre still shows it. **`notifications.status` is the state of the *delivery*, not of the notification** — the in-app record is the storage, and it was delivered by being written.

`notification_logs.attempt` stays in the schema, always `1`, and is where a retry policy lands when somebody builds one. The scope this decision leaves open is that policy — a backoff, a cap, and somewhere for the permanently undeliverable to go — and FR-NTF-006/007's reminders and escalations, which need a scheduler rather than a sender.

## 3. Options Considered

| Option | What it would have bought | What ruled it out |
|---|---|---|
| **One attempt, logged** *(chosen)* | A `notification_logs` row per attempt, so *did this arrive* is answerable; no new failure modes; the smallest thing that satisfies FR-NTF-004 | A transient relay outage loses every email in its window, permanently. See §4 — this is the cost, and it is real |
| **Retry on the next pass, while `FAILED`** | A relay that was down for a minute costs nothing | The loop wakes every few seconds. A relay refusing one message gets a copy of it every few seconds, for ever, with no cap and no backoff — this is how a delivery loop becomes the outage. A correct version needs a backoff column, a cap, and a terminal state, which is a larger item than the one being built |
| **Retry inside the pass, a few times with a delay** | Covers the common flap without new schema | The delay is held by the loop, so one unreachable address slows delivery for every notification behind it. It also hides the failure the log row exists to expose: a send that succeeded on the third try looks identical to one that succeeded on the first |
| **A dead-letter table** | The permanently undeliverable have somewhere to be found | Nothing reads it in this release, and a table nobody reads is a table that drifts. `notifications.status = 'FAILED'` with its log rows already answers the same question from a table that is already read |
| **Send inside the announcing transaction and give up the worker** | No queue, no poll interval, no second place a notification can be | **D-35**'s shape exactly: a database lock held open across an SMTP round trip. It also makes a mail-server timeout roll back the approval that triggered it, which is the one outcome #251 AC3 exists to prevent |

## 4. Consequences

**Good.**

* *Did this person get told* is answerable from a table, per channel, with the error text. That question had no answer at all before this.
* A failure costs a delivery and never a notification. The centre is unaffected by anything the sender does.
* A channel this build cannot send on — `SMS`, a plugin's — is a recorded failed attempt rather than silence, so an unbuilt channel is distinguishable from a working one.

**Bad, and stated rather than discovered.**

* **A relay that is down for five minutes loses those five minutes of email permanently.** Nothing retries and nothing tells the recipient. The mitigation available today is that the notification is still in the centre; the fix is the retry policy this decision defers.
* `notification_logs.attempt` is a column with one value, which is a reader's question until it has an answer. This record is that answer.
* `Mailer::Logged` — a deployment with no SMTP host — **reports success**, so a deployment that has not configured mail logs `SENT` rows. That is deliberate: it has said it does not send mail, and a log full of failures would turn a stated configuration into an alert somebody triages. The warning line in `mail.rs` is that deployment's evidence.

## 5. Compliance

`notification_email.rs` holds the tests, and two of them are this decision's:

* *a failed delivery is recorded and the notification is not lost* — the notification is `FAILED`, both attempts are logged, and the title and `read_at` are untouched.
* *a delivery already recorded is not overwritten by a second worker* — `mark_delivered` writes only over `PENDING`, so a duplicated pass costs an email and changes no row twice.

A reviewer who sees a retry appear checks that it arrived with a backoff, a cap and a terminal state, and that this record was superseded rather than edited.

## 6. Revisit When

* A deployment reports email that did not arrive and `notification_logs` shows a `FAILED` row with a transient error — that is the retry policy becoming worth its cost, and it is observable rather than a matter of opinion.
* FR-NTF-006 or FR-NTF-007 is scheduled: a reminder is a scheduler deciding that something has *not* happened, and whoever builds it is already building the machinery a retry needs.

## 7. Notes

The renderer is deliberately single-pass: a value substituted into the template is never re-scanned, so a notification whose body contains `{{title}}` sends those eight characters rather than expanding them. A template naming a placeholder the sender cannot resolve falls back to the notification's own title and body — **silence is the failure this epic exists to end**, and an email nobody receives because somebody mistyped a placeholder in a configuration table is that failure wearing a different hat.

`template_for` asks for locale `en` and nothing else, because nothing in this release knows what language a person reads. That is a gap rather than a simplification, and `notification_templates.locale` is where it closes.
