# ADR-0021 — `allowedBy` Authorizes and Does Not Select

**Status:** Adopted · **Last updated:** 2026-09-01

**Decision date:** 2026-08-28 · **Deciders:** Product owner · **Supersedes:** — · **Superseded by:** — · **Related:** `D-40`, `D-39`, [ADR-0005](0005.%20An%20Internal%20Workflow%20Engine%20Instead%20of%20BPMN.md)

## 1. Context

**Recorded retrospectively on 2026-09-01** from `D-40` and `D-39` in [Product Backlog](../../../projects/planning/02.%20Product%20Backlog.md) §6.2; the operative statement is [JWSS](../../schema/JSON%20Workflow%20Schema.md) §4 (standard §9).

A transition's `allowedBy` was parsed, validated at save, projected to `workflow_transitions.allowed_by_json` — and read by nothing (#226). Enforcing it forced a choice, because where a state offers two transitions for one action the engine already chooses between them by `condition` (S7, fallback last). Adding `allowedBy` to that choice would make it a **selector**: an edge the caller may not take is skipped and the next one tried. Treating it as an **authorizer** refuses instead.

A second question had to be answered first (`D-39`, #225): `user_roles.department_id` was written by the assignment resolver and read by nothing, so a task offered to Finance's approver was decidable by any holder of that role anywhere — and enforcing `DEPARTMENT_ROLE` needs a meaning for the grant that names no department.

## 2. Decision

**`allowedBy` authorizes; it does not select.** The engine chooses the edge by `condition`, then refuses the caller who may not take it. **And a role grant naming no department satisfies a department-scoped task** — a null grant is the role held generally, not the role held nowhere.

## 3. Options Considered

| Option | Summary | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Authorize the chosen edge** | `condition` selects, `allowedBy` refuses | **Chosen** |
| B. Select by `allowedBy` | Skip an edge the caller may not take, try the next | Rejected — routes silently: a refusal becomes somebody's recorded decision |
| **C. A null department grant is the role held generally** | The broader reading, for `D-39` | **Chosen** |
| D. Scoped-or-nothing | A null grant satisfies only an unscoped task | Rejected — inverts the column's meaning and strands every existing deployment's live approvals |

### A. Authorize — chosen

A selector routes silently: an approver who may not take the `APPROVE` edge would be sent down whatever comes next — a return, or a fallback — and the resulting record would read as their own decision rather than as a refusal they never saw. ***A rejection routed as a return is a signature on the wrong document.*** It also keeps `condition` and `allowedBy` doing one job each, which is how JWSS §4 describes them.

### C. The null grant

`0002` calls the column an *optional* department-scoped grant, and the natural reading of that is a role held generally — a scoped grant is the **narrowing**, so treating its absence as the narrowest case inverts the column's meaning. The stricter rule also fails on contact with the data: no deployment has ever populated the column, because nothing read it, so scoped-or-nothing would make **every** `DEPARTMENT_ROLE` task in every existing deployment undecidable the moment the fix shipped — stranding live approvals, which is the outcome [ADR-0019](0019.%20A%20Workflow%20Definition%20Is%20Narrowed%20at%20Save%20Time.md) refuses two assignee types at save time to avoid.

## 4. Consequences

**Positive** — a caller who may not take an edge is told so, and the history records what happened rather than a decision nobody made. One resolver serves both the queue and the decision: `assignment::permits` is built on `resolve`, so an edge and a task read the four assignee types identically.

**Negative** — a state offering two same-action edges to *different* roles now refuses for one of the two callers rather than routing. If an author wants that routing, the answer is a `condition`, not an `allowedBy`.

**Not settled** — a tenant that wants unscoped grants to mean nothing has no way to say so. That is a configuration question rather than a correctness one.

## 5. Compliance

Enforced in `engine::fire`, against the chosen transition, before the instance moves. `repository::task::holds_role` carries the null-grant reasoning, and the three candidate arms of `repository::inbox` use the same predicate — so the queue and the decision cannot answer differently without one of them being a bug.

## 6. Revisit When

A deployment needs role grants that mean nothing unless scoped, or a routing device that genuinely depends on who is asking — the case where option B's behaviour is what an author wants and `condition` cannot express it.
