# ADR-0037 — ERP Posting Runs Inside the Document Transaction

**Status:** Draft · **Last updated:** 2026-09-08

**Blocked by:** [#371](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/371) — the ERP scope decision is not taken; no `D-n` adopts an ERP layer, and no `FR-*` covers one

**Decision date:** — · **Deciders:** Product owner · **Supersedes:** — · **Superseded by:** — · **Related:** [ADR-0016](0016.%20An%20Unenforceable%20Rule%20Refuses%20Rather%20Than%20Passes.md), [ADR-0020](0020.%20The%20Workflow%20Owns%20the%20Document%20Status.md), [ADR-0034](0034.%20One%20Delivery%20Attempt,%20Recorded%20Rather%20Than%20Retried.md), [ADR-0036](0036.%20The%20Hook%20Chain%20Ships%20Its%20Before%20Half%20First.md), `D-73`, issue [#371](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/371)

## 1. Context

**Kelir has no ledger, and every ERP reference in its documentation assumes somebody else does.** Document [03](../03.%20Kelir%20Modules%20for%20Interfacing%20with%20External%20Systems.md) §6.1 is *Purchase Requisition to ERP*; `external_systems.system_type` carries `'ERP'` as a value; document [01](../01.%20Basic%20Framework%20Concept%20and%20Architecture.md) §12.3 gives *"Write to ERP"* as its example of what an `after_document_post` hook does. The question of where posting runs has therefore never had to be answered, because posting has always been somebody else's transaction.

[Architectures/06](../06.%20Building%20an%20ERP%20on%20Kelir.md), written 2026-09-08 at the product owner's request, inverts that assumption and needs the answer. Four facts bound it:

- **The `POST` lifecycle stage is specified and unimplemented.** Document 01 §12.1 defines it — *"approved result written to the business module or master data"*, resulting status `COMPLETED` — and §12.3 gives it a hook pair. `DocumentStatus` terminates at `COMPLETED` with nothing between it and `APPROVED`.
- **Only half the hook chain exists.** [ADR-0036](0036.%20The%20Hook%20Chain%20Ships%20Its%20Before%20Half%20First.md) shipped `before_*`, synchronous and inside the caller's transaction, driven by a service task. `after_*` hooks are unbuilt, and JWSS `actions` are stored and never invoked.
- **There is no general outbox.** Notifications are delivered by a worker over `notifications` — a queue for one subject, chosen in [ADR-0034](0034.%20One%20Delivery%20Attempt,%20Recorded%20Rather%20Than%20Retried.md) — and `notification::service` records that a general outbox is a later phase. Document 01 §12.5 makes idempotency, at-least-once delivery, a retry schedule and a circuit breaker the outbox worker's properties.
- **Document 01 §12.2 carries a design rule** that reads, in full: *a `before_*` hook is part of the business rule; an `after_*` hook is a reaction. Never put a reaction in a `before_*` hook — a slow integration call must not be able to block or roll back a user's submission.* Whether a ledger write is a business rule or a reaction is the question this record settles, and the rule as written does not answer it.

Two properties of a ledger bear on the answer and are not properties of anything Kelir currently writes. A balance must be **derivable at any instant from what is committed** — an accountant reading a trial balance is entitled to assume it accounts for every document the system says is complete. And a posting must be **refusable**: a closed period, a blocked account, stock that is not there and a budget that is exhausted are each a reason the completion must not happen, rather than a reason to record that it should not have.

## 2. Decision

**Kelir posts an ERP document's ledger effects synchronously, inside the transaction that completes the document, through a `before_document_post` handler.** The posting seam resolves the document type's posting rule, projects its line items into relational rows, and calls the ledger, inventory and subledger modules with a transaction handle; any of them may refuse, and a refusal aborts the completion with [ADR-0016](0016.%20An%20Unenforceable%20Rule%20Refuses%20Rather%20Than%20Passes.md)'s discipline and LHCS §6's `HOOK_REJECTED` envelope rather than recording a problem after the fact. `documents.status = COMPLETED` and the ledger rows are written by one transaction and are therefore both true or neither. **Every external consequence of a posting stays on the after half** — e-invoicing, bank files, tax filings, supplier notification — and waits for the outbox, which this decision does not build. The decision covers where posting runs and what may refuse it; it does not settle what a posting rule may express (architectures/06 §5.4), the valuation methods, or whether an ERP is in scope at all, which is issue [#371](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/371)'s.

## 3. Options Considered

| Option | Summary | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A. `before_document_post`, in the caller's transaction** | The ledger write is part of the completion | **Chosen** |
| B. `after_document_post`, through the outbox | The ledger write is a reaction to the completion | Rejected — a window in which the document says complete and the ledger disagrees, and nothing that can refuse |
| C. A separate posting endpoint, called after completion | The user completes, then posts, in two steps | Rejected — makes *completed but unposted* a durable state rather than a transient one, and puts a clerk in charge of closing it |
| D. An external posting service over the plugin API | The ledger is its own deployable | Rejected — structurally cannot enlist in the transaction |
| E. Post from the workflow transition, with no lifecycle stage | The engine writes the ledger when it reaches a final state | Rejected — puts ledger knowledge inside the workflow engine, and a workflow is not the only route to completion |

### A. In the caller's transaction — chosen

**It is the only option under which the trial balance is true.** A balance is derived from committed rows; if the completion commits before the entry does, then for the duration of that gap the ledger is a wrong answer to a question somebody is entitled to ask. The gap cannot be closed by making the worker faster, only made smaller, and a smaller wrong answer is still a wrong answer that somebody will screenshot.

**And it is the only option with a veto.** A closed period, a blocked account, insufficient stock and an exhausted budget are refusals, and a refusal must prevent the thing rather than follow it. LHCS §5.1's `REJECT` already means exactly this, [ADR-0036](0036.%20The%20Hook%20Chain%20Ships%20Its%20Before%20Half%20First.md) already implements it, and a `REJECT` on a before-hook already rolls back what it should. Nothing has to be invented; the mechanism is the one a guard already uses.

**Document 01 §12.2's rule survives intact and is not stretched.** Its subject is the *slow integration call* — the thing that fails for reasons the user cannot fix and takes time the user should not wait for. A ledger write is neither: it is a set of inserts on the connection the transaction already holds, against tables in the same database. The rule's own first clause is the one that applies — *a `before_*` hook is part of the business rule* — and *this document is now financially real* is the business rule, not a reaction to it.

**What it does not solve:** the transaction gets longer, and §4 states what that costs.

### B. After the commit, through the outbox

**The tempting one, because the platform is already shaped for it** and because document 01 §12.3 literally names ERP posting as an after-hook example — written, though, when the ERP was external and the after-hook's job was to *tell* it.

Two costs rule it out. The window above, which is permanent rather than a transitional defect. And the absence of a veto: an after-hook is delivered at least once and cannot refuse, so a posting into a closed period becomes an entry that has to be found and reversed rather than one that was never made. That inverts the burden from the system, which knows the period is closed, onto whoever reconciles the month.

There is also a timing problem the option cannot answer: at-least-once delivery plus a retry schedule means the ledger needs idempotency at the entry level anyway, which architectures/06 §5.3 provides — so the option does not even save that work. It only moves the failure from the approver, who can act, to an operator reading a dead-letter queue, who cannot.

### C. A separate posting endpoint

Defensible, and it is what several established ERPs do — a document is completed, and posted later by somebody with the authority to post. It buys a clean separation and a posting batch an accountant controls.

It is rejected here because it makes **completed but unposted a durable state**, which is precisely the state option B produces accidentally and this one produces on purpose. The difference is that here somebody is responsible for closing it, which means it stays open at exactly the moments a business is busiest. Kelir's own precedent points the other way: [ADR-0020](0020.%20The%20Workflow%20Owns%20the%20Document%20Status.md) refuses a manual status transition on a document with a live instance rather than allowing a second route to the same state, on the reasoning that a disagreement the system can prevent should not be reachable at all.

If a deployment genuinely wants a controlled posting run, it is expressible **inside** this decision rather than against it — a workflow state before the final one, whose approval is the posting authorisation. That keeps one route to `COMPLETED` and puts the control where the rest of the product's controls already are.

### D. An external posting service

**Structurally impossible, and worth recording as such** so that it is not proposed again as a scaling measure. Document [04](../04.%20Kelir%20Plugin%20and%20Extension%20Management%20Concept.md) §1.4 offers external plugin services for isolated or untrusted extensions, and they are the right answer for connectors. A separate process cannot enlist in Kelir's transaction: posting from outside means two-phase commit across a boundary neither side was designed for, or eventual consistency in a general ledger — which is a reconciliation job with no end condition.

The constraint is a property of the ledger, not a preference about deployment, which is why it is here rather than in a discussion about services.

### E. From the workflow transition

The shortest path — the engine already writes the document's status when it reaches a final state ([ADR-0020](0020.%20The%20Workflow%20Owns%20the%20Document%20Status.md)), so it could write the ledger in the same place.

Rejected twice over. It puts ledger knowledge inside the workflow engine, which is the module [SDD](../../design/01.%20System%20Design%20Document.md) §8.5.1 keeps to *one place a process moves* precisely because it is the shape a system most easily grows two of. And a workflow is not the only route to completion: a document type may bind no workflow at all, and a period-end accrual has no document. A posting that only happens when a workflow says so is a posting half the ERP cannot reach.

## 4. Consequences

**Positive** — the trial balance is derivable from committed state at every instant, with no window and no reconciliation job; a posting that must not happen does not happen, refused to the person holding the task with an envelope that names why; the completion and the entry are atomic without any distributed-transaction machinery; and the mechanism is the hook chain [ADR-0036](0036.%20The%20Hook%20Chain%20Ships%20Its%20Before%20Half%20First.md) already built rather than a second execution surface beside it.

**Negative** — **the transaction gets longer, and it is already the longest one in the product.** The submit path holds a numbering counter ([SDD](../../design/01.%20System%20Design%20Document.md) §8.4.1) and a decision holds the instance lock; posting adds ledger writes and a balance update inside both, so concurrent approvals of the same document type serialise for longer than they do today. The mitigation is that the writes are local and indexed, not that they are free, and a deployment that outgrows it has a real problem rather than a tuning exercise.

**A failed posting is a failed approval.** The approver sees it, not an operator, and there is no retry: they cannot approve until whatever is wrong — the period, the account, the budget — is fixed by somebody else. That is the correct place for the refusals this decision exists to enable, and it is genuinely worse than option B for the class of failure that is transient and self-healing. This decision asserts that a ledger has no such class; if one is found, §6 is the trigger.

**It forbids something a handler author will want to do.** No external call may enter the posting chain — not a tax-authority number, not an exchange rate fetched live, not a credit check. Each of those is a reasonable thing to want at posting time and each would reintroduce exactly the failure document 01 §12.2's rule is about. The rule has to be stated and enforced by review, because nothing in the signature prevents it.

**Neutral** — a `POST` lifecycle stage becomes reachable and `documents` gains a `posted_at` and a posting-run reference; `posting_runs` is a new table whose id appears on every ledger row; the `erp:` permission namespace is introduced; and architectures/06 §6 lists seven platform changes this makes urgent, three of which (the stage itself, the outbox, posted-document immutability) are worth doing whether or not an ERP follows.

## 5. Compliance

**Nothing mechanical can exist yet**, because the code this constrains is unwritten — which is the case standards/06 §1's *"constrains code that has not been written yet"* test describes, and the reason this is a record rather than a comment.

What a reviewer checks when it is written, in order of how easily each is violated:

1. **`posting::post` takes a transaction and never the pool.** This is the rule [SDD](../../design/01.%20System%20Design%20Document.md) §8.4.1 already states for the submit path and §8.5.1 for the engine — a second pooled connection taken inside a transaction deadlocks at the concurrency the pool can serve, and this path holds more locks than either.
2. **No Tier 3 module imports `modules::document`** (architectures/06 §4.1). Mechanisable later as a dependency test over the module tree, in the shape `audit_object_types` already has.
3. **No external call in the posting chain** — no HTTP client, no queue publish, no `integration::` call below `posting::post`. This is the §4 negative that has no signature-level guard.
4. **When built, a test asserting that a refused posting leaves no document at `COMPLETED`** and no ledger row, and its pair: a successful one leaves both. That assertion is the decision, restated as something that can fail.

## 6. Revisit When

**A posting genuinely cannot be completed locally.** Indonesian e-Faktur is the realistic candidate: if a tax authority must issue a number synchronously before an entry is valid, then a posting has an external dependency and §4's forbidden thing becomes a requirement. The answer then is not to move posting onto the outbox but to split it — a locally-valid entry plus a separately-tracked external obligation — and that split needs its own record.

**Or the outbox lands and somebody proposes moving posting onto it.** The proposal is legitimate once the retry, idempotency and circuit-breaker machinery exists; §3's option B should be re-read against it rather than re-derived, because the window and the missing veto are the objections and neither is fixed by the outbox arriving.
