# Sprint 15 RAD Authoring Construction Plan

**Status:** Adopted · **Last updated:** 2026-09-08

This decomposes [Sprint Plan](01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md) §5's Sprint 15 into the order it runs in. It sits **one level below** that plan, whose entry and exit criteria it does not change; where the two disagree the sprint plan wins.

It is the seventh construction plan, after [03](03.%20Sprint%208%20Form%20Renderer%20Construction%20Plan.md), [04](04.%20Sprint%209%20Document%20Construction%20Plan.md), [05](05.%20Sprint%2010%20Workflow%20Construction%20Plan.md), [07](07.%20Sprint%2012%20Collaboration%20Construction%20Plan.md), [08](08.%20Sprint%2013%20MVP%20Construction%20Plan.md) and [10](10.%20Sprint%2014%20RAD%20Engine%20Construction%20Plan.md). Related documents: the requirement authority is SRS §4.5, the schema authority is [JFSS](../../docs/schema/JSON%20Form%20Schema.md) 2.0.1 with its [validation](../../docs/schema/JFSS%20Validation%20Rule%20Registry.md) and [calculation](../../docs/schema/JFSS%20Calculation%20Rule%20Registry.md) registries, the rendering and resolution designs are [SDD](../../docs/design/01.%20System%20Design%20Document.md) §8.2.3 and §8.2.4, and the revision rule is [ADR-0027](../../docs/architectures/adr/0027.%20A%20Document%20Pins%20the%20Form%20Revision%20It%20Was%20Filled%20Against.md).

## 1. Three things this plan says before anything else

### 1.1 The phase's deliverable is already met, so this sprint is not finishing anything

**Every previous construction plan decomposed a phase that was still owed something.** This one does not. [SDD](../../docs/design/01.%20System%20Design%20Document.md) §14 names five things for Phase 7 and all five exist — four from Sprint 14, and conditional field visibility from **Sprint 8**, found there by [#357](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/357) five weeks after it shipped. **D-74** records the re-aim.

That changes what a cut means here. In Sprint 14, cutting a row deferred a phase obligation. In this sprint, **cutting a row defers nothing that any document promises** — which sounds like slack and is the opposite. A sprint whose scope rests on judgement rather than on an obligation is a sprint whose scope erodes quietly, because every individual cut is defensible. §3 sets the line against that, and §8 records the argument rather than leaving it to be re-derived at the boundary.

**The one thing that is owed is not in §14's list.** `v0.6.0`'s release notes say document types are configured through the API *with the builders scheduled for Phase 7* — plural. The document type and menu builders shipped; the form and list builders are the rest of that sentence, and Sprint 16 is the last sprint in which it can be made true.

### 1.2 Five of the retrospective's seven actions are rows in this sprint, and that is unusual

[Retrospective 12](../retrospectives/12.%20Sprint%2014%20Retrospective.md) produced seven actions. **Two were discharged by the act of planning** — no row reads `to file`, and both large rows carry the check that shows them unbuilt — and **five are construction rows here** (§6).

The last two sprints have gone the other way: [plan 10](10.%20Sprint%2014%20RAD%20Engine%20Construction%20Plan.md) §6 put two of Sprint 13's seven actions *below* its cut line and said plainly that an action below a line is an action that may not happen. Both were eventually built, after the boundary, on request.

**So this plan puts none of them below the line**, and the reason is that retrospective 12's own review found the pattern: the actions that close are the ones naming an artefact, and the ones that do not are the ones whose end state is *a person does a thing at a time*. Items 3, 4 and 5 each name a file. Item 6 names a person, and §1.3 is about that.

### 1.3 One row cannot be started by a builder, and it has now failed as an exit criterion once

**Item 6 ([#335](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/335)) is blocked on D-66, which was Sprint 14's exit criterion and was carried.** `main`'s protection is `enforcement_level: non_admins`, so required contexts constrain everybody except the party who merges — and all thirteen of Sprint 14's merges went through that gap, as did all of this sprint's will.

The sprint plan makes answering it an exit criterion for the second time. **This plan does not restate it as an action**, because retrospective 12 withdrew permission to restate the *other* twice-failed rule and the same logic applies here. What it does instead is position the row where the decision is due (§7 step 2) rather than at the end, so that a second carry is visible in the first half of the sprint rather than discovered at the close.

## 8. Decisions this plan takes, and the one it owes

**Taken here:**

- **The cut line is at six**, against a sprint that built seven, because item 3 is larger than anything Sprint 14 built and item 5 is not much smaller. §3.
- **Items 3 and 5 are sequential, not parallel.** They are the same screen over two tables — an admin CRUD surface over a stored definition, with a server round trip that returns a verdict rather than a value. Building them at once produces two shells that must later be reconciled, or one shell designed by whoever finishes first; building them in order costs the calendar and buys the second one cheaply. **The cost is stated: this is the sprint's single largest scheduling risk**, because if item 3 overruns, item 5 has nowhere to go, and item 5 is then the row that is cut.
- **Item 3's first cut authors structure and rule *selection*, not rule *expressions*.** A component tree, sections, labels, keys, types, and validation rules **chosen from the Validation Rule Registry** with their parameters. A `calculate` or a `conditional.logic` is edited as JSON Logic in a text field, validated by the server's own `rad::domain::engine` and reported through the S10.3 envelope. **A visual expression builder is not in this sprint** — it is the part with no ceiling, and the row is already the largest in the plan. The bar [#373](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/373) sets is *a form can be authored without `curl`*, and a text field with a real validator clears it.
- **The editor reuses the server's verdict rather than carrying its own rule engine.** [ADR-0008](../../docs/architectures/adr/0008.%20One%20JSON%20Logic%20Engine%20on%20Both%20Sides.md) put one evaluator on both sides; a *second* rule catalogue in the browser — one that decides which names are registered and where a cycle is — would be a third opinion about a question `rad::domain::engine` already answers at the write and at the publish. The screen may pre-validate for speed; it may not disagree.
- **Item 1 is first.** A control that governs this sprint's own status report and merges in its last week was never in force. §4.
- **Item 4 precedes mid-sprint refinement**, so that a pass which does fire is reading evidence rather than intentions. §4.
- **None of the retrospective's actions sits below the cut line.** §1.2.

**Owed, and not this plan's to take:**

> **D-66 — is the person merging inside the gate?** Unchanged from [plan 10](10.%20Sprint%2014%20RAD%20Engine%20Construction%20Plan.md) §8, which is the problem. It was that sprint's exit criterion, it was carried, and it is this sprint's exit criterion again. **The one thing this plan adds is a position on the sequence** (§7 step 2): asked in week one rather than discovered at the close. A plan cannot answer a question reserved to the product owner; it can refuse to let the answer arrive too late to act on.
