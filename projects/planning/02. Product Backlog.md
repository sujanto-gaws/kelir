# Kelir Product Backlog

**Status:** Living · **Last updated:** 2026-08-13

The [Sprint Plan](01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md) says *when* work happens; this document says *what the work is* and proves that every requirement has a home. It is the scope inventory: each of the 164 functional requirements in the [SRS](../../docs/requirements/srs.md) §4 is assigned to an epic, a backlog item, and a target sprint — or listed explicitly as unscheduled.

Scope authority remains the SRS. Where this backlog and the SRS disagree, the SRS wins and this document is corrected; where this backlog and the sprint plan disagree on sequence, the sprint plan wins.

---

## 1. How This Backlog Is Organized

- **Epic** — one per SRS §4 requirement area, keyed by the area's FR prefix (`AUTH`, `IDM`, …). Epics are stable; items inside them churn.
- **Item** — a demoable slice, sized to fit inside one sprint, carrying the FR IDs it satisfies. Items are the unit that enters a sprint and the unit a status report reports on.
- **Target** — the sprint from the [sprint map](01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md#3-sprint-map). `Stretch` means the item is planned into that phase but is the first thing cut when velocity demands it. `Unscheduled` means no phase claims it yet.
- **Priority** — from the SRS (`Must` / `Should` / `Could`). Priorities are not re-assigned here; they are copied.

Every FR ID appears in exactly one item row or in the unscheduled list (§7). §8 reconciles the counts.

## 2. Definition of Ready

The sprint plan defines *Done*; an item is **Ready** to be pulled into a sprint when:

1. It names its FR IDs, and its acceptance criteria are stated in SRS terms.
2. Its data model exists in the [Database Schema](../../docs/design/02.%20Database%20Schema.md), or the migration it needs is identified by file name.
3. Its API surface is expressible under the [naming convention](../../docs/standards/02.%20Naming%20Convention.md) §5, and its permission strings (`module:resource:action`) are decided.
4. Its upstream dependencies are Done or in the same sprint.
5. It is demoable on its own — an item that cannot be shown is a task, not a backlog item.

**Result: all 15 criteria are met at `v0.6.0`**, the end of Phase 6. This holds because of two decisions taken on 2026-08-11: **D-1** moved the MVP tag from `v0.5.0` (where criteria 6, 11, 12 and 13 were still unbuilt) to `v0.6.0`, and **D-2** moved RAD's storage layer and form renderer into Phase 4 (where criteria 4 and 5 need them). At `v0.5.0` the count would have been 10 of 15.

Four `Must`-priority FRs still land after the MVP tag — FR-RAD-006 (rule engines, Phase 7) and FR-RPT-001, 002, 003 (dashboard, Phase 8) — as does the dynamic-list half of FR-RAD-003/010. That is legitimate, not a gap: SRS v0.5 defines `Must` as "required for the 1.0 release" and names §9 as the sole MVP gate (decision **D-3**), so a `Must` requirement outside §9 is post-MVP by design. The converse no longer occurs — FR-API-004 was raised to `Must` so that nothing §9 depends on can be cut as a `Should`.

| **D-1** | The MVP milestone was tagged at the end of Phase 5 (`v0.5.0`), but SRS §9 criteria 6, 11, 12 and 13 are Phase 6 work. ## 9. Change Control

- Items enter and leave through backlog refinement, priced against the [sprint map](01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md#3-sprint-map). `Must` displaces `Should`, never the reverse.
- Adding a requirement means adding it to the SRS first, then to an epic here — this document never originates scope.
- Update the coverage reconciliation (§8) in the same change that moves an item between scheduled and unscheduled; a stale total is how coverage claims rot.
- Re-verify §5 against SRS §9 at every phase boundary and before tagging the MVP release.
