# Sprint 10 Workflow Construction Plan

**Status:** Adopted · **Last updated:** 2026-08-28

This plan decomposes Sprint 10's seven items — [#174](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/174) the migration and the definition, [#175](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/175) instances and variables, [#176](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/176) tasks, [#177](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/177) approve and reject, [#178](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/178) the document–workflow seam, [#187](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/187) the type's workflow binding, [#179](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/179) the task inbox — into files, order, and the decisions each takes before it is written. It sits **one level below** the [Sprint Plan](01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md), which settles what is in Sprint 10 and why; nothing here changes that scope, and where the two disagree the sprint plan wins.

It exists for the reason the [Sprint 8](03.%20Sprint%208%20Form%20Renderer%20Construction%20Plan.md) and [Sprint 9](04.%20Sprint%209%20Document%20Construction%20Plan.md) plans existed and then earned. It carries one thing neither of those did: **Sprint 10 opens a phase and builds an engine**, and an engine is the shape a codebase most easily grows two of. §4–§10 are therefore written so that there is exactly one function that moves a process, and everything else calls it.

Related documents: storage is [Database Schema](../../docs/design/02.%20Database%20Schema.md) §7; the definition semantics are the [JSON Workflow Schema](../../docs/schema/JSON%20Workflow%20Schema.md) v1.0.0 and the [JFSS Calculation Rule Registry](../../docs/schema/JFSS%20Calculation%20Rule%20Registry.md) its conditions are bounded by; the engineering rules are [docs/standards/](../../docs/standards/), chiefly [coding standard](../../docs/standards/01.%20Coding%20Standard.md) §2.5, §2.9 and [naming convention](../../docs/standards/02.%20Naming%20Convention.md) §4, §5, §6.

## 1. What this plans, and what it deliberately does not

| In | Out, and where it lives instead |

| Approve and reject, as an API | Approving **from the task screen** (FR-TASK-004, 005) and the **decision comment** (FR-TASK-006) — Sprint 11's [#182](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/182). §7.5 says what that costs and why it is still right |
| The document–workflow seam: the link, and the document's status as a projection of the instance's state | The **workflow history record** (FR-WF-012, [#181](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/181)) — Sprint 11. `workflow_task_history` is written this sprint because a task's own status moves; the *document's* history of "how did it get here" is #181's |
| A document type that carries a workflow, checked against a published definition | Escalation and parallel approval (FR-WF-010, 016) — unscheduled, and the SRS defers the second itself |
| The task inbox and the task detail | Acting from the inbox. The inbox links to the document workspace; the buttons are Sprint 11's |

**Sequential approval only** ([#174](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/174), sprint plan §5). This falls out of the model rather than being enforced on top of it: an instance is in exactly one `current_state`, a state declares at most one `task`, and therefore an instance has at most one open task. §6.2 states that as an invariant with a database constraint behind it, so that "we only built the sequential case" is a property somebody can check rather than a sentence in a plan.

A second module writing SQL against another's table is how the visibility rule #179 AC3 states gets two implementations that disagree. §10.1 says the same thing about its permission.

Frontend: `features/tasks/` and `features/workflow/` are empty folders. §10.4 fills the first and §9.4 the second, and both consume `features/documents/` unchanged.

## 3. The migration, and the four things it settles

`0025_workflow.sql`. **It was written as `0024` and renumbered**: `0024_one_live_role_per_party.sql` ([#115](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/115)) landed the same day and took the number, which is the naming convention §4.3 rule working as designed — a migration takes the next free number when it is written, and an early arrival shifts every unwritten migration below it. `0021` and `0023` did the same to this one before it was written at all. It creates the nine tables of §7 plus one the schema does not have yet, adds the two foreign keys Phase 4 deferred, and seeds eight permission rows.

### 3.1 The debt `0015` promised this migration would close

`0015_document.sql`'s header names two columns it created without their constraints, both pointing at tables that did not exist:

* **`document_type_workflows.workflow_definition_id`** — [#174](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/174) AC2, and the reason [#187](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/187) can be built at all.
* **`documents.process_instance_id`** — the seam [#178](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/178) is about.

**The Sprint 9 status report's fifth entry criterion is wrong on a detail and it is corrected here rather than carried.** It reads *"#157's **nullable** `workflow_definition_id` still nullable and still unconstrained"*. The column is `NOT NULL`: [Database Schema §6.4](../../docs/design/02.%20Database%20Schema.md) specified it that way, `0015` implemented it that way, and both say in as many words that this overrules #157's AC3 because where a plan and a `docs/` document disagree the document wins. The half that was genuinely deferred is the foreign key, and that half of the criterion is met. Nothing in this sprint changes the nullability.

### 3.2 Two constraints that make an invariant a fact rather than a habit

**`workflow_instances (workflow_definition_id, current_state)` references `workflow_states (workflow_definition_id, state_code)`.** This is [#175](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/175) AC4 — *"an instance cannot be in a state its definition does not contain, enforced by the database rather than by convention"* — and it is available for free because `uq_workflow_states_definition_state` is already a unique index over exactly that pair, which PostgreSQL accepts as a foreign-key target. An engine bug that writes `FINANCE_APPROVAL` into an instance of a definition that has no such state becomes a constraint violation at the moment it happens rather than a stuck instance discovered by whoever was waiting for it.

It also forces an ordering that is correct anyway: **`workflow_states` is written on publish, so only a published definition can start an instance.** That is not a second rule to remember; it is the same one.

**A partial unique index gives [#178](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/178) AC1 the same treatment:**

```sql
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_workflow_instances_live_document
    ON workflow_instances (document_id)
    WHERE status IN ('STARTED', 'RUNNING', 'SUSPENDED') AND deleted_at IS NULL;
```

*A document links to at most one live process instance, and starting a second while one is live is refused.* The service refuses it first, with a message; the index is what makes the refusal true under concurrency, and §8.3 says which of the two a test reaches.

### 3.3 `workflow_ref_sequences` — one counter table, two prefixes

§7.4 and §7.6 give `workflow_instances.instance_ref` (`WFI-9001`) and `workflow_tasks.task_ref` (`TASK-7001`) as `NOT NULL` and unique per tenant, and nothing produces either. The shape is `document_ref_sequences`', which is `0020_numbering_buckets.sql`' — one row per bucket, insert-or-advance in one statement, `RETURNING next_sequence - 1`, no read to race.

**One table serves both, keyed by the prefix and the year** (`WFI-2026`, `TASK-2026`), rather than two tables differing only in what they count. Two instances of a proven allocator is two places for the next fix to land in one of, which is what §6.3 of the schema used to be and what [#200](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/200) was.

It allocates **inside** the transaction that creates the row it names, so a rolled-back transition leaves no hole and no orphan reference. That is `document_ref_sequences`' trade taken again, deliberately and for the same reason: an internal handle whose counter is contended for the length of one short transaction costs nothing a user can see, and the alternative is a reference on a task that was never created. Recorded in §12 as the same owed question, not as a new one.

### 3.4 Eight permissions, and why the inbox invents none

The resource segment is **required** here, because this module manages several resources — which is the case [naming convention](../../docs/standards/02.%20Naming%20Convention.md) §6 names with `workflow:task:execute` as its own example.

| Permission | Grants |
|---|---|
| `workflow:definition:create` | Create a workflow definition, or the next revision of one |
| `workflow:definition:read` | View workflow definitions |
| `workflow:definition:update` | Edit a draft revision |
| `workflow:definition:publish` | Publish a revision, fixing it for every instance that starts against it |
| `workflow:definition:delete` | Retire a workflow definition |
| `workflow:instance:read` | View a running process and its variables |
| `workflow:task:read` | View tasks — the inbox, and one task's detail |
| `workflow:task:execute` | Claim a task, and record a decision on it |

**`publish` is separate from `update` for the reason it is separate on forms** (§5.13 of the schema): publishing is not an update with a different value, it fixes a revision that running instances will execute for as long as they run. A deployment that wants the same person to do both grants both.

**`workflow:task:execute` covers claiming as well as deciding, and that is a decision.** Claiming an unassigned role task is not a decision, so a separate `workflow:task:claim` was the alternative. It is rejected: a permission that lets somebody take a task off the queue and then not act on it is a permission to stall an approval, which is a worse power than the one it was trying to split off. The two questions are one question — *may this person work this queue* — and §7.6's authorization check is what makes the difference between *may work the queue* and *may act on this particular task*.

**The task inbox seeds nothing.** `GET /api/v1/tasks` requires `workflow:task:read`, the same permission the workflow module's own task read requires, because it reads the same rows. A `task:read` beside it would let a deployment grant the inbox without granting the task — which is the gap §5.13 refused to create for `rad:lookup:read`, one module over.

## 4. Item 1 — the definition, its validation, and its projections (#174)

### 4.1 The shape is `rad::service::form`'s, because it is the same problem

A definition is `(workflow_key, version)`; publishing freezes a revision; editing a published revision creates the next one as a draft; running instances pin the exact `workflow_definitions.id` they started against. That is `rad_forms` with the nouns changed, and [#175](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/175) AC1 asks for precisely the guarantee `documents.form_id` already provides one module over.

**So the pin is the foreign key and there is no `definition_version` column on the instance.** `workflow_instances.workflow_definition_id` names a *revision row*, and a revision row never changes. Recording the version number beside it would be a second copy of a fact the reference already carries, which is the failure #175 AC3 is about in the other direction. The instance read returns `version` by joining, so the API answers AC1's question without the database storing the answer twice.

### 4.2 Three checks on save, and the one that runs again at publish

`domain/jwss.rs` is `domain/jfss.rs`'s counterpart and is written the same way: the vendored meta-schema compiled once, every problem reported rather than the first, paths dotted for the envelope.

| Check | Source | What it catches that the others do not |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | the JWSS v1.0.0 meta-schema, vendored as `jwss-meta-v1.0.0.json` | A document that is not a JWSS document. §1.3 makes the meta-schema normative, so this is definitional |
| **Operators** | the [Calculation Rule Registry](../../docs/schema/JFSS%20Calculation%20Rule%20Registry.md), via JWSS §6.2 | The meta-schema's `jsonLogic` accepts any object. **D-10**'s engine evaluates a far wider surface than the registry approves, on both sides — parity is not governance, and this is where the allow-list is enforced. It is the same constant set `jfss.rs` uses, imported rather than re-listed |
| **Structure (S1–S10)** | JWSS §8 | Reachability, dead ends, duplicate triples, a transition out of a final state, a fallback that is not last. None of it is expressible in JSON Schema and §8 says so |

**The meta-schema is extracted to a file in this sprint**, which JWSS's closing paragraph says happens "when the publish validator is implemented". This is that. The block in the specification stays and `tests/workflow_jwss_meta_schema.rs` asserts the two are identical, which is the arrangement `rad_jfss_meta_schema.rs` already holds for JFSS.

**S11 is not implemented and the definition says so where a reader will find it.** It resolves guard and action handler references, and there is no hook registry to resolve them against — `document_lifecycle_hooks` has no reader and the hook chain is architectures/01 §12's, unbuilt. `guards` and `actions` are therefore **stored and not executed** this sprint. Refusing them at save was the alternative and it is worse: a definition authored today would have to be rewritten when the chain lands. So they are accepted, stored, and `service::engine` states in one place that it does not invoke them, so that nobody reads their presence in a stored definition as evidence that they run.

**All three run on save, and the structural set runs again at publish.** #174 AC3 is explicit — *"refused at **save** time rather than at run time"* — and JWSS §8 is equally explicit that the S-rules are publish-time rules whose consequence is that a violating definition never reaches `ACTIVE`. Running on save satisfies both and is stricter than either; running again at publish is the conformance requirement of §1.3 clause 2, and it is a real second line rather than a decorative one, because a row can reach `ACTIVE` from a database somebody wrote to directly.

Per coding standard §2.5, that second line does not get a paragraph explaining why it exists and no test. `tests/workflow_definitions.rs` inserts an invalid definition through the pool, publishes it through the API, and asserts the refusal — the same technique that made `update_draft`'s `AND status = 'DRAFT'` reachable.

### 4.3 The assignment rules Kelir refuses, and where it refuses them

JWSS §5.1 declares six `assigneeType` values. **Four resolve against tables Kelir has; two do not**, and both of the two are refused **at save** with a message naming what would have to exist first:

| `assigneeType` | Sprint 10 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `USER` | resolves | `users` |
| `ROLE` | resolves | `roles.role_code`, and an unclaimed role task is exactly what #176 AC2 is about |
| `DEPARTMENT_ROLE` | resolves | `roles` plus `user_roles.department_id`, which has carried a department-scoped grant since `0002` |
| `OWNER` | resolves | `documents.created_by` |
| `MANAGER_OF_OWNER` | **refused at save** | There is no user-to-manager edge. `departments.manager_party_id` names a **party**, and party-to-user is not a resolvable relation in this schema — FR-ORG-002's reporting line is unbuilt. Resolving it by guesswork would route an approval to somebody chosen by a coincidence of data |
| `EXPRESSION` | **refused at save** | JSON Logic returning a user id or a role code. The evaluator exists (**D-10**), the *context* does not: §6.1's condition context is document, formData, variables and actor, and an expression resolving to a principal needs a directory this sprint has no reader for |

**Refused at save rather than at run time**, which is `jfss.rs`'s own discipline — *a definition is written once and rendered thousands of times, and the render path has no good failure*. A workflow that publishes cleanly and then cannot assign its first task is a stalled instance nobody is told about, which is exactly the failure #176 AC1 names.

This is a **registry-level** constraint of the kind JWSS §1.3 anticipates rather than a departure from the specification: the document defines six values and this implementation supports four, refuses two by name, and says so in the error. It is the same relationship `domain/lookup.rs`'s source allow-list has to JFSS's open `type` vocabulary.

### 4.4 The projections, rewritten whole on publish

`workflow_states` and `workflow_transitions` are regenerated from `definition_json` **in the publish transaction** (JWSS §9). Delete-then-insert for the definition's rows rather than a merge: the JSON is authoritative, so a projection that survives a republish is a row the authority did not ask for.

They are read by exactly two things this sprint — the foreign key of §3.2, and the designer that does not exist yet. **The engine does not read them.** It reads `definition_json`, because that is what §1 of the JWSS calls the single source of truth, and an engine that read the projection would be executing a copy: two answers to "what does this workflow do", which is the failure this sprint has been told twice to avoid at the status layer and which is no better one layer down.

### 4.5 Routes

| Route | Permission |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/v1/workflow/definitions` | `workflow:definition:read` |
| `POST /api/v1/workflow/definitions` | `workflow:definition:create` |
| `GET /api/v1/workflow/definitions/{id}` | `workflow:definition:read` |
| `PUT /api/v1/workflow/definitions/{id}` | `workflow:definition:update` — drafts only |
| `POST /api/v1/workflow/definitions/{id}/publication` | `workflow:definition:publish` |
| `POST /api/v1/workflow/definitions/{id}/revisions` | `workflow:definition:create` |
| `DELETE /api/v1/workflow/definitions/{id}` | `workflow:definition:delete` |

`publication` and `revisions` are the spellings `rad::handlers` already uses for a form; two names for one operation across two modules would be a difference with no reason behind it.

## 5. Item 2 — the instance, and its variables (#175)

### 5.1 What `start` does, and what it refuses

`engine::start(transaction, ctx)` — never a pool, because every caller of it is already inside somebody else's transaction (§8.3, §9.2). It:

1. Loads the definition **by id**, refuses it if it is not `ACTIVE`.
2. Allocates the instance reference (§3.3).
3. Inserts `workflow_instances` with `current_state = initialState`, `status = 'RUNNING'`, `business_key = document_number`, `started_by`.
4. Computes and inserts the declared variables (§5.2).
5. Enters the initial state — which generates that state's task if it declares one (§6.2), and projects the document's status (§8.2).

`STARTED` is in §7.4's `CHECK` and this engine never writes it. An instance is running from the moment it exists; a status that every row leaves within one statement is a status a reader will spend time on for nothing, and the honest thing is to say here that it is unused rather than to write it and move off it in the same transaction.

### 5.2 A variable the definition does not declare is refused, and the types are the definition's

[#175](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/175) AC2. `variables[]` in the JWSS document is the whole vocabulary: a key not in it is a 422 naming the key, and a value that does not parse as the declared `dataType` is a 422 naming the type. `workflow_variables.variable_value` is `TEXT` with a `data_type` beside it, so the parsing is Kelir's and there is nowhere else it could be done.

A variable's `source` is JSON Logic over §6.1's condition context, evaluated **at instance start** through `rad::evaluator` — the same engine, for **D-10**'s reason stated one more time: a second dialect on the surface that decides who approves an invoice would throw away the parity D-10 was bought for.

**A `source` that fails to evaluate leaves the variable unset rather than failing the submit.** That is **D-24**'s answer to the same question — an average over zero rows fails before the first keystroke — applied here: a document submitted at the moment its `source` cannot be computed is a document whose approval never starts, and the failure would surface at the submit, on a person who has done nothing wrong.

### 5.3 The instance's state is the only copy of where the process is

AC3. `workflow_instances.current_state` is it. `workflow_tasks` carries a status of its own, and that is the *task's* status rather than the process's — the module doc (§8.2) distinguishes them in the same paragraph that distinguishes the three document-status notions, because the pattern is the same and the reader meeting it a second time should meet it in the same place.

The state moves by compare-and-swap: `UPDATE ... WHERE current_state = <the state the transition was chosen against>`. Zero rows is a 409, not a retry. This is `move_status`'s statement one module over, written for the reason record 03 gave when it found the identical guard untested in the facility arm of the shared transition service — *a check-then-act that lives in a service is a rule somebody can step around by writing a second caller*.

## 6. Item 3 — the task, its assignment, and the claim (#176)

### 6.1 One state, one task, one transaction

AC1. Entering a state generates the task that state declares, in the transaction that moved the instance. There is no separate task-generation pass and there is no queue: a state that says it needs approval and a process with no task to approve it is the stalled instance the issue names, and two transactions is how it happens.

### 6.2 Sequential, as a constraint rather than an intention

An instance is in one state; a state declares at most one `task` (JWSS §3, `task` is an object rather than an array). So an instance has at most one open task, and `0024` says so:

```sql
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_workflow_tasks_open_per_instance
    ON workflow_tasks (workflow_instance_id)
    WHERE status IN ('CREATED', 'ASSIGNED', 'IN_PROGRESS') AND deleted_at IS NULL;
```

When FR-WF-016 is scheduled, this index is the thing that has to be argued with — which is the point. Parallel approval will be a design decision taken deliberately, against a constraint that says what the current design assumes, rather than discovered as an emergent property of code that turned out to allow it.

### 6.3 Assignment resolves to a user **or** a role, and never to both

AC2. `service/assignment.rs` is the only place this question is answered, and it answers with a `ResolvedAssignment`:

| Rule | `assignee_user_id` | `candidate_role_id` | `candidate_department_id` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `USER` | the user | — | — |
| `OWNER` | the document's `created_by` | — | — |
| `ROLE` | — | the role | — |
| `DEPARTMENT_ROLE` | — | the role | the scope (§6.3.1) |

**A task assigned to a role has no assignee until somebody claims it**, which is the difference #179 AC1 has to be able to show a person. Writing both — an owner *and* a role, or the first holder of a role as its assignee — would erase that difference at the moment the task is created, and the inbox would be unable to tell an unclaimed queue item from work that is already somebody's.

**A rule that resolves to nobody is refused at the transition**, not stored as a task with no assignee: a `USER` whose id is not a live user in this tenant, an `OWNER` on a document with no `created_by`, a `ROLE` whose code names no live role. The transition fails, the transaction rolls back, and the caller is told which rule and which value. An unassignable task is an approval that has stopped, and the moment to find out is the moment it was created.

#### 6.3.1 `departmentScope`

`REQUESTED_DEPARTMENT` is `documents.requested_for_department_id`; `OWNER_DEPARTMENT` is the creating user's department; any other value is a department **code**, resolved in the tenant. A scope that resolves to nothing is refused with the rest of §6.3.

### 6.4 The seam delegation occupies, named here and in the code

AC4, and it is the reason **D-13** unscheduled [#24](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/24) with a return condition rather than dropping it.

JWSS §5.1 already says where delegation goes: *"Delegation windows (`delegations`, Database Schema §3.8) are applied by the assignment resolver **after the rule resolves**; they are not part of the rule."* This plan does not invent a seam; it builds the resolver so that sentence is implementable, and says so in `assignment.rs`'s module documentation in those words.

Concretely: `resolve` has two phases and they are separate functions. `normalize` turns the JWSS rule — object form or §5.2 shorthand — into a principal; `direct` turns a principal into a `ResolvedAssignment` against the tenant's tables. **A delegation window redirects between them**, and it can do so without either function changing, because a window rewrites *who the principal is* and touches neither the definition's rule nor the columns the task is written to.

**No empty function is written for it.** A no-op named `apply_delegations` would be a reader's first assumption that delegation exists and does nothing, which is worse than a documented gap — the failure **D-13** was avoiding when it refused to leave `delegations` a table with a writer and no reader. What Sprint 11's [#184](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/184) inherits is a boundary and a paragraph, which is what its entry criterion asks it to confirm before starting.

### 6.5 The claim, and the two callers who both want it

AC3. `POST /api/v1/workflow/tasks/{id}/claim`, permission `workflow:task:execute`. One statement:

```sql
UPDATE workflow_tasks SET assignee_user_id = $caller, status = 'ASSIGNED', ...
WHERE tenant_id = $1 AND id = $2 AND deleted_at IS NULL
  AND assignee_user_id IS NULL AND status = 'CREATED'
```

Zero rows is a **409 naming who holds it**, after a read that says whether it was claimed or completed — two different answers a caller acts on differently. The caller must hold the candidate role: checked in the same transaction, against `user_roles`, not against the token's `roles` claim, for §10.2's reason.

The concurrency test runs **above the pool ceiling** — the level [#118](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/118) showed a fix's own tests can fail to reach — and asserts exactly one 200 and the rest 409. It is **seen to fail** first against a read-then-write claim (§2.9), and the pull request cites the red run against the named test. Per the [Sprint 9 retrospective](../retrospectives/07.%20Sprint%209%20Retrospective.md)'s second action, it **enumerates every status the product may legitimately answer** rather than asserting `409` and treating anything else as a pass.

### 6.6 What a task generation audits

AC5. `Workflow.TaskCreated`, action `CREATE`, object type `WORKFLOW_TASK`, carrying the instance, the document, the transition (`from`, `to`, `action`) that caused it, and the resolved assignment. **The transition is the payload's point**: a task that appears with no record of what produced it is the question "why am I being asked to approve this" with no answer.

## 7. Item 4 — approve and reject (#177)

### 7.1 One function moves a process, and this is it

`engine::fire(transaction, instance, action, actor)`. Approve, reject, the initial entry, and every Sprint 11 action are calls to it with a different `action`. Its steps:

1. Read the instance `FOR UPDATE`.
2. Choose the transition: from `current_state`, matching `action`, first whose `condition` holds, the unconditioned fallback last (S7). No match is a 422 naming the state and the actions available from it.
3. Write the instance's new state — compare-and-swap (§5.3).
4. Complete the task that the old state's spec created, if there was one.
5. Enter the new state: generate its task (§6.2), or, if it is final, complete the instance with its `outcome`.
6. Project the document's status (§8.2).

**Conditions are evaluated here and this is not FR-WF-015.** S7's fallback ordering is a *structural* rule the validator enforces and the engine must honour to execute a compliant definition at all; conditional routing as a feature — authoring conditions, the designer, the variable surface behind them — is [#186](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/186) in Sprint 11. A `fire` that ignored `condition` would execute a valid JWSS document incorrectly, which is not a deferral.

### 7.2 The check that guards the write, and the lock that covers what it read

AC3, and it is the fifth time this project has met the shape. The task is read `FOR UPDATE` **in the transaction that writes it**, and the instance is locked too — because the check reads the *instance's* state to choose a transition, and coding standard §2.5 says to choose the lock by what the check read.

**Two rows, and therefore an ordering.** Every path in this module takes the **instance first, then the task**, and `engine.rs` says so at the top. Two paths taking them in opposite orders is a deadlock at exactly the concurrency the feature is for, and it is a defect no single-threaded test can see.

### 7.3 A decided task cannot be decided again

AC2. The predicate is in the statement — `AND status IN ('CREATED', 'ASSIGNED', 'IN_PROGRESS')` — and zero rows is a **409 naming the current status**, after a re-read. AC4's test is concurrent approve **and** reject on one task, above the pool ceiling, asserting exactly one outcome and that the instance state and the document status agree with whichever won. It is seen to fail first.

### 7.4 Who may act, and it is not the permission

AC5. `workflow:task:execute` says the caller may work tasks at all. **Which task** is a second question, answered against the row:

> The caller may act on a task when they are its `assignee_user_id`, **or** when it has no assignee and they hold its `candidate_role_id`.

A third party holding `workflow:task:execute` gets a 403. The test proves it and is **seen to fail** against a build where the row check is removed — the permission alone still passing is precisely the false pass §2.9 is about, because a suite that only ever calls with the assignee cannot tell the two apart.

**A holder of the role may act without claiming first**, and claiming stays useful: it is how a person tells the rest of the queue they have started. Requiring a claim would add a step whose only effect is a second round trip, and would make the inbox's "unclaimed" state a gate rather than information.

### 7.5 No comment field, and the column stays empty

The sprint plan puts FR-TASK-006 in Sprint 11 explicitly. `workflow_tasks.comment` and `approval_decisions.comment` exist from `0024` and **nothing writes them this sprint**, which is `documents.security_level`'s arrangement one sprint earlier and the same honest encoding of a cut: the column is there because §7 put it there, the product does not fill it, and this paragraph is where a reader finds out which.

The cost is real and belongs here rather than in a retrospective: **a rejection recorded this sprint has no reason on it.** [#182](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/182) adds an optional field to a request and a column write; it does not reshape anything, which is why taking it early would be scope and leaving it is not a debt.

### 7.6 What a decision writes, and the two rows that are not duplicates

* `approval_decisions` — the formal record: who, which task, which instance, the decision, the level. §7.8 calls it "denormalized from tasks for reporting and signature binding".
* `workflow_task_history` — append-only, one row per *task status* move.

They are not one row twice and `mod.rs` says which is which, because this sprint has been told twice already what happens when two records of one event have no stated relationship. `approval_decisions` answers *what was decided about this document*; `workflow_task_history` answers *what happened to this task*. **Neither is FR-WF-012**, which is the document's own "how did it get here" and is [#181](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/181) in Sprint 11 — and #181's own text says it must distinguish itself from the audit trail, so it will be the third statement of the same distinction and should read like the first two.

Audit: `Workflow.TaskCompleted` (naming convention §7's own example) with action `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, carrying who, which task, the transition, and the resulting state — AC6's list exactly.

## 8. Item 5 — the document–workflow seam (#178)

### 8.1 Where the instance starts, and what happens when there is no workflow

`document::service::submit` gains one step, **inside the transaction it already has**: after the status is written and the history row appended, it resolves the type's workflow binding and calls `engine::start`.

The binding is `document_type_workflows`, in `priority` order, first match wins, honouring `status = 'ACTIVE'` and the `valid_from`/`valid_to` window. **`condition_expression` is not evaluated this sprint** — it is a `TEXT` column holding the superseded string form (`'amount <= 10000000'`), JWSS §1.1 supersedes it in favour of JSON Logic, and a binding *selection* condition is FR-WF-015's territory. A binding carrying one is skipped with a `warn`, rather than silently treated as unconditional, which would route a document by a rule nobody wrote.

**A type with no binding submits and starts nothing** ([#187](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/187) AC4). Not every document is approved, and this is a valid configuration rather than a missing one — so the submit path's shape is *if a binding resolves*, never *the binding must resolve*.

The submit already runs `numbering_service::allocate_in` inside this transaction and must not acquire a second pooled connection (**D-35**, coding standard §2.5). `engine::start` therefore takes `&mut PgTransaction` and never `&AppState`, which is stated at its signature — this is the third time this project has written that defect and the first time the type signature refuses it.

### 8.2 The projection, and the three status notions in the words the module doc must use

AC2, AC4, AC5. `modules/workflow/mod.rs` says this, and #178 requires it to be in these terms:

> **`record_status` answers "how far has this record got through governance"** — a master-data record is drafted, approved, active, suspended, archived (FR-MDM-007, #99). It is a property of a record about a real-world thing that keeps existing.
>
> **`workflow_instances.current_state` answers "where is this process"**, and it is the single source of truth for that (#175 AC3). It is a state of *this definition*, so it is free-form and means whatever the workflow that declared it means.
>
> **`documents.status` answers "where is this document in its own life"** (#169), and from this sprint on, **for a document with a live instance it is a projection of the instance's state and not a parallel record.** The projection is one-way. A workflow transition sets it; setting it does not move the workflow.
>
> The map from state to status is `mapsToDocumentStatus` **in the definition**, never in code (AC4). A new workflow says what its own states mean for a document, and adding one needs no backend change.

**Enforcing the one-way rule costs `PUT /documents/{id}/status` something, and this is the decision.** That route refuses a document that has a live instance, naming the instance and the action that would move it. The alternative — letting it through — is a document whose status disagrees with the process driving it, which is the exact defect this item exists to prevent. AC2's *"if a manual override is wanted, it is a separate, audited action"* is a future route with its own permission, and it is not built here: nothing has asked for it, and the refusal is what makes the need visible if anything does.

The projection is written **in the transition's transaction** (AC3) and audited as `Document.StatusChanged` with action `TRANSITION`, carrying the workflow transition that caused it (AC6) — not as an ordinary update, so that the trail distinguishes a status a person moved from a status a process moved.

### 8.3 What a test reaches, and what the index catches

AC1's refusal has two layers and §2.5 requires the second to be exercised or declared. The service refuses a second instance after reading; `uq_workflow_instances_live_document` refuses it under concurrency. **The test arranges the interleaving rather than racing for it** — one transaction holds the document's lock while a second submit blocks on it — which is `an_edit_blocked_by_a_publish_applies_to_nothing`'s technique and the one coding standard §2.5 names.

## 9. Item 7 — a document type carries a workflow (#187)

### 9.1 What is already built, and what is missing

`document_type::domain::WorkflowBinding` and `repository::replace_workflows` have existed since #157: a type's workflows are a collection on the aggregate, sent whole on create and update, priority-ordered. **The gap is that nothing checks the id names anything**, and the domain type says so in a comment ending *"the workflow migration is what makes it checkable"*. This is that.

### 9.2 The check, in the transaction, under the lock

AC2. `check_workflow_bindings` joins `check_bindings` in `document_type::service`, and it is the same shape for the same reason: `SELECT ... FOR SHARE` on each named `workflow_definitions` row, inside the write's transaction, before the write.

A definition that does not exist in this tenant is a 422 naming `workflows.N.workflowDefinitionId` with `NOT_FOUND`; one that is not `ACTIVE` is `NOT_PUBLISHED`, and the message says why — an instance executes the revision it starts against, so binding a draft would bind a definition that can still change under documents already routed by it. That is `check_bindings`' `NOT_PUBLISHED` arm, restated for the other artefact, and the wording matches so a reader meets one rule rather than two.

The comment in `WorkflowBinding` that says the id is unverifiable is deleted in the same commit. A comment describing a state that has ended is worse than no comment, which is **D-34** one document over.

### 9.3 Rebinding, decided rather than defaulted

AC3, and it is [#165](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/165)'s question about forms asked about workflows. **D-30** answered the form version by refusing the rebinding when documents exist that pinned nothing.

> **Changing a type's workflow binding is allowed, and running instances are unaffected. Future submissions of that type use the new binding.**

And unlike the form case, **there is no population to guard**: `workflow_instances.workflow_definition_id` is `NOT NULL` and names a revision row, so every running instance has pinned one and nothing can reach it through the type. What made D-30 hard was `documents.form_id` being nullable — a document that pinned nothing renders against whatever the type currently binds. No instance can be in that condition, so the guard D-30 needed has nothing to guard, and **this is why the answer differs rather than an inconsistency between two similar surfaces**. `guard_rebinding`'s doc comment carries that sentence, next to the one that explains the form case.

The test is the one that makes the claim checkable: a type bound to definition A, a document submitted and running, the type rebound to B, and the running instance still on A with its current state intact.

### 9.4 The surface, and the frontend

No new route. The binding is a field of the document-type aggregate, so `PUT /api/v1/document-types/{id}` already carries it, which is AC1's *"the same configuration surface #165 established"*. Every route on that surface is already in the OpenAPI document, which is AC6 — and `every_annotated_route_reaches_the_document` covers the workflow module's new ones.

Frontend: `features/workflow/` gets nothing this sprint. The document-type configuration screen is Sprints 14–16 (**D-2**); the binding is configured through the API, exactly as the form binding it sits beside is. §10.4's e2e flow does it that way, and that is what makes the sprint's exit demo performable rather than described.

## 10. Item 6 — the task inbox and the task detail (#179)

### 10.1 The module exists, and it owns no storage

`modules/task_inbox/` is a *view* module: it has a domain, a service and handlers, and its one statement lives in `workflow::repository::inbox` for §2's reason. Its service calls `workflow::service`, holds no SQL and invents no permission (§3.4).

**Why it is a module at all rather than three more routes on `workflow`.** FR-TASK-* is its own SRS area (§4.9) with nine requirements, seven of them still to build, and the inbox is a surface over the workflow engine rather than part of it — the same relationship `rad::lookup` has to master data. The module boundary is where that stays visible.

### 10.2 The visibility rule, in the statement

AC1, AC3. One statement, and the rule is a predicate in it:

> A caller sees a task when it is in their tenant **and** either they are its `assignee_user_id`, **or** it has no assignee and its `candidate_role_id` is a role the caller currently holds.

**The roles come from `user_roles`, not from the token's `roles` claim.** A claim is a snapshot from sign-in: a role granted an hour ago is not in it, and a role revoked an hour ago still is. The second of those is the one that matters — an inbox built from a stale claim shows a person tasks they may no longer act on, and #177's own check would then refuse them, which reads as a broken product and is a leak. `user_roles`' validity window (`valid_from`, `valid_to`) is honoured in the same predicate.

The cross-user test **reaches the query rather than asserting around it** — the #106/#121 lesson that cost three sprints of coverage findings — and the fixture holds a second user, a second role and a second tenant, because one subject cannot tell *scoped* from *unscoped* (§2.9, and it is [#218](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/218)'s single root cause).

### 10.3 Mine and unclaimed are different situations, and the payload says which

AC1. Every row carries `assignment: "MINE" | "ROLE"`. Not a nullable `assigneeId` for the reader to interpret: an unclaimed role task and a task already mine are different situations for the person looking at them, and a client that has to derive that will derive it differently in two places.

`meta` reports page, pageSize and total (AC2), through `crate::extract::QueryParams`, which is what puts a bad `page` **inside** the error envelope (AC5). **This does not close [#122](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/122)** and the status report says so rather than implying otherwise — the API-wide instances are unchanged. It does mean the sprint adds no new one.

### 10.4 The detail, and what a task has to say for itself

AC4: *"a task that says only 'approve?' is a task its holder cannot responsibly action."* `GET /api/v1/tasks/{id}` therefore answers with the task, **the document** (number, title, type, status), **the instance** (its definition, name and current state), and **the decision being asked** — the transitions available from the current state with their action, target state and the target state's name, read from the definition.

The document half is subject to the document's own read permission, delegated to `document::service::get_document` rather than re-checked here — #161's construction, for the third time in three sprints, and the same reason: *a task detail cannot open what the document surface does not, because it is that surface.*

## 12. Decisions this plan takes, and the one it owes

Taken here, and each is stated at the code as well as here:

1. **§3.3** — one reference counter table serves instances and tasks, keyed by prefix and year.
2. **§4.2** — validation runs on save *and* at publish, and `guards`/`actions` are stored without being executed, said once where a reader will find it.
3. **§4.3** — `MANAGER_OF_OWNER` and `EXPRESSION` are refused **at save**, naming what would have to exist.
4. **§4.4** — the engine reads `definition_json` and never the projections.
5. **§5.1** — `STARTED` is never written; an instance is `RUNNING` from the moment it exists.
6. **§5.2** — a variable `source` that cannot be evaluated leaves the variable unset rather than failing the submit (**D-24**, applied one module over).
7. **§6.2** — one open task per instance, as a partial unique index, so parallel approval has to argue with a constraint.
8. **§6.3** — an assignment that resolves to nobody fails the transition rather than creating an unassignable task.
9. **§7.2** — instance first, then task, on every path in the module.
10. **§7.4** — a role holder may act without claiming; the claim is information, not a gate.
11. **§8.2** — `PUT /documents/{id}/status` refuses a document with a live instance.
12. **§9.3** — a type's workflow binding may be changed, running instances are unaffected, and the reason it differs from **D-30** is that no instance can be unpinned.

Two of these are large enough to belong in the [Product Backlog](02.%20Product%20Backlog.md) §6 rather than only here, and go there as **D-36** (§8.2, because it withdraws a capability the document surface shipped in Sprint 9) and **D-37** (§4.3, because it is a documented standard's vocabulary being narrowed by an implementation).

**Owed, and it is §7.5's:** whether a decision without a reason is acceptable for the length of one sprint. It is taken the way the sprint plan says — the comment is Sprint 11's — and it is named here so that if the closing pass finds a rejection nobody can explain, the finding has somewhere to attach.

Any decision taken against this plan during construction goes to the [Product Backlog](02.%20Product%20Backlog.md) §6 as a numbered decision rather than being settled inside this document.
