# Phase 6 MVP Close Plan

**Status:** Adopted · **Last updated:** 2026-09-04

This plans the three things standing between `main` as it is today and **`v0.6.0` — the MVP** — as a tag. It sits **one level below** the [Sprint Plan](01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md), whose Sprint 13 §Exit states the four criteria; nothing here changes them, and where the two disagree the sprint plan wins.

It exists because **Phase 6's scope is complete and the phase is not closed**, which is the same shape the [Phase 5 Close Plan](06.%20Phase%205%20Close%20Plan.md) was written for and the same distance apart. Sprint 13's ten construction items are on `main`, the three findings its independent pass produced are fixed and merged ([#321](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/321), `7516137`), and [status report 14](../status/14.%20Sprint%2013%20Status.md) is `Final`. What is missing is not code.

Related documents: the checklist this plan executes is [Release Process](../../docs/standards/04.%20Release%20Process.md) §4 and §7, the rollback rule is its §6, the MVP's definition is SRS §9 and the mapping is [Product Backlog](02.%20Product%20Backlog.md) §5, and the records this plan produces follow [record 13](../verifications/13.%20Sprint%2013%20Independent%20Pass.md)'s shape.

## 2. The order, and why the browser flow comes first

**The browser flow goes before the §9 verification, and the §9 verification before the rehearsal and the tag.** The sprint plan's list is a list of criteria, not a sequence, and the [Phase 5 Close Plan](06.%20Phase%205%20Close%20Plan.md) §2 already established that executing it in written order is a mistake this project has paid for.

Here the ordering argument is different and sharper. **Criterion 1 says §9 is verified *against the running product*.** Two of the fifteen are *users can upload attachments* and *comments can be added*. Their surfaces shipped in Sprint 13, and their only evidence today is 545 jsdom component tests.

**That is the construction plan's own §1.2 argument, one level down.** §1.2 established that *a criterion that says **users can** is not met by an endpoint*, bought two screens with three `Should` rows to fix it, and was right. A component test is not a user either. Verifying §9 before the flow exists would sign two criteria off on the class of evidence this sprint rejected — and would do it in the record that says the MVP is met.

So:

1. **§3 — the browser flow.** It produces the evidence §4 reads for two of its fifteen rows.
2. **Anything it finds, fixed.** Not a step that can be scheduled, because a flow whose purpose is to drive a path nothing has driven cannot have its consequences planned. The Sprint 12 exit demo found three defects in its first ten minutes; this is the first time anybody has uploaded a file through this product's own screen.
3. **§4 — the §9 verification**, against release images, once §3's evidence exists.
4. **§5 — the release rehearsal**, against the artefact that will be tagged.
5. **§6 — the tag**, and the release record.

**Steps 1 and 2 may interleave. No other pair may.** In particular the rehearsal must not precede the verification: rehearsing a release of code that then changes proves the rehearsal, not the release.

## 3. Step 1 — the browser flow over attachments and comments

**One flow, driven in a browser against the release stack**: attach a file to a document, watch it become downloadable, download it, post a comment, and read both off the timeline.

It is one spec rather than an extension of `a-document-is-created-and-submitted.spec.ts`, because that file's second test exists to assert that *no panel renders a placeholder* and its subject is the workspace's tabs. This one's subject is a file and a conversation.

**The scan gate is the part that needs designing rather than writing.** Upload returns immediately with `virus_scan_status = PENDING`; the worker clears it asynchronously. The flow therefore **polls the panel until the download control appears**, with a timeout generous enough for a cold ClamAV — the measurement is ~169 ms for 25 MiB against a scanner that is already up, and the compose stack's `clamav` reports healthy about 20 seconds after start. A fixed wait would be flaky in the direction that reads as a product defect.

**What it must assert, and why each one:**

| Assertion | Why |
|---|---|
| The file is listed after upload, with its own name | SRS §9 criterion 6, *users can upload attachments*, in a browser |
| It is **not** downloadable while `PENDING`, and the reason says so | [#246](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/246) AC3 and #295's own second criterion: three refusals, not one spinner |
| It becomes downloadable once the scan clears, and the bytes come back | The gate opening is the half no test drives today |
| A comment posts and appears | SRS §9 criterion 11, *comments can be added*, in a browser |
| Both reach the Activity timeline | The seam between three modules, which is where Sprint 12's findings lived |

**It is also the only assertion in the suite that fails on an unwired object store**, and that is the second reason to write it. The [Sprint 13 retrospective](../retrospectives/11.%20Sprint%2013%20Retrospective.md)'s fifth action asks for a one-shot container's failure to fail *something*; `minio-init` now ends with `mc ls kelir/kelir` and exits 1 without the bucket, and **nothing observes that exit code** — `backend` declares no `service_completed_successfully` dependency and `deploy.sh` smoke-tests only `/health/ready` and `/version`. A stack whose bucket step fails still deploys green today. This flow is what turns that into a red pipeline, and it is why Sprint 12's D1 and D2 would have been caught.

**It runs in CI**, in the existing `End-to-end (browser)` job, which already stands the release stack up from `deploy/staging` and drives Playwright against it.

## 4. Step 2 — SRS §9 verified against release images

**Fifteen criteria, one at a time, against a running deployment** — the sprint plan's words are *against the running product rather than against the backlog's own coverage table*, and [Product Backlog](02.%20Product%20Backlog.md) §5 says of itself that it is this project's belief about §9 and is not evidence.

**The output is a verification record**, `projects/verifications/14. MVP Verification.md`, with a row per criterion naming what was executed and what answered. A criterion demonstrated by a passing test cites the test; one demonstrated by hand says so.

**Thirteen of the fifteen are demonstrable from work already merged.** The two that are not without §3 are criteria 6 and 11 — which is the whole reason §3 precedes this step.

**Two rows need reading before they are ticked, because their evidence is not where the table says:**

- **Criterion 13**, *audit log records approvals and status changes*, is covered by FR-AUD-001/003 from **Sprint 3** and FR-WF-012 from Sprint 11 — **D-44** corrected the row that said Sprint 12. Verify against the trail that exists, not against the audit *search* Sprint 13 added, which is FR-AUD-004 and a different requirement.
- **Criterion 15**, *the application runs using Docker Compose*, was **false for attachments until 2026-08-31** — the deployed stack had no storage configuration and no scanner, and [record 11](../verifications/11.%20Sprint%2012%20Exit%20Demo.md) §3 found it. It is verified against the staging compose, from release images, not the development one.

**Against release images, not the working tree** (§7 step 6 of the [construction plan](08.%20Sprint%2013%20MVP%20Construction%20Plan.md)), because the only two demos this project has run both found defects that only a deployment shows.

## 5. Step 3 — the release check, rehearsed at the close

§2's rule 3, rehearsed **before** the tag so a failure is a finding rather than a hotfix.

**The version bump comes first.** `kelir-backend/Cargo.toml` and `kelir-frontend/package.json` both read `0.5.0` and move to `0.6.0` in a single `chore(release): v0.6.0` commit ([release process](../../docs/standards/04.%20Release%20Process.md) §4 step 3).

**The `CHANGELOG` section exists and needs curating, not writing.** `[Unreleased]` holds every Phase 6 entry and opens *"Phase 6 opens: a document starts carrying the things people put on it"* — which was true when it was written and is not the heading of a closing phase. It becomes `[0.6.0]` with a date, and **the notes must say this one is the MVP** as plainly as `v0.5.0`'s said it is not (**D-1**).

**Nine migrations are new since `v0.5.0` — `0031` through `0039` — and one of them is the reason this step is rehearsed rather than reasoned about:**

| Migration | The N−1 question it raises |
|---|---|
| **`0031_attachment`** | **The only one that touches a table `v0.5.0` knows.** It adds `fk_document_type_attachment_rules_category_id` to `document_type_attachment_rules`, which `0015_document` created in Sprint 9. Every other object it creates is new |
| `0032`, `0033`, `0034`, `0037`, `0038` | Tables `v0.5.0` has never heard of — comments, activity, notifications, external references, change requests |
| `0036_comment_thread` | `ALTER TABLE comments`, and `comments` is `0032` — also newer than `v0.5.0`, so the tightening is invisible to it |
| `0035`, `0039` | **No DDL at all.** Permission rows and seeded notification channels and templates |

**The exposure on `0031` is smaller than it looks, and the rehearsal is what says so rather than this paragraph.** `category_id` was declared `NOT NULL` by `0015` with no foreign key, and **`v0.5.0`'s source references `document_type_attachment_rules` nowhere** — no release has ever written a row into it. A constraint can only reject rows that exist. That is the claim the rehearsal tests, and §6's rule is that it is tested.

**The rehearsal, per §6:** migrate an empty database with the **`v0.6.0`** binary, then start the **`v0.5.0`** image against it and require `/health/ready`. **Record the result either way** — a blank row reads as *passed* to everyone who comes after.

**Then the smoke pass**, from release images rather than the working tree, per §4 step 7: `/health`, `/health/ready`, `/health/live`, and `/version` reporting the expected version *and* SHA.

**One thing this release has that the last one did not:** the four infrastructure images are pinned (**D-62**), so the stack the rehearsal exercises is the stack a later deployment gets. `v0.5.0` was rehearsed against `:latest`.

## 6. Step 4 — the tag, and the record

Checklist items 5 and 6: an annotated `v0.6.0` on the release commit with the changelog section as its message, then images built **from the tag** with `KELIR_BUILD_SHA` supplied — it is baked in at build time and is what `/version` reports, so it is not optional. `v0.5.0` left that row open for two days and it is the row most easily forgotten.

**The release record is `projects/releases/06. Release v0.6.0.md`**, from [the template](../releases/00.%20Release%20Checklist%20Template.md), carrying the rollback result from §5 including a failure.

**Checklist items 7 and 8 cannot be satisfied as written** — there is no staging host and no production; **D-9** retired the staging clause in favour of the compose stack. The record says which check stood in for which rather than leaving two blocking rows looking passed.

**The release notes carry one sentence this project has been owed since 2026-08-11:** that `v0.6.0` is the MVP, that SRS §9 is met in full, and — per §7 — on what evidence the claim rests.

## 7. The decision this plan cannot take

> **Sprint plan §2 gets an owner and a date, or it changes.** The [Sprint 13 retrospective](../retrospectives/11.%20Sprint%2013%20Retrospective.md)'s first action carries a deadline of **this tag**, and it is the fifth sprint the question has been open.

**The pass ran, and the rule still did not fire.** [Record 13](../verifications/13.%20Sprint%2013%20Independent%20Pass.md) exists because the product owner directed a pass after the sprint had closed — not because §2 named anybody, not on a date §2 set. A control that needs an instruction each time it fires is a practice, not a control, and the retrospective's action was written to refuse a sixth restatement of that.

The action's own terms: **if neither branch is taken by the tag, the second one is taken by default** — §2 says what a self-pass is worth, `author-verified` becomes the expected label, and the release record says what the tag rests on. **That default is worse than naming somebody and better than pretending**, and this plan cannot choose between them. It can only record that §6 is where the choice becomes visible.

## 8. What could stop this, and what to do

| Risk | What it looks like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **The browser flow finds a defect in criterion 6 or 11** | The two criteria the MVP is being tagged for do not work through a browser | **Fix before the tag.** That is what §3 is for, and finding it here rather than at the tag is the entire argument for the order in §2 |
| **The scan never clears in CI** | The flow times out waiting for `CLEAN` and reads as flaky | Diagnose before widening the timeout. A gate that does not open is [#246](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/246)'s failure mode, and a longer wait would hide it |
| **A §9 criterion cannot be demonstrated** | The MVP is not met, whatever the coverage table says | **Do not tag.** SRS §9 is not this project's to rewrite — the construction plan §1.2 said so about the same two criteria |
| **The N−1 rehearsal fails on `0031`** | The `v0.5.0` binary will not start against a `v0.6.0` schema | A real N−1 break; the release notes need a maintenance-window plan (§6). Do not tag around it |
| **No party is named for §2 by the tag** | §7's default takes effect silently | Take it **explicitly**: amend §2, and say in release record 06 what the tag rests on. Silence is the sixth restatement |
| **Docker is unavailable** | §3, §4 and §5 all stall | They are the same blocker and the same fix. None is worth attempting without it |
