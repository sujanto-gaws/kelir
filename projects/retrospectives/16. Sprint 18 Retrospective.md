## What Didn't

**The independent pass was not dispatched at mid-sprint refinement, again.** Construction plan 14 §7 step 7 put the dispatch there and predicted the outcome in writing: with the release moved to Sprint 19, nothing would force it. Nothing did. **The pass was dispatched on 2026-09-16, at the close, because the product owner chose it over closing `author-verified`.** **Sprints 17 and 18 are the first two sprints without a release since the pass was tied to the tag, and neither produced a pass at refinement.** Sprint 17 remains unread, and record 16's brief excludes it.

**The commit that documented the trailer gap has no trailer.** Item 6 ([#459](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/459)) wrote into §2 that ~~a reader's independence is checked by `git log` against `Claude-Session` trailers~~ **a reader's independence *could* be checked that way** (corrected 2026-09-16, [record 16](../verifications/16.%20Sprint%2018%20Independent%20Pass.md) §7.5), and that nothing runs that check. **#459's own squash commit carries no `Claude-Session` trailer**, so no `git log` can say who wrote it. #450–#458 all carry one. The author is this document's author, which is known only because this document says so. **Record 16 §7 measured the gap more widely than this paragraph did**: git's trailer parser reads **none** of the session lines on `main`, and **64 of 268** commits co-authored by Claude have none, four of them this author's from 2026-09-16.

**The independent pass was dispatched as a subagent of #459's author, and the dispatch was reported as something else.** The session that wrote #459 launched the reader with `isolation: "remote"` and told the product owner it would run as a separate session. **It ran locally, as a subagent, under the dispatching session's ID.** Record 16's reader found this itself, from `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID`, and made it finding 1. It did not let a clean `git log` stand for it. **#459 then needed a second reader, and only the product owner could start one**: a top-level session in its own worktree, which landed as record 16 §7. Record 15's reader had also been dispatched by its author. **The mechanism is the same in both: a session cannot dispatch its own independent reader.**

**Four of this sprint's controls claim more than they do**, and a reader found all four in one pass (record 16 findings 2–5). The pattern refusal's registry says the divergence is closed, and about twenty constructs are still stored. The new refusal messages are wrong for every negated class. Release gate rule 10 accepts an unfiled follow-up four ways. Sprint report rule 4 accepts three things that are not a browser flow. **Each was built with probes and a positive control, and each probe set tested the shape its author expected.**

**Rows moved between sprints were recorded in the plan they left and not in the one they joined.** FR-RPT-006 was recorded as *labelled* in Sprint 19 when neither the issue nor the label existed; that was found and corrected on 2026-09-14. **#449 moved to Sprint 19 the same day**, is labelled `sprint:19`, and is still absent from the [sprint plan](../planning/01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md)'s Sprint 19 table, which says *there is deliberately nothing below this line*.

**An issue's premise went stale while it waited, and only the plan noticed.** #448 said the gate runs in a depth-1 clone. Item 3b changed that on 2026-09-14, and #448 was built on 2026-09-16 without its text being amended. **Construction plan 14 §8 carried the correction**, so the row was built on the right premise. It would not have been if the builder had read only the issue.

**A lockfile nearly misreported the sprint's largest row.** Item 5's pull request reads **4,007** added lines; 1,793 of them are `package-lock.json`. Against an estimate of 1,800–2,400, the raw figure says 1.7–2.2× and the real one says 0.9–1.2×. **The measure "added lines in the merged pull request" was never defined beyond those words**, and Sprint 17's table included 103 lines of `.sqlx` without saying so.

## Root Causes

**Every control that has worked binds a record or a gate that something reads. The two that failed this sprint bind a moment.** *Dispatch at mid-sprint refinement* and *add the session trailer* are both instructions addressed to whoever is working at that moment, and nothing reads either one when it is skipped. `sprint_reports_are_verified.rs` reads the report and made this sprint's exposure unsayable; it did not make the pass happen, as it said it would not. The trailer has no reader at all. **This is [retrospective 14](14.%20Sprint%2016%20Retrospective.md)'s finding a third time**, and the Sprint 19 release gate is the only thing that will bind the pass.

**A move is written by whoever is holding the plan being amended, and nobody is holding the other plan.** Plan 14 was the document open on 2026-09-14, so the move out was recorded carefully, with its reason. The sprint plan's Sprint 19 table is written by a different change, at a different time, and **no check reads a `sprint:N` label against the sprint's table**. FR-RPT-006 and #449 are the same defect in two directions: one row claimed where it did not exist, and one row existing where it is not claimed.

**A control's probes are chosen by its author, so they probe the author's model of the input.** #455 probed rows that open with the wrong link. It did not probe the word `none` inside another word, or a list broken by a blank line. #456 probed citation styles, not a comment or a foreign URL. #451 enumerated the constructs record 15 named, not the two grammars. **Record 15 §7 reached the same conclusion about the gate it defeated.** The probe practice is sound. What it cannot supply is an input its author did not think of, which is the whole case for a second reader.

**A measure used in a table becomes a definition only when someone writes it down.** *Added lines* meant a PR's `additions` in Sprint 17 because that is what `gh` returns. It never had to be chosen until a row added a dependency.

## The sprint, in one paragraph

Sprint 18 built all seven of its rows and, for the first time in two sprints, built them close to what they were estimated at. The correction came from measuring Sprint 17's overruns instead of arguing about them. Its best result is quiet: a gate asking whether a sprint's screens are driven by a browser merged before either screen, and both screens then shipped a browser flow without the gate refusing anything. **Its worst is the same finding as the last three sprints**: the independent pass did not run at refinement because nothing forced it, and it was dispatched at the close only because the product owner chose that. **Its most specific is its own**: the row that documented that the criterion depends on commit trailers was committed without one. Its author then dispatched the sprint's reader as a subagent carrying the same ID. **Only a session the product owner started could read that row.** The pattern under the pass, the trailer and both one-sided sprint moves is one this project keeps meeting from different sides: **a rule addressed to whoever is working at the moment holds only when something reads the result.**
