## Root Causes

**The merge control is a sentence addressed to the merger, and the merger is the person it reports to.** Plan 18 §7.3 wrote it as *a row is reported ready only when its body carries the campaign section*. The main session kept to it: it reported neither row 10 nor row 17 ready. The product owner merged both anyway, from drafts that said not to. Row 18's #677 went the same way, a draft marked ready and merged six seconds later. **This is [retrospective 14](14.%20Sprint%2016%20Retrospective.md)'s root cause applied to the merge button**: a control holds when it blocks, and fails when it asks for diligence. Here the diligence was asked of the one person no session can hold to it.

**The floor was set at Sprint 21's smallest row, not at what a campaign costs.** Retrospective 19 took 450 from Sprint 21's row 13, the minimum. **The campaign's tests do not shrink with the feature.** Row 10 carried 1,155 test lines on 46 feature lines, and row 13 carried 867 on 52. The seven campaign rows with under 260 feature lines, row 17 apart, came in at 686–1,727. Row 5 is not among them: it is a control, at 27 feature lines and 606 in total.

**A text row that pins its claims with mutation-checked tests runs a campaign under another name.** Row 16's six backend tests were each seen red under a scratch mutation, and its four screen sentences were pinned by specs. That is the campaign's work, priced at row 7's comparable, which carried 33 test lines.

**A comparable was matched by shape, not by its count of subjects.** Row 17 was sized against row 6 as *a walk and an ADR*. Row 6's walk covers 33 list routes. Row 17's covers 143 operations by 3, which is 429 cells (plan 18 §3.2).

**Plan 18 §5 grew from what its own updates mentioned.** Issues a row's builder or gate filed, and deferred to plan 19 in their own bodies, appeared in no update. So the list missed them, though §1.1 said every open issue had a position.
