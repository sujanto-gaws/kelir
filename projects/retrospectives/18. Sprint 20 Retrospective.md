Worktrees made the parallel work possible, and this is their cost. It is recorded, not actioned.

## Root Causes

**Nothing between a plan's order and the merge button carries the order.** CI checks what a PR contains, and it knows nothing about a different PR that should merge first. **The ADR half is the same gap inside a test.** `adr_records_are_current.rs` rule 4 accepts any `Draft` that names a blocker. The blocker ADR-0041 named was the row's own issue, #519, which was open while CI ran and was closed by the merge. **The sprint plan's exit criterion named a test that could not tell.** This is [retrospective 16](16.%20Sprint%2018%20Retrospective.md)'s *four controls claim more than they do*, one more time.

**Row 7 was sized by its label before its surface was counted, and nothing re-sized it afterwards.** Plan 16 §3.2 sized it on 2026-09-18 as *the integration migration, `modules::integration`, the permission rows, OpenAPI and an admin page*, against a comparable that had one endpoint. The analyst's criteria (plan 16 §7 step 2) arrived on 2026-09-24 and named three sub-resources and their permissions. **The estimate was never checked against the criteria.** A route, a dialog and a sub-resource are countable before construction. A *page* is not a unit.

**A builder's mutations test the builder's model of the code, and the campaign ran after the merge.** This is retrospective 16's root cause again, now measured: 44 of 48 mutations were outside the builders' lists. **Once per sprint at mid-sprint means the campaign sees whatever has merged by then.** So its findings are fixes to `main`, and a row that merges late escapes it.

**Kelir's CI depends on registries it does not control.** D-80 answered the first closure by moving the pin to another third party. D-92 answered the second by owning the copy, **for MinIO only**.

## The sprint, in one paragraph

Sprint 20 built what it was for. The outbox exists, JWSS `actions` run for the first time since ADR-0036, and FR-INT-001 shipped whole with a browser flow. The reading of the post-tag work landed first and handed the outbox its lock order. **What it did not do is let anyone else read it.** Its gates were thorough, and they found a double delivery, a secret in a URL and an unused index. Its campaign was real and found seventeen more gaps. Every one of those readers was the authoring session's own subagent, as §2 (b) now says out loud. **Its two slips were the same shape.** A plan said *this merges first*, and a criterion said *this test says so*, and nothing on the merge path read either sentence. **Its miss was a unit.** A row sized as *a page* was fifteen routes. **So the sprint's lesson is the project's lesson one level down: a control holds when it sits on the path the work actually takes.** The next plan puts merge order, sizing units and the campaign on that path.
