# Sprint 13 Retrospective — 2026-09-03

**Status:** Final · **Last updated:** 2026-09-03

**Sprint:** 13 (Phase 6, *Attachments, Comments, Activity* — **the MVP sprint**) · **Goal:** the collaboration surfaces become usable rather than merely present. **Phase 6 closes, the MVP is verified against SRS §9 in full, and `v0.6.0` is cut.**

**Sprint outcome:** **construction exceeded, goal missed.** · **Velocity note:** seven items planned after the §3.1 trade, **ten built**; seven of eight carried findings closed; all three step-0b actions landed; **none of the three exit steps ran.**

Landed with the [status report](../status/14.%20Sprint%2013%20Status.md), which is `Draft` and not `Final`, because the independent pass §2 requires has not happened **for the fifth sprint running** — this time at the boundary the whole plan was built around.

## What Worked

- **The allowance was scheduled by name and position, and it is the difference between this sprint and the last one.** [Sprint 12's fifth action](10.%20Sprint%2012%20Retrospective.md) asked for exactly that; [construction plan](../planning/08.%20Sprint%2013%20MVP%20Construction%20Plan.md) §5 put two findings ahead of item 1, placed four more *with* the items that touch their code, and marked one unschedulable and said why. **Seven of eight closed, against none of eight in Sprint 12.** The placement is what did it, not the intent: [#279](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/279) was placed with item 7, went to Sprint 14 when item 7 was cut, and came back and was fixed in the same sitting when item 7 did — because building the completed view meant touching the three statements the finding was about. **That is what "placed with an item" was supposed to mean, and it is the first time it has demonstrably paid.**
- **The exit was re-read against the construction plan at planning, and the re-read found two MVP criteria with no surface.** Sprint 12's fourth action; plan §1.2 is the re-read. *Users can upload attachments* and *comments can be added* had APIs and no screens, and **nothing in the sprint as planned would have built one** — items 4 and 5 were the epics' `Should` tails and were API work. Found at planning, it cost a paragraph and a trade. Found at the tag by whoever reads SRS §9, it would have cost the release.
- **The cut order was honest, so the reversal was cheap.** §3.1 cut the three rows the sprint plan already named as its cut line, in that order, and said what the cut cost. When the capacity turned out to exist, item 6 came back, then 7, then 8 — **last, because it was the lowest row, so its re-inclusion needed the most evidence.** A cut list that is really ordered can be walked back one row at a time; one that is a set has to be re-argued.
- **Three rules moved out of convention and into shape**, which is the Sprint 12 retrospective's best observation applied three more times. An external reference is its own table with no `virus_scan_status` column, so **a link cannot read `CLEAN`** — held by the schema rather than by the second screen remembering ([ADR-0031](../../docs/architectures/adr/0031.%20An%20External%20Reference%20Is%20Not%20an%20Attachment%20Row.md)). `count_for_document` reads the document's own columns, so **removing the join stops the crate compiling** rather than changing a number. `notify` takes the caller's transaction and returns its error, so a notification cannot outlive a rolled-back approval.
- **The decisions got a home and the practice took immediately.** [Standards/06](../../docs/standards/06.%20Architecture%20Decision%20Records.md), a template, a skill, and a one-time retrofit of **ADR-0001…0028** — twenty-eight records, twenty-one from the `D-n` table and seven from `architectures/01` — and then **D-50 through D-56 were filed as ADR-0029…0034 in the branches that took them**, which is the practice the retrofit is explicitly not. The standard settles what earns a record and what does not, so sixteen scope and planning decisions stayed where they were rather than being swept in.
- **An unsatisfiable acceptance criterion was diagnosed instead of rerun.** [#274](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/274) asks for *a failing run captured with the backend's log*. The log line existed; **no test binary had ever installed a subscriber**, so in `tests/` it was written to nothing, and every rerun would have produced the same absence. Four sprints of "seen once, cannot reproduce" was a tooling gap wearing a defect's clothes.
- **Two findings were produced by writing documentation.** Diffing the configuration reference against the code by hand, while adding a twelfth variable, found eleven missing rows ([#316](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/316)); writing one of those rows found that `KELIR_STORAGE_SECRET_KEY` defaulted to `minioadmin` in production ([#317](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/317)). Both are now held by a test rather than by the next person happening to look.

## What Didn't

- **The exit did not run — not partially, not late. Steps 5, 7 and 8 did not start.** No independent pass, no SRS §9 verification against release images, no `v0.6.0` rehearsal and no tag. `git tag` ends at `v0.5.0`; `projects/verifications/` ends at record 12; `projects/releases/` ends at record 05. **All four exit criteria are outstanding at the sprint that carries the MVP.**
- **The plan said twice that it fixed the pass's date, and it fixed no date.** §1.1: *"it fixes the date (§7, step 5)"*. §7 step 5: *"The independent pass, on a **fixed date** — §1.1"*. Neither states one; a grep for `2026-\d\d-\d\d` over the file returns eight dates and none of them is step 5's. So the escape clause — *if no party is named by step 5's date, §2 is what changes* — **had no date to fire on**, and §2 did not change: `01. Sprint Plan.md` is untouched by all twenty commits.
- **Step 0's own ordering argument was inverted in execution.** §5 put [#278](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/278) before item A because *"item A puts a delete button within reach of the same documents"*. Item A merged at 00:26 on 2026-09-01; #278's fix at 13:12, after item B as well. The other half held — [#292](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/292) at 11:36 against item 1 at 15:42 — which is the half where the screen would otherwise have been built twice, so the cost this time was luck rather than sequencing.
- **The two screens the sprint traded three `Should` rows for are not demonstrated in a browser.** `e2e/tests/` asserts that the attachments and comments panels show their **empty states** and that the activity timeline renders; **nothing uploads a file, downloads one, or posts a comment.** PR #297's own body says so. The evidence for MVP criteria 6 and 11 is 545 jsdom component tests.
- **The postcondition that closed Sprint 12's second action fails a container nothing watches.** `minio-init` now ends `mc ls kelir/kelir` and exits 1 without the bucket — verified both ways. But `backend` declares no `depends_on: minio-init: {condition: service_completed_successfully}`, and `deploy.sh`'s smoke test covers `/health/ready` and `/version`. **A stack whose bucket step fails still deploys green.** With the point above, that means a Sprint 12-shaped deployment defect would still reach a tag today.
- ~~**The mutation campaign's evidence thinned, and what remains cannot be read from the repository.**~~ **Struck 2026-09-03 by [record 13](../verifications/13.%20Sprint%2013%20Independent%20Pass.md) §6.** This was measured on commit bodies and it was wrong: the campaign is recorded in the **test files**, dated and naming the reddened test for each mutation — **thirty tabulated, plus two the compiler refuses**, against Sprint 12's twenty. **What survives is one gap:** `document_comments.rs` carries no mutation record, so items B and 4 have none in the tree.
- **The capacity estimate was wrong by three items, in a sprint that had already re-baselined its rate twice.** Seven planned against a measured six-to-seven; ten built, plus eight findings, plus three process actions, plus an unplanned twenty-eight-record ADR retrofit — in three days.
- **PR [#308](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/308) is an empty commit** duplicating #305's three commits verbatim. Nothing broke. It is a merge that reports work it did not do, which is the exact shape of the defect #305 was fixing one file over.

## Root Causes

- **A deadline enforced by the document that owes it is not a deadline.** Sprint 12's first action set *Sprint 13's planning* as the moment the independent pass gets a party or §2 changes — and the Sprint 13 plan was both the thing under the deadline and the only thing that could observe it. It observed itself, recorded that it could not name a party, wrote that it had fixed a date, did not, and no one was outside the loop to notice. **Every control in this project that has worked lives outside the thing it governs** — the title linter, the configuration-reference test, `every_annotated_route_reaches_the_document`, the type signature that will not compile. **Every one that has failed for five sprints is a sentence in a plan asking the plan's author to comply.** That is not a discipline problem, and it will not be fixed by a sixth sentence.
- **The exit ran out of sprint because construction never stopped.** Step 5 was placed before the tail precisely so that a finding would still have somewhere to be fixed. Then the tail was built, and three cut items were built on top of it, and every one of those was defensible on its own — the capacity was real, the items were owed, the findings came with them. **A step whose only predecessor is "everything above" starts last, and a sprint that keeps finding capacity never reaches "everything above".** The exit needed to be positioned as work with its own slot, not as the thing that happens once the work is done.
- **§1.2's argument stopped one level short of itself.** It established that *a criterion that says **users can** is not met by an endpoint*, and then accepted a component test as the demonstration that a user can. The same sentence answers both: jsdom is not a user either. What made the endpoint version visible was that `features/` had no directory — a fact a `ls` could produce. There is no equivalent cheap signal for "this screen has never been driven", so nobody looked.
- **The action said *assert the postcondition* and the postcondition is asserted; nothing in the sentence required anyone to be listening.** The Sprint 12 root cause was *a `command:` that exits 0 is not a command that worked*. The Sprint 13 version is one turn further out: **a container that exits 1 into a `compose up -d` nobody reads is not a check either.** An action naming the assertion and not the observer buys the smaller half.
- **Evidence written into a pull request leaves the repository.** §2.9 offers two places and only one of them is in the tree. Under time pressure the commit body carried the reasoning — which is why this sprint's commits are long and good — and the mutation counts went wherever they went. The standard permitted it, so nothing failed; the cost lands on the next reader, which is who the campaign exists for.
- **The trade was priced at planning and settled at execution, and only the pricing was wrong.** §3.1 cut three rows on an estimate, in the right order, with the cost stated. Nothing about the cut was careless and the reversal cost a paragraph. **The mistake was making the decision at the only moment when the least was known.** A cut line that is walked when capacity is observed does not need the estimate to be right.

## The sprint, in one paragraph

Sprint 13 built more than any sprint in this project's history and did not do the one thing it existed for. Ten construction items against seven planned, three of them cut at planning and all three built anyway; seven of eight carried findings closed after a sprint that closed none; the decisions got a home and thirty-four of them moved in; two MVP criteria got the screens the plan proved they needed. Then the sprint ended with `v0.5.0` still the newest tag, no verification record, no SRS §9 verification, and two screens that no browser has ever driven — so the MVP's own acceptance criteria are still evidenced by an argument rather than by a run. **The independent pass is now five sprints unmet, and this is the sprint that proves it is not a discipline problem**: seven actions were carried in, and the four that pointed at a file all landed while the sprint was busiest, and the three that pointed at somebody's future judgement all failed. The plan that was meant to break the run wrote that it had fixed a date and did not, and nothing outside it was looking. Sprint 14 opens holding the MVP exit, and the first action above is that this stops being a sentence.
