# Sprint 6 Retrospective — 2026-08-24

**Status:** Final · **Last updated:** 2026-08-24

**Sprint outcome:** goal met · **Velocity note:** six items planned, six built, and fourteen more issues closed inside the sprint — every one of them filed by a verification pass rather than by a plan. Three independent passes filed twenty issues against this sprint's own surface, four of them release-blocking. The construction estimate was right; the sprint was three times its size, and the difference was verification and its consequences.

## What Worked

- **Independent verification is now structural rather than discretionary, and it paid three times.** [Sprint plan](../planning/01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md) §2 makes an author-verified item `In progress`, so the passes happened because the rule fired, not because anybody was worried. [Record 01](../verifications/01.%20Party%20Surface%20Verification.md) found seven defects in a surface Sprint 5 had reported clean; [record 02](../verifications/02.%20Role%20View%20and%20Fix%20Verification.md) found six, one a regression a fix had introduced; [record 03](../verifications/03.%20Sprint%206%20Surface%20Verification.md) found seven. Both required CI checks were green throughout all three.
- **Running the pass before the sprint's own construction.** #96 came first, so #98, #99 and #100 were built on a party surface whose four worst defects were already closed. The alternative — the same fixes after three new surfaces existed — is the doubling the plan's sequencing note predicted and this sprint avoided by scheduling.
- **Findings became rules instead of patches.** #118 produced [coding standard](../../docs/standards/01.%20Coding%20Standard.md) §2.5's one-connection-per-request rule; #133 and #137 produced the same section's lock rule, which says to choose the lock by what the check *read* rather than by what the write touches, and gives the two worked cases; #138 produced a test that discovers the routes it checks instead of listing them. Each is a rule a future module inherits without reading this sprint's history.
- **The arithmetic was written down in public before it was needed.** The 2026-08-21 re-plan stated plainly that ten items against a rate of six to seven means three must move, named which three and in what order, and said the third re-scoped a release and therefore belonged to the product owner. The decision on 2026-08-23 was to build in full — taken as a decision, with the cost visible, rather than discovered halfway through.
- **A retrospective action was honoured at the moment it cost something.** #112 split three files ahead of #98 rather than inside it. The cheaper move was to fold the split into the feature branch, as Sprint 5 twice did; it was not taken.
- **The verification method held over new ground.** Record 03 covered a frontend for the first time and the record's shape did not strain. It also recorded the mutation shapes that produce no evidence under `sqlx::query!`, so the next pass does not rediscover them.

## What Didn't

- **A defect class came back inside the sprint that had just taught it.** #105 was check-then-act. #99 was designed against it, got it right, and wrote the reasoning into its own module. #98 — same sprint, same agent, same table family, the module next door — reintroduced the identical shape twice, in `refuse_cycle` (#133) and in `delete_facility` (#137), and #134 showed the same guard could be defeated with no concurrency at all.
- **A Definition-of-Done clause went unmet for a whole sprint without anyone noticing (#138).** Nine handlers served traffic that reached no client generated from the spec, while `the_openapi_document_lists_every_party_route` passed by naming party routes one at a time. Three items were reported built against a clause nothing asserted.
- **A coverage finding for the third sprint running** — #106, then #121, then #139 — each found by mutation and by nothing else, each in code the previous sprint had just added or just fixed. Seventeen of 48 mutations came back green this time, and the sharpest was the whole of FR-MDM-007's concurrency design on the facility arm: thirteen passing tests over a file with half its statements untouched.
- **The sprint closes on fixes nobody outside their authors has read.** PRs #141–#144 close the record-03 findings and carry no verification record. This is exactly the position record 02 was created to check, and it found #118 there — a fix that closed a real race and opened a worse one.
- **The release rehearsal that verification rule 4 puts at the sprint close has not been run.** The rule exists because the project's one rehearsal found a release-blocking defect (#76), and it was written to move that discovery earlier. It did not move it this time; the discovery is still ahead of the tag.
- **The frontend is verified by reading only.** Record 03 drove no browser, wrote no E2E and aimed no probe at the UI, and the repository carries no harness that would let it. #101 is the second frontend feature in the project and the first ever covered by an independent pass, and the coverage stops at the source.
- **Two actions are carried again**: migration numbers are restated in the plan for a third sprint, and merges are still manual for a fifth.

## Root Causes

- **A lesson lives in the module where it was taught until it is written as a rule.** #99's check-then-act reasoning was correct, well argued and in a doc comment. Nothing carried it thirty files away, and nothing had to: the coding standard said nothing about it until #141. The same is true of the claim in `repository/facility.rs` that no cycle could be written through the module — a confident sentence with no control behind it, which is what record 03 took as its first hypothesis and broke twice.
- **A test that enumerates its subjects fails the way the list it checks fails.** Both have to be remembered, and the OpenAPI test was remembered for parties and forgotten for everything after. The other §2 clauses are in the same position: "audit and permission checks in place" is carried by tests written per surface, and "demoable from release images" is carried by nobody at all — which is why the rehearsal is a step someone must decide to take.
- **Mutation is this project's only detector of unexercised predicates, and it runs once per sprint, inside an independent pass, at the end.** So coverage gaps arrive in batches, after the code they concern is merged, and the batch is largest in the code most recently added — including the code that fixes the previous batch. Three sprints have now produced one of these findings each.
- **Verification produces work at roughly twice to three times the item count, and the plan still sizes a sprint by items built.** Sprint 5 proved the planning half of the rule and had no verification to absorb; Sprint 6 absorbed it by growing rather than by cutting, which worked because nothing external depended on the date.
- **The closing gate has no gate of its own.** A pass over the fixes needs a further pass over its fixes, and the recursion has to be stopped somewhere. Stopping it is a decision about how much risk a release carries; this sprint stopped it by not asking.
