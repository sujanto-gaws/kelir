## What Worked

**A module was opened from a one-line scaffold and a dashboard exists, in one sprint.** `modules::reporting/mod.rs` was `// reporting module scaffold.` and `DashboardPage.vue` called `/version`. FR-RPT-001, 002 and 003 now stand on **one endpoint contract**, and **[ADR-0039](../../docs/architectures/adr/0039.%20A%20Dashboard%20Widget%20Is%20a%20Purpose-Built%20Endpoint.md) held for all three widgets without amendment** — **D-78** taken before the first widget was written rather than after a list definition had been stretched to fit one, which is the whole of what taking it early was for.

**Both gate rows landed in the sprint before the sprint that tags, and the position argument is now three for three.** [#412](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/412) shut four ways the release gate could be satisfied without a pass — **in Sprint 17, not in Sprint 18** — on Sprint 16 item 1's rule that *a control merged after the release it governs was never in force*. [#408](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/408) went first because a required check that can fail on a third party's outage blocks the merge that would fix it, which had already happened once for forty minutes.

**The probe practice was turned around and used properly.** Item 2 wrote **five synthetic release records, one per new rule**, each run against the gate alone and deleted, **with the positive control last** — [record 15](../verifications/15.%20Sprint%2016%20Independent%20Pass.md) §7's own method, aimed at the gate that record 15 had defeated with three one-character edits. **A gate nobody attacks is a gate nobody has tested**, and this is the first time the project attacked one on purpose before shipping it.

**A surviving mutation was recorded as a finding and closed rather than replaced.** Item 5's **M3 came back green** on the first pass; closing it took `reporting_dashboard.rs`'s FR-RPT-003 section from 27 tests to 28. [Coding standard](../../docs/standards/01.%20Coding%20Standard.md) §2.9 says a green mutation is a finding and not a nuisance, and this sprint is the third consecutive one where that sentence produced a test rather than a shrug.

## What Didn't

**Every construction row overran its estimate, in the same direction, and the row the plan called cheapest overran most.** Measured in [construction plan 14](../planning/14.%20Sprint%2018%20Reporting%20Tail%20Construction%20Plan.md) §1.1 and carried in [status report 18](../status/18.%20Sprint%2017%20Status.md): **six rows, six overruns, median ≈ 2.3×**. [Construction plan 13](../planning/13.%20Sprint%2017%20Reporting%20Construction%20Plan.md) §3 called FR-RPT-003 *the cheapest `Must` row in the phase* — **it came in at 2,162 lines, the largest row in the sprint** — and named FR-RPT-001 as *the one most likely to overrun*, which it was not. **One sprint earlier [retrospective 14](14.%20Sprint%2016%20Retrospective.md) had called the estimate rule fixed**, on Sprint 16's evidence, and it was fixed for the kind of row Sprint 16 built.

**No browser flow reaches the dashboard, and no row said so.** The sprint shipped **three user-facing screens** and `e2e/tests/` still holds **thirteen specs, unchanged since Sprint 15** — the last two were added on 2026-09-08 for the form and list builders, by the sprint that built them. [Sprint plan](../planning/01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md) §5's seventh verification rule is explicit: *a frontend item's status row says `verified by inspection only` unless a flow in `e2e/tests/` reaches the surface it delivered*. **Sprint 15 honoured it by building the flows. Sprint 17 built neither the flows nor the sentence**, and the phrase appears in [status report 18](../status/18.%20Sprint%2017%20Status.md) only because this close went looking. The rule last appeared in a status report for **Sprint 10**.

**The independent pass did not run — and the sprint that would have been forced to have one no longer is.** No record 16 exists, so Sprint 17 closes `author-verified`, which [construction plan 13](../planning/13.%20Sprint%2017%20Reporting%20Construction%20Plan.md) §7 step 7 predicted in writing. **What is new is the second half**: §2's binding form fires at the release, and **D-81 moved the release out of Sprint 18**, so the exposure this sprint accepted is now Sprint 18's too. A third sprint bought separation for the release controls and handed a sprint back its old exposure.

**The close ran last again, for the third time in three sprints.** Sprint 16's close landed after all six of Sprint 17's rows had merged; **Sprint 17's close — this document — lands after Sprint 18's construction plan was written and after D-81 re-baselined the phase.** [Construction plan 14](../planning/14.%20Sprint%2018%20Reporting%20Tail%20Construction%20Plan.md) §6 is the first to attach a date to the promise rather than an intention, and it did so because §1.3 could see this coming.

**The suite-count discrepancy was never a mystery; it was a number nobody checked.** Two status reports carried it as *unexplained and not this sprint's row*. **The check took seconds and needed no services** — see below, and [status report 18](../status/18.%20Sprint%2017%20Status.md) for the arithmetic.

## Root Causes

**A comparable measures the feature and the row is paid for as feature plus suite.** The three widget rows are **50%, 54% and 54% test code**, and `reporting_dashboard.rs` grew by 530–899 lines *per widget* — **the shared test file grows with each row rather than being inherited by it**, which is the exact opposite of what [construction plan 13](../planning/13.%20Sprint%2017%20Reporting%20Construction%20Plan.md) §1.1 predicted when it reasoned that items 4 and 5 *inherit item 3's endpoint, its permission and its card*. They did inherit those. **Coverage is not inherited, and coverage is where the lines are.** [Retrospective 14](14.%20Sprint%2016%20Retrospective.md) declared the rule fixed on a sprint of documents and tests, where the ratio does not bite; **one sprint of feature rows was enough to find the boundary of the fix**, which is an argument for measuring a rule on the kind of work it will next govern rather than on the work that motivated it.

**`Unexplained` became a status a report could carry, because nothing ever required an attempt.** Two reports said the gap was unexplained and not that sprint's row, and both were honest and neither was wrong. **What was missing is that no document named a party, a date or a method** — so the sentence was reusable, and a reusable sentence about a defect is a defect that ages. [Retrospective 14](14.%20Sprint%2016%20Retrospective.md)'s fourth action worked precisely because it named the *report* the answer had to appear in and said the result counts **either way**, including *the hypothesis was wrong* — which is what happened. **A finding needs a destination more than it needs an owner.**

**Every control this project has built binds a record; the two things still missing bind an act.** The pass and the browser flow are the two, and both failed this sprint in the same shape: `sprint_reports_are_verified.rs` makes an unverified report **say so**, and the `verified by inspection only` rule makes an uncovered screen **say so** — and **neither sentence got written this sprint until the close wrote it.** The report control fires because a test reads the report; the browser-flow rule has no reader at all. **It is the same finding [retrospective 14](14.%20Sprint%2016%20Retrospective.md) reached about the previous-actions review, one document over**, and it is the last place in the close where a rule depends on somebody remembering.

## The sprint, in one paragraph

Sprint 17 opened a module from a one-line scaffold and closed every issue it carried, six of six, walking its cut line by building the row below it for the third sprint running. Its two gate rows landed in the sprint *before* the one that tags, which is the third success for a position argument this project had to learn twice; and item 2 attacked its own gate with five synthetic records before shipping it, which nothing here had done on purpose before. **Its bad news is arithmetic**: every row overran, by a median of 2.3×, and the row the plan called the cheapest in the phase arrived as the largest in the sprint — because tests are half of a widget and the shared test file is not inherited. **Its quietest failure is that three screens shipped with no browser flow touching them and no row said so**, a rule live since Sprint 7 that last reached a report for Sprint 10. **And the finding two reports had called unexplained turned out to need a static count and no services at all** — the suite never shrank; one report overcounted by 26, and nobody had looked because no document had ever named a place for the answer to go.
