# Sprint 8 Retrospective — 2026-08-27

**Status:** Final · **Last updated:** 2026-08-27

**Sprint outcome:** goal met, exit met in two halves nothing joins · **Velocity note:** six construction items planned and six built, plus one counted separately that arrived built; verification allowance of ten with four spent and **four closed**, against Sprint 7's seven filed and zero closed

## What Worked

**The construction plan written before the first commit, and overruled by the third.** [Sprint 8's plan](../planning/03.%20Sprint%208%20Form%20Renderer%20Construction%20Plan.md) is the first document in this project to decompose one requirement into files and order *ahead* of the work, and the thing that proves it earned its place is not that it was followed — it is **D-27**, which reversed its §6.3 on the order the server re-evaluates in. A plan that cannot be wrong in a specific place is not carrying any risk. It was wrong in one specific place, the reversal was argued from JFSS §9.2 and from a named test, and the plan now says so where it was wrong.

**Five findings came out of construction and all five closed in the branch that found them.** Four could not have been caught by the layer that found them being more careful — a `NumberField` that threw on the first keystroke into any number field, a build assertion that came back green against the defect it exists to catch, a browser-only label collision, and a submitted-versus-stored banner that cried wolf on every repeater row because `serde_json` sorts map keys and JavaScript does not. The last one is the argument for the harness in one sentence: **no stub could have produced it**, because every stub echoed the payload back exactly as it arrived.

**Rule 7 stopped applying, on schedule.** Every frontend row in the [status report](../status/09.%20Sprint%208%20Status.md) cites a Playwright flow. Sprint 6 shipped a list UI verified by reading; Sprint 7 built the harness; Sprint 8 is the first sprint whose frontend work was covered by it as it landed. **D-14** cost one sprint of construction and bought a defect class.

**The allowance was spent.** Four inherited findings, four closed, including the two that were not patches: #202 was closed by *measuring* the oracle before and after and writing both numbers into the module header, and #206 was closed by a rule in two standards plus a named step in the sprint plan rather than by sixteen tests. The Sprint 7 retrospective asked for artefacts instead of intentions and got them.

**Decisions went to the backlog rather than into the plan.** Eleven this sprint — **D-21** through **D-31** — each with what was rejected and what it costs, and none of them settled inside the construction plan that raised it. The one that matters most is the one that admits a limit: **D-31** records that the forgot-password timing oracle is *narrowed and not closed*, with the residual measured, rather than declaring the finding fixed.

## What Didn't

**The mid-sprint independent pass did not run.** It was the first action of the [Sprint 7 retrospective](../retrospectives/05.%20Sprint%207%20Retrospective.md), adopted as a Sprint 8 entry criterion, Six of that retrospective's eight actions are done and two are not: this one, and the pull-request permission that has never been the team's to grant. **It is the only action this sprint failed that it could have kept.** The construction plan §7 placed it *"between #163 and #164"* — a position in a dependency chain, not a date. #163 merged at 15:04 on the 26th and #164 at 05:38 on the 27th, **fourteen and a half hours apart and overnight**: there was no midpoint between them for a pass to run in.

**The findings were closed by their fixer, and the fixer verified them.** Four rows in the status report read `Closed, author-verified`. That is the honest label under sprint plan §2, and it means the pass that closes them is still owed. This sprint closed Sprint 7's findings and produced findings of its own with the same property.

**The exit sentence outran what was built, for the second sprint running.** Sprint 7's goal said a document *"submits and takes its number"* when no document existed; Sprint 8's exit says *"a user opens that type, fills the form … and submits it"* when the renderer opens a form by form id and no screen traverses the type-to-form binding. Both halves work. Neither sprint's exit was written against an artefact that would exist at the end of it.

**The migration-restatement action reached its fifth sprint before being closed** — and it was closed by deleting the restatement rather than by maintaining it, which is what it should have been on the first sprint it was raised. The paragraph in sprint plan §6 was stale again by this sprint's end: it said the workflow migration would take `0020`, and two more migrations had landed since.

**The coverage ratio fell again, on the first run of the rule written to watch it.** [Record 05](../verifications/05.%20Sprint%208%20Surface%20Verification.md) ran the campaign sprint plan §2 now names: 27 mutations over the predicates this sprint added, **6 red — 22%**, against 37% in Sprint 7 and 65% in Sprint 6. Counting only the tests Sprint 8 itself shipped it is 11%, because three of the six reds are held by tests written to close [#206](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/206). The rule worked exactly as intended — it produced a number nobody could argue with — and the number is worse than the one that prompted it.

**Seventh occurrence: the working token cannot merge a pull request.** Carried from Sprints 0–1, 2–4, 5, 6 and 7.

## Root Causes

**An action whose done-when names a position in a sequence slips with the sequence.** "Between #163 and #164" is not a schedule; it is a dependency, and dependencies compress under time pressure until the gap they name has no duration. Every action of the previous retrospective that *held* named an artefact that either exists or does not — a rule in a standard, a named step in §2, a test — and the one that failed named a moment.

**An exit written as a story about a user is a story about the parts, not about the seam.** "A user opens that type and submits it" decomposes into item 5 (configure the type), item 2 (render the form) and item 4 (submit) — and every one of those was delivered, so every status row is honestly Done while the sentence is not. Nothing in the sprint owned the *traversal*, because the traversal is not any item's acceptance criterion. An exit that named the Playwright spec which must exist would have been owned by whoever wrote the last flow.

**A predicate goes unexercised because of the *fixture*, not the assertion.** Five of Sprint 8's survivors are the only guard on their behaviour, and every one is a scope filter — by document type, by bucket, by active flag. Each test that touches them builds a database holding exactly one document type, so *scoped by type* and *not scoped at all* produce identical observations and the assertion cannot tell them apart. That is the three-move rule this project adopted a fortnight ago, one level up: two moves cannot distinguish separate from overwritten, and one subject cannot distinguish scoped from unscoped. The rule now sits beside it in [coding standard](../../docs/standards/01.%20Coding%20Standard.md) §2.9, which is where the actions that hold have gone.

**A finding fixed and verified by one session reproduces the condition §2 exists to catch, one level down.** The sprint plan makes an *item* whose author is its only verifier `In progress`. A *finding fix* has the same property and no rule, so four security-adjacent changes — an audit hash format, a mail path, a tenant-scope assertion, four repository predicates — carry only their author's evidence. Each names its mutation and each was seen red, which is what §2.9 requires and is not the same as a second reader.
