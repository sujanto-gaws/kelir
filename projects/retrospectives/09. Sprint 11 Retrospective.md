## What Worked

- **A price set at planning held, and it held for the stated reason.** Conditional routing (#186) was planned as cheap because **D-10**'s evaluator and #174's operator bound already existed; AC1 and AC2 arrived as regression assertions rather than as work. This is the first sprint in which an estimate's *justification* — not just its number — survived contact with the item.
- **A seam named two sprints early took its occupant as a value.** #176 AC4 asked for *"a named seam, documented as the place a delegation window will later apply"*, and **D-13** unscheduled #24 rather than dropping it, on the reasoning that `delegations` had a writer and no reader. #184 landed in that seam as one function, `redirect`, between the two that were already there — neither `normalize` nor `direct` changed. The alternative, which D-13 was written to avoid, was a redesign of assignment in the sprint that also closes a phase.
- **The mutation campaign was aimed rather than sampled, and the report says so.** Sixteen mutations over predicates this sprint added, sixteen red — chosen to attack **named claims in doc comments** instead of spread across the surface, with the status report stating that the number therefore says the claims are honest and not that the surface is covered. A ratio reported with its denominator is worth more than a higher ratio reported without one.
- **A considered decision of this project's own was reversed deliberately, and recorded as a reversal.** An unevaluable routing condition used to read as `false` and take the fallback; it now refuses the transition. The reasoning is in the code — *the fallback is not a safe branch, it is a different branch chosen because the intended one broke* — and in JWSS **R-4** §6.4. Reversals that are recorded as amendments are how a specification stays trustworthy; the same change made silently would have left two readings of §6.4 alive at once.
- **The verification allowance was actually spent, for the first time since Sprint 8.** Six findings were filed and **all six closed** inside the close window — [#259](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/259) (blocking), [#260](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/260), [#263](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/263), [#264](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/264), [#266](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/266) and [#271](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/271). Sprint 7 filed seven and closed none.
- **The exit demo did the job an exit demo is for, on its first run.** [#271](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/271) — with two `APPROVE` edges out of one state the task screen names the first edge's destination for every document — is a defect no unit test was ever going to state, found by driving the path. And the three items with no browser coverage went into the existing flow as **one** scenario rather than three, because they share a branch.

## What Didn't

- **The sprint had no construction plan, and the previous retrospective's actions were addressed to one.** Sprints 8, 9 and 10 each have a plan in [`projects/planning/`](../planning/); Sprint 11 has none. The [Sprint 10 retrospective](08.%20Sprint%2010%20Retrospective.md)'s fifth action — *"a sprint whose exit is 'driven through the harness' carries the deployment as a step with a duration"* — names Sprint 11's construction plan as where it lands. There was nothing to land it in, and the deployment cost was then stated for the first time in the [Phase 5 Close Plan](../planning/06.%20Phase%205%20Close%20Plan.md) §4, written *after* the sprint closed.
- **The independent pass took three sittings, two readers and three days, and only a phase boundary forced it.** The sprint closed on 2026-08-29 with no second reader, for the third sprint running. The first sitting was by an author of half the surface and said so; the second read the frontend; the third — 2026-08-31, by a reader who has written nothing in this repository — read the three items the first could not. The Sprint 10 action put the choice as *get an owner or stop promising one*, and what happened is the third option nobody named: it happened late, in pieces, driven by the close plan rather than by the sprint.
- **The third sitting could not run anything.** No Docker, no Rust toolchain, no PostgreSQL — the same wall [record 05](../releases/05.%20Release%20v0.5.0.md) hit at the tag two days earlier. The first sitting confirmed #259 end to end against a real database; the third traced three findings through statements and executed none of them. Two of the last three sessions to touch this phase could not run the product they were verifying.
- **`v0.5.0` was tagged with a row outstanding**, and the row is the one that makes `/version` mean something: the images are not built from the tag, because `KELIR_BUILD_SHA` is baked in at build time and Docker was unavailable. The rehearsal and the demo both ran against release images built from a **branch**. The release record stays `Draft` for it, which is right, and the gap is still open.
- **Four of six items added no browser coverage during the sprint.** #181, #184, #185 and #186 changed the task and workflow screens and nothing drove them until the exit demo at the phase boundary — where three of them then produced a defect on the first run.
- **The tracker disagreed with the artefacts, twice, in opposite directions.** [#24](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/24) stayed open after the work merged, because it was absorbed into #184 and no `Closes` line named it. [#240](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/240) was **closed** by a pull-request keyword on 2026-08-30 while its own record said in as many words that it stayed open — and it stayed wrong for a day, during which the tracker said the criterion this report waited on had been met.
- **Four of the last twelve headers on `main` exceed the convention's maximum**, at 74, 78, 79 and 79 characters. This is the second sprint in a row that observation has been made.

## Root Causes

- **A retrospective action has to be addressed to a document that will exist.** Sprint 10's fifth action named *Sprint 11's construction plan*, and the plan was never written; the action did not fail on its merits, it failed because its address was vacant. Three of the four actions carried below have the same shape — they name an artefact and assume somebody will produce one.
- **An unowned pass happens when a boundary forces it, and a boundary is later than a sprint.** §2 makes the pass a condition on `Done` and names no owner and no date. The one pass in this project that has ever run on time is Sprint 9's **mid-sprint** pass, which had a fixed date set at planning ([Sprint 8 retrospective](06.%20Sprint%208%20Retrospective.md), first action) — and the [Sprint 9 retrospective](07.%20Sprint%209%20Retrospective.md) observed that a date can be missed visibly where a position cannot. The closing pass has never had one.
- **The Definition of Done names an environment the work cannot assume it has.** §2's environment clause says *demoable from the Docker Compose stack built from release images*, and **D-9** chose it precisely because it was meetable where staging was not. Two consecutive sessions could not reach it. A clause that is unmeetable in the session doing the work becomes a waiver, which is the failure mode D-9 was written to end — and the honest response is not to weaken the clause but to say, in the artefact, what the session could and could not run.
- **A precise doc comment is a testable claim, and this codebase is full of them.** The sprint's own campaign chose its sixteen mutations by attacking claims in comments and got sixteen reds; the third sitting found **two of its three findings** the same way — a `count_for_caller` whose comment forbids the divergence it has, and a `soft_delete` whose comment names the harm its predicate does not prevent. The technique works here because the comments are unusually exact. What it cannot find is the claim nobody thought to attack, which is what a second reader is for.
- **The commit convention's maximum is measured against a header no author types.** The check runs on a branch's commits; the repository squash-merges, and GitHub appends ` (#NNN)` to the title. PR #277's title was 71 characters and landed on `main` at 78. The rule is not being broken by authors — it is being measured against a string the merge produces.

## The sprint, in one paragraph

Sprint 11 built everything it planned and closed nothing on time. The six items are the approval loop finished — a decision with its reason, a return that keeps the number, a history that names both parties, a window that keeps a queue moving — and each of them was merged within the sprint and verified by nobody outside it. What closed the phase was a plan written after the sprint ended, executing four criteria in an order the sprint plan had listed rather than sequenced, and finding six defects while it did. The pass that §2 has required since Sprint 5 finally completed on the third sitting by the second reader, three days late, and its last sitting could not run the product at all. The construction was the easy half, and it was the half that went to plan: the process is a phase boundary behind, and the actions above are all one shape — a rule this project already has, addressed to a document, a date or an owner that does not exist.
