## What Worked

**The ninth attempt at the independent pass is the one that produced a pass, and the variable is what the rule was attached to.** Eight sprints asked for it and got nothing. Sprint 15 made the *absence* unsayable with `sprint_reports_are_verified.rs` and the pass still did not run. [#392](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/392) attached it to the `v0.7.0` tag, and [record 15](../verifications/15.%20Sprint%2016%20Independent%20Pass.md) existed inside the same day. **Three instruments, three outcomes, one variable** — the sentence, the record-check, the gate on a thing the project wants.

**The pass earned its cost on its first run, on a defect no test in this repository could have found.** Finding 2: **the `v0.7.0` upgrade notes stated the opposite of the case.** A bare `\d` pattern compiles and works; the notes told a deployment it was already failing, and offered `[0-9]` as an equal remedy when it is *narrower*. Every required context was green while the release notes were wrong, **because nothing in CI reads release notes against the behaviour they describe**. Eight sprints of a rule addressed to somebody's diligence found nothing of this kind; the first release that could not tag without a reader found it in one pass.

## What Didn't

**The pass was not independent of the work it was created to read, and the gate that made it happen cannot see that.** Record 15's reader carried the same `Claude-Session` trailer as `aa3af07`, `64c2e89`, `1cd3f99` and `191662e` — item 6 and the release run (finding 4). §2's criterion was met for items 1, 3, 4, 5 and [#398](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/398) — every line of product code the sprint contains — and **not** for item 6 or the release. It took a narrow re-read from a separate session ([record 15](../verifications/15.%20Sprint%2016%20Independent%20Pass.md) §7, 2026-09-11) to make the claim true of the whole range, and the re-read then found four more things, one of them blocking. **`releases_are_independently_verified.rs` cannot check any of this**, because §2's criterion is a statement about `git log` and CI clones at depth 1.

**The sprint's own gate shipped defeatable and governed a release in that state.** Three edits, each one character to one line wide, defeat it — including **a `Final` record citing a verification file that does not exist** (finding 3, with findings 7 and 8 folded in). It was found by attacking the test with synthetic records rather than by reading it, which is the practice [#392](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/392) established and which the pass then used against [#392](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/392)'s own output. Closed in Sprint 17 by [#412](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/412).

**A follow-up that is reproduced, written up in detail, and named in an `Aftermath` list can still have no issue.** [Record 07](../releases/07.%20Release%20v0.7.0.md)'s Aftermath carries three follow-ups; two have numbers and the third reads **`not yet filed`**, and the record went `Final` that way on 2026-09-12. It is not a small one: **`deploy.sh`'s own rollback command exits 1 on a healthy rollback to `0.6.0`**, and `v0.6.0` is the only rollback target `v0.7.0` has — so the first operator to follow the script's own instruction after this release gets a red rollback that is not one. The mechanism is understood ([#367](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/367)'s `/version.json` assertion reading Caddy's single-page fallback as an empty version), which is what makes the absence of an issue the whole defect. **Filed 2026-09-13 as [#440](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/440)**, in this change, because a retrospective that names an unfiled follow-up and leaves it unfiled is the same failure one level up.

**[Status report 16](../status/16.%20Sprint%2015%20Status.md)'s suite-count discrepancy is unexplained for a second sprint.** That report recorded a figure 18 below Sprint 14's while the suite had grown by eight tests, and said the gap was unexplained and not that sprint's row. [Status report 17](../status/17.%20Sprint%2016%20Status.md) says the same thing and is careful to say that this sprint's arithmetic working on its own inputs is a different statement. **The hypothesis — an unexecuted target — is written down and has never been run.**

**[Retrospective 13](13.%20Sprint%2015%20Retrospective.md)'s fourth action asked for three issues and got two, and nothing noticed until this retrospective counted them.** **D-15**'s three artefacts all shipped — the `conditional.logic` tier (registry 1.7.0 §2.5), the `regex` refusal at save ([#400](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/400)) and the dialect ADR ([ADR-0038](../../docs/architectures/adr/0038.%20Kelir%20Patterns%20Are%20the%20Linear-Time%20Subset.md)) — but across **two** issues rather than the three the action's *Done when* named. **The substance closed and the letter did not**, and a previous-actions review reading a `Done` column cannot tell that apart from a real miss. One sprint later [#404](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/404) found the case where it mattered.

## Root Causes

**A control holds when it blocks something the project wants, and fails when it asks for diligence — and this sprint is the first time that is measured rather than argued.** Three instruments were aimed at the same act with one variable between them. §2 as a *sentence*: eight sprints, no pass. §2 as a *test on the record* (`sprint_reports_are_verified.rs`): the absence became unsayable, the act still did not happen — [retrospective 13](13.%20Sprint%2015%20Retrospective.md) predicted exactly this and the prediction held. §2 as a *gate on the tag*: the act happened on the first release it governed. **The limit sits in the same data.** The gate makes the pass occur and cannot check the property that makes a pass a pass, because independence lives in commit trailers and the gate runs in a depth-1 clone. **A control can compel an act it cannot inspect**, which is a better position than the last eight sprints and is not the finished one.

**The previous-actions review is the one place that checks whether actions close, and it has no instrument — it reads a verdict somebody wrote.** **It has now been wrong in two consecutive instances, and neither was caught by the instance itself.** [Retrospective 13](13.%20Sprint%2015%20Retrospective.md)'s review of Sprint 14's actions marked seven of seven `Done`, and one of the seven — the mutation action — had a documentation half that had never been written; a builder tripped over it a sprint later while constructing something else ([#404](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/404)), two sprints after the row was written. **This retrospective's review of retrospective 13's own six** finds one short of the form its *Done when* named, by running `gh issue list` and counting. Two reviews, two stale verdicts, and in both cases the thing that found it was somebody looking at an artefact for an unrelated reason. The review is therefore in precisely the position every control in this project has had to be dragged out of: **a practice that reads what a previous author concluded, rather than the artefact the conclusion is about.** It is also the last one, because every other document in the close now has a test over it.

**A rule earned by a release run governed the description of a follow-up and not its existence.** [Retrospective 12](12.%20Sprint%2014%20Retrospective.md)'s fifth action put *reproduced or only observed* into the [release checklist template](../releases/00.%20Release%20Checklist%20Template.md), and it worked — all three of record 07's follow-ups carry the word. **Nothing asks whether the row is a row anywhere else.** The rule made the description honest, and an honest description of an unfiled follow-up is still an unfiled follow-up; `not yet filed` is the template being obeyed.

## The sprint, in one paragraph

Sprint 16 did the thing eight sprints had failed to do, and it did it by giving up on asking. The pass ran because the tag could not be cut without it, found a release note that told a deployment the opposite of the truth while every required check was green, and then turned the same technique on the gate that had summoned it and defeated it with three one-character edits. The phase closed on time — `v0.7.0` at `2bd2e4a`, six of six issues closed, the row below the cut line built, every capacity estimate inside a fifth for the first time in the project's history. **Its most uncomfortable output is that the pass was not independent of the release run it read**, which took a hand-run re-read to repair and which the new gate structurally cannot check; and its quietest is that a reproduced rollback defect sat in a `Final` release record for four days under the words `not yet filed`, obeying the rule that governs it. **The pattern under both, and under the two stale entries in the last retrospective's own scorecard, is the one this project keeps re-learning**: a record that reports on an act is not the act, and the only reviews that have ever caught the difference are the ones that went and looked at the artefact.
