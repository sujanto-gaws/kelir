Sprint 12 closed none of eight; Sprint 13 closed seven of eight after adopting the practice; this sprint closed three of three. **It is no longer a new practice, it is the one that reliably works.**
- **A rule moved out of a checklist and into the compiler for the third time, and the move found something the checklist could not.** [#323](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/323) made `AuditEntry.object_type` an `ObjectType` — exhaustive `match`, no wildcard, so a variant without a permission is `E0004`. Deleting the eleven per-module `&str` constants then exposed **`WORKFLOW_INSTANCE`: an arm, a constant, and no writer anywhere.** D-61's own direction-2 test had stayed green because *a declared `…OBJECT_TYPE` constant was one of the three shapes its source walk counted as a write* — **the test was satisfied by the very declaration that made the arm unreachable.** A list nothing regenerates, found inside the fix for a list nothing regenerates.
- **Rehearsed rather than reasoned about, and past the bar the criterion set.** [#301](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/301) AC3 asked only that the N−1 release reach `/health/ready` against the new schema. The same habit ran through the sprint's tail: [#324](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/324)'s ClamAV limit was **measured** before and after rather than inferred from the compose comment, and [#359](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/359)'s dependency was demonstrated against the real staging file by making the bucket step fail.

## What Didn't

- **The independent-pass amendment failed on the first sprint it governed.** §2 gained an owner (the project manager), a point (mid-sprint refinement) and a criterion (*a session in no construction commit trailer for the range*) at `cdcd704` on 2026-09-04. Refinement passed. **Nothing fired.** `projects/verifications/` still ends at record 14, and the sprint plan's exit says in as many words that a pass which runs on a direction rather than on the rule is recorded as the rule failing.
- **The cut line was not walked at the boundary, and then it was walked because somebody asked.** [Retrospective 11](11.%20Sprint%2013%20Retrospective.md)'s seventh action requires rows below the line to be dropped **at the boundary** with the reason recorded. At the close, rows 8–15 were neither built nor dropped; status report 15 recorded that as an outstanding action. **They were then walked two days later, on a direct request** — six closed, one found already built, one confirmed unclosable. The work was worth doing and the trigger was a person, which is the same trigger the independent pass has always had.
- **A requirement was scheduled for a phase in which it had already shipped.** Row 8, conditional field visibility (FR-RAD-011), sat below the line on a *dependency* argument — *it is rule-driven, so it cannot start before item 1 lands*. Both halves have worked since **2026-08-27**: the renderer shows, hides, enables and disables; the server strips hidden values per JFSS S9.2; a browser flow opens the branch. The claim survived the [sprint plan](../planning/01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md), the [backlog](../planning/02.%20Product%20Backlog.md) (*Sprints 14–16*), a construction plan's dependency argument **and an issue I wrote to build it**, and died to one `grep`.
- **Items 1, 2 and 3 name their mutations and do not record running them.** Seven doc comments read *the mutation that must make this red is …*, which states what **would** redden a test. Item 4 ran a real campaign and reported a ratio (thirteen, twelve red); rows 5, 6, 7 and #346 ran twenty-five between them, all recorded with dates. **The two shapes are indistinguishable to anyone skimming**, and §2.9 asks for the second.
- **A finding recorded in a release record became a premise nobody re-tested.** Row 14 said `deploy-local.sh` exits 0 with no ingress, from [record 06](../releases/06.%20Release%20v0.6.0.md)'s Aftermath. Measured: `docker compose up -d` **exits 1** on a port collision, and `deploy.sh` runs `set -euo pipefail` with that command unguarded, so the described run should have aborted. **The mechanism as written could not be reproduced.** The real hole is narrower and was in the issue's own hypothesis: `KELIR_PUBLIC_URL` is an address, not a container, so a previous deployment still listening on that port satisfies every smoke assertion.
- **My own issue contradicted the schema it was implementing.** [#357](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/357) AC2 required that a hidden field's value is not submitted. That is JFSS **v2.0.0**'s rule, which v2.0.1 §10.1.1 says in as many words is not implementable, and the product correctly does the opposite. An acceptance criterion was written from memory of the shape rather than from the specification on disk.
- **D-66 was an exit criterion and is carried.** *Is the person merging inside the gate?* `main`'s protection is `enforcement_level: non_admins`, so adding contexts constrains everybody except the party who merges. **All thirteen of the sprint's own merges went through a gate that consumes neither the browser suite nor the title check**, as has everything merged since while the cut line was walked. [#335](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/335) cannot close without the answer, and it is not a builder's row to close: this session's token cannot even read the protection settings (`403`).

## Root Causes

- **The independent pass and the cut-line walk are one failure, and the amendment proves the shape rather than fixing it.** [Retrospective 11](11.%20Sprint%2013%20Retrospective.md)'s root cause was *every control that works lives outside the thing it governs; every one that has failed for five sprints is a sentence asking a plan's author to comply*. §2 was then amended — with an owner, a point and a criterion — and **it is still a sentence asking a person to act at a time.** It failed identically, in the same sprint as the cut-line walk, which is also a sentence asking a person to act at a time, and which also happened only when somebody asked. **The amendment was the sixth restatement wearing a control's clothes.** The distinguishing feature of this project's working controls is not that they name an owner; it is that **something fails when they do not run.**
- **A plan row is an assertion about the code, and nothing in this project treats it as one.** Row 8 travelled through three documents claiming a `Should` requirement was unbuilt. Every other assertion of that kind here is checked by something — `configuration_reference.rs` checks the environment table, `adr_records_are_current.rs` checks the index, `audit_object_types.rs` checked the map. **The planning documents are the last large body of claims about the code with no check at all**, and the cheap signal existed the whole time: one `grep` closed it.
- **A record of a finding inherits the authority of the record it is in.** Record 06's Aftermath is a careful document and its `exits 0` claim was written by somebody who had just watched the run. It then propagated into the sprint plan and into an issue as fact. **D-60's fix was reproduced before it was believed and that is why it was right**; row 14's premise was not, and it was wrong. The rule this project already applies to defects — reproduce before fixing — does not currently apply to findings once they are written down.
- **Prose evidence and prose intention are the same shape.** *The mutation that must make this red is X* and *Seen red, 2026-09-06* sit in the same position, in the same comment style, and only one is evidence. The campaign has moved into the test files, which [record 13](../verifications/13.%20Sprint%2013%20Independent%20Pass.md) §6 established is the right place — but nothing distinguishes the two claims mechanically, so the weaker one reads as the stronger to every future reader.
- **An acceptance criterion written from the shape of a problem rather than from the specification is a plausible wrong answer.** #357's AC2 was the *obvious* rule — hidden fields should not be sent — and JFSS spends a numbered subsection explaining why the obvious rule is unimplementable. Writing criteria without opening the authority produces criteria that survive review, because they sound right.

## The sprint, in one paragraph

Sprint 14 built exactly what it planned, for the first time in three sprints, and then had its two process rules fail in the same way on the same days. The construction is not in doubt: seven items, two migrations, two ADRs adopted at their merges, six decisions, three carried findings closed, and — once the cut line was walked — six of the eight rows below it as well, including a `Should` requirement discovered to have shipped in Sprint 8 and a release-record finding discovered to be unreproducible. What did not happen is the independent pass, on the first sprint under a rule amended specifically to make it happen, and the boundary walk of the cut line, under a rule written one retrospective earlier for the same purpose. **Both were performed eventually, and both were performed because a person asked** — which is the state this project has been in for six sprints and which the amendment was supposed to end. The next action is therefore not another owner and another date; it is a test that goes red when the record is missing, because that is the only kind of control this project has ever kept.
