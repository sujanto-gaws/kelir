## Root Causes

**Coverage is sized on comparables that predate the gate it now carries.** Retrospective 18's sixth action put the campaign's tests inside each row. In Sprint 20 they were a separate pull request ([#542](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/542), 634 lines). **Nobody re-priced coverage for that move.** Rows 11–13 were sized on [#538](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/538), [#507](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/507) and [#513](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/513), and none of those ran a campaign at its gate. A row now carries a fixed cost that does not scale with its feature: the campaign's gap tests, the gate's probes, and a trace. **So the smaller the row, the larger the ratio.** Row 13's 44 feature lines carried 402 test lines.

**A conditional count is a count nobody took.** Row 18's *if* is the same shape as Sprint 20's row 7, one level down. Retrospective 18's fix was to re-size a row when its analyst criteria land. **Row 18 had no analyst criteria** (plan 17 §7 step 2 named seven rows), so the rule never saw it. A rule tied to one artefact misses rows that do not produce that artefact.

**Independence is structural, and only a reading supplies it.** The gates are subagents by construction, and §2 (b) says so. **The only independent reader is one the product owner starts.** Record 19 shows that works when it is placed before the work that builds on it. Sprint 21 had no such placement for itself, because its own reading can only come after it. **So the reading of Sprint 21 falls into the sprint that tags**, as D-85's did for Sprint 19.

**A value PostgreSQL refuses reaches it, because every route validates its own inputs.** NUL in `text` and dates outside PostgreSQL's range are storage limits, not field rules. No shared layer knows them, so each campaign finds them again, one route at a time.

## The sprint, in one paragraph

Sprint 21 did everything its table asked, and then everything below its line. The ADR rule was in force before the ADRs, the gate rows landed before the tag, and D-91's three answers shipped with FR-WF-017. The first outbound call and the log that shows it were built, the reading of Sprint 20 landed first, and every dependency merged in order. **The campaign now happens where it can still change the code**, and it did: five defects never reached `main`. **What it did not do is let anyone else read it**, and at 33,818 lines that is the largest unread sprint the project has had. **Its miss was a fixed cost.** Moving the campaign into each row was right, and it made every small row cost at least what row 13 cost, which no comparable knew. Row 18's miss was an *if* nobody checked. **Sprint 22 is the sprint that tags**, and it inherits the reading, the open defects and a gate that now checks the reader.
