## Root Causes

**A control is built in the repository and switched on outside it, and nothing tracks the switch.** D-112 A ended with *the product owner makes it required on `main` after it merges*. That step is a branch-protection setting no test reads and no plan step owns, so a merged check was taken for an enforced one. **This is [retrospective 14](14.%20Sprint%2016%20Retrospective.md)'s root cause again**: a control holds when it blocks, and this one blocks nothing until the setting changes. Row 2's inversion is the same shape one step earlier: the merge order was a sentence in plan 19, read by nobody at the button.

**The page rows were sized without a comparable that builds what they built.** Plan 19 §3.2 said so for rows 4 and 11, and row 10's comparable was a chart with no infrastructure attached. **Each row then absorbed work that was not in its row.** Row 10 closed ADR-0046 §5's stylesheet half of `check:bundle` and wrote the first `no-restricted-imports` rule. Row 11 took the nested S10.3 error paths from part 2, #698, and the icon regression's fix. Both carried an independent campaign that added 1,730 and 983 test lines and fixes. §3.1's rule 4 prices tests as a share of feature, so a feature that runs 4× its estimate takes its tests with it. **The floor is the reverse case.** It was derived from Sprint 22's smallest campaign row, and it prices a row by the campaign it carries, not by the feature the campaign attacks. A 19-line change riding on another row's reader does not need 686 lines to prove it.

**§5 grew from what the main session's own gates filed, not from the tracker.** The state notes added #706, #721 and #722, which row gates filed in sessions the plan's author saw. Record 22's two issues came from a session the plan's author did not see, and #710, #712, #716 and #718 were filed in the sprint without a state note that named them. Retrospective 20's third action said *each later state note reads the tracker again*, and the notes did not.

**Measurements and times were recorded only when a note happened to need them.** Plan 19's dated notes measured the rows that overran, because an overrun needs explaining. Rows that landed quietly have no figure. A merge time was written down only for #693, because it was a breach. So the close had to measure the sprint after the fact, in a pass of its own.

**A pointer test measured the world before acting on it.** Sortable animates the list away from where the card started, and the release aimed at a box read before the drag. A fast local machine released inside the animation, and CI released after it had settled.

## The sprint, in one paragraph

Sprint 23 did what it set out to: an administrator now builds a workflow in the browser, publishes it, retires it, and a document runs through it, and a real browser proves it in CI. Every row was built, the four below the line too, and the independent campaign kept ten defects out of `main` that the builders' checks had passed. **What it did not do is hold the merge or count itself.** The check built to stop a premature merge is not switched on, and two rows went through it anyway. Its figures had to be measured after the fact, in a pass of their own. **It was the largest sprint measured, at 34,877 lines**, and the two rows that overran most, 10 and 11, came in at 2.1–5.9× and 1.9–4.3× their estimates. **Sprint 24 carries the form builder's second half on those measurements**, and a `Must` defect that no plan had placed.
