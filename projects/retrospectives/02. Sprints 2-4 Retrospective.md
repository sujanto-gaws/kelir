# Sprints 2–4 Retrospective — 2026-08-13

**Status:** Final · **Last updated:** 2026-08-13

**Sprint outcome:** Sprint 2 partially met (staging outstanding) · Sprint 3 met · Sprint 4 partially met — the Phase 2 `Must` scope was built, and two of its items failed verification · **Velocity note:** Sprint 1 closed 6 issues, Sprint 2 four of five, Sprint 3 all seven, Sprint 4 five merged pull requests against ten planned issues, re-baselined mid-sprint to five. Construction runs at six to seven items a sprint; Sprint 4 is the first measurement of what verification costs on top, and it consumed the difference.

> **Why one retrospective for three sprints.** The convention is one per sprint, and the [Sprints 0–1 retrospective](01.%20Sprints%200-1%20Retrospective.md) closed by saying "Sprint 2 gets its own." It did not get one, and neither did Sprint 3. That omission is a finding in this record rather than a note in its preamble — see *What Didn't* — and it is why the previous-actions review below is three sprints late.

## What Worked

- **Verification by an agent that did not write the code.** Every merged and pending Sprint 4 item was checked by someone other than its author, and that is the only reason the sprint has an accurate record: the rate-limit key was evadable and aimable at a third party, the lockout was permanent, the first-run bootstrap was not one-shot, and FR-IDM-004 had no implementation anywhere in the stack. CI passed all four.
- **Proving a test by reintroducing the defect it claims to catch.** Used on PR #66's two session defects and on three of PR #61's claims. It is cheap, it takes minutes, and it is the only step that distinguishes a test which exercises the code from a test which merely passes.
- **Gates that blocked work CI had cleared.** Two rounds did real work: PR #63 broke the integration harness on rebase — `ClientAddress` fails closed without `ConnectInfo`, so every `/auth` request outside the binary returned 500 — and PR #61's claim on FR-IDM-004 did not survive reading the backend.
- **Running things rather than reading them.** Sprint 2's finding (no CORS layer, so the two halves could not talk) and Sprint 4's (the limiter never saw a request) are the same lesson from opposite ends, and both stacks were individually green when it was found.
- **The integration harness.** Real PostgreSQL, per-test isolation, asserted cleanup, and it cannot silently skip — the one Sprint 4 deliverable whose foundation nothing has yet contradicted.

## What Didn't

- **Two sprints closed without a retrospective**, so the six Sprints 0–1 actions went unreviewed for three sprints. One of them — name a staging host — is now the item blocking the `v0.2.0` tag.
- **Work was recorded delivered that was not.** FR-IDM-004 was claimed twice in Sprint 3, by #21 and again by #22, with no implementation behind either. Rate limiting was reported complete in the Sprint 3 report and failed verification in Sprint 4. Two Sprint 4 items entered this sprint marked delivered and left it marked otherwise.
- **Passing tests certified nothing.** Seven rate-limiter unit tests were green throughout, because the limiter never saw a request. Sprint 3's authorization sweep has the same shape and passes to this day: it proves a caller holding *no* permission is refused, while nothing binds a route to *its* permission string (#58). FR-IDM-005 and FR-API-008 rest on that check.
- **Four pull requests sat green and unmerged for most of Sprint 4.** The working token cannot merge (`mergePullRequest` is not accessible to it) — the same constraint the Sprints 0–1 retrospective recorded. The rebase cycles that queueing produces are where composition defects hide, and one did.
- **Sprint 4 was planned at ten issues against a measured six or seven**, and twelve further issues (#54–#60, #62, #65, #67–#69) were filed from verification during it. Five of those twelve are decisions, not work.
- **Migration version numbers drifted across documents twice** — Sprint 3's audit migration arriving below its documented phase, and Sprint 4 consuming `0005` and `0006`, which the plan had promised to Phase 4.
- **The staging clause of the Definition of Done was waived three sprints running** before Sprint 4 attached an expiry to it.

## Root Causes

- **"Tests pass" was read as evidence of behaviour, when it is only evidence that some code ran.** A test whose request never reaches the component under test is indistinguishable, at the CI summary, from one that exercises it. The rate limiter and the authorization sweep are one defect wearing two names, and neither was visible without reading what the test actually drives.
- **Status was written from intent rather than from the code.** An issue closed, a pull request merged and an FR mentioned in a title were treated as the same evidence. FR-IDM-004 was recorded delivered by whichever issue named it, and nothing in the process required a reader to open the backend and look for a write path.
- **Ceremonies fire on calendar boundaries this project does not have.** Sprints 0 and 1 merged for the same reason; here the retrospective — and with it the previous-actions review, which is the mechanism that would have surfaced the staging action — was the step that fell out. Nothing was skipped deliberately; nothing triggered it.
- **A waiver without an expiry becomes the standard.** "Demoable from a staging deploy" was unmeetable for three sprints, so it stopped being read as a requirement at all.
- **Facts that live in more than one document have no single writer.** Migration versions are assigned by the Database Schema and restated in the sprint plan and the backlog; restatements drift, and each drift was found downstream of where it was introduced.
