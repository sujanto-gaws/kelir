## What Worked

**The coverage-apart sizing held on the row it was written for.** [Construction plan 15](../planning/15.%20Sprint%2019%20Phase%208%20Close%20Construction%20Plan.md) §3 re-sized FR-RPT-006 to 1,500–2,000 on #457's measured 69% test share, over the sprint plan's 1,200–1,600. **#481 landed at 1,901 lines with tests at 72%.** Its feature half (518) and coverage half (1,366) each landed within 5% of their own ranges.

**Every reading before the tag was done by a top-level session the product owner started. Each reading's §1 printed its session ID before it read anything.** Those are [record 17](../verifications/17.%20Sprint%2019%20and%20Sprint%2017%20Independent%20Pass.md) §1, §7.1 and §8.1. That is [retrospective 16](16.%20Sprint%2018%20Retrospective.md)'s fifth action performed three times. **So the independence criterion held as a fact, not as an absence of matches**: all 22 commits in both ranges name a session, and none of them is the reader. #476's refusal (**D-84**) is why every Sprint 19 commit named a session.

**Phase 8 closed with every sprint read.** Sprint 17 had been read by nobody. **D-85** put it in record 17 beside Sprint 19, and the pass found #487 through Sprint 17's widgets: a deleted role, which record 16's probes had not tried. **The two exceptions, the strike and the release run, were each decided and written into record 08** rather than left for a reader to infer.

**D-88's stopping rule ended a loop that had no end of its own.** The registry claim was corrected three times, and a reading split each correction. **The fourth split triggered a remedy fixed in advance**: strike the claim and keep one sentence (#501). `v0.8.0` tagged the same day. **The rule worked because it changed the kind of claim, not its accuracy.** A claim that promises nothing about a set cannot be split by a member of the set.

**The release order held without the gate holding it.** Record 17 finding 3 (#484) showed that the gate turns red only after a tag exists. So record 08 created the tag locally, ran the gate with the tag present, and pushed only after every row was met and the product owner approved. **The blind spot was known, named and covered by a person for this run.**

**The rollback rehearsal found what CI cannot.** It ran the harness twice on one populated database, so `build-a-list`'s first-page assumption failed (#503). **Deploying forward again as a control put the failure in the harness, not the rollback**, on both releases. That is the release process §6 design doing its job.

**This close lands before the next sprint's plan.** Construction plan 16 does not exist. [Retrospective 15](15.%20Sprint%2017%20Retrospective.md)'s fourth action can be met by its first branch for the first time, after four closes in a row that met it by the second.

## Root Causes

**A positive claim about a set can be refuted by one member, and each correction replaced a refuted claim with a narrower positive one probed only by its author.** This is [retrospective 16](16.%20Sprint%2018%20Retrospective.md)'s root cause four times in a week: *a control's probes are chosen by its author, so they probe the author's model of the input.* Each new reader found an input outside the author's model: a negated class, a long value, a second engine, twelve stars. **The stopping rule worked because it stopped the author from writing a positive claim at all.** Nothing yet makes that the standing rule. D-88 was a one-off decision.

**Work after a tag belongs to no plan, so nothing counts it and nothing places its reading.** Plan 15 §7 has the tag at step 9 and the close at step 11, with no step in between for work. Record 17's non-blocking findings were ready to fix and cheap to start, and §5's *left for Phase 9* is a placement with no table on the receiving side, because Phase 9's plan does not exist yet. [Sprint plan](../planning/01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md) §2 does say that a finding's fix is read by the next independent pass. **The next pass has no date**, and the unread work sits under whatever the next tag's gate asks for.

**A small row is sized with a floor that does not fit it, and a hardening row is sized as a fraction of the thing it hardens.** Item 3's 60–150 carried plan 14's range for five sites that were one-line strikes. Item 2b assumed that closing four probe shapes costs less than the gate. **A hardening pays again for the probes the original never had**, one test per shape plus a control, in the same file. So it costs at least as much as the gate did.

**Every environment the harness runs in starts empty, so a spec that assumes an empty database cannot fail there.** That is the *test isolation* clause in #503's own description. The rehearsal found it only because release process §6 runs the harness twice on one database, and nothing else in the pipeline does.

## The sprint, in one paragraph

Sprint 19 did what it existed for. It tagged `v0.8.0`, closed Phase 8 with every sprint read, and landed its one feature row inside an estimate that was sized on measurement. **Its release week was spent on a single sentence.** The registry's claim about which patterns agree was corrected three times and split four times, by three readings that each found an input its author had not tried. It ended only because a decision taken in advance said the fourth split would strike the claim rather than correct it. **Its quietest fact came after the tag.** Seven pull requests, as large together as the sprint's feature row, fixed findings the plan had sent to Phase 9 and settled a product decision along the way, and nobody has read them. The pattern is the one [retrospective 16](16.%20Sprint%2018%20Retrospective.md) named, seen from the other side. **A control holds when something reads its result, and the work after a tag is the one place in this project's cycle where nothing does.**
