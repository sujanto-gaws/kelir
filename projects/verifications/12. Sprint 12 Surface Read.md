## 1. Method, and what an author's read can and cannot do

Four questions, the ones the [Phase 5 Close Plan](../planning/06.%20Phase%205%20Close%20Plan.md) §3 established:

1. **Absent predicates** over what this sprint added and something now reads.
2. **Doc-comment claims against the code beside them.** This produced two of the three findings, as it did in record 09 §7 — the comments in this codebase are precise enough to be testable, which is what makes a wrong one a defect with a witness.
3. **The record distinctions** — `activity_events` against `audit_events`, already asserted over rows by `the_timeline_and_the_audit_trail_are_written_separately`.
4. **Tenant scoping on the new statements.**

**What this read cannot do is find what its author did not think of**, which is the whole of why §2's rule exists. The three findings below are all in the seam *between* items — the timeline reading what the other epics wrote — which is where an author's attention is weakest and where record 08's [#225](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/225) also lived.

## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — the timeline tells a caller what is in a document they may not read the contents of ([#292](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/292))

**`activity::service::list_activity` requires `activity:read` and the document's own read, and nothing else.** The events it returns carry, in `details_json`:

| Event | Discloses |
|---|---|
| `Attachment.Added` | the file's **original name** and its size |
| `Attachment.Downloaded` | the file's **original name**, and who took a copy |
| `Comment.Added` | that somebody commented, and who |
| `Workflow.Decided` | the action, the states, and `onBehalfOfUserId` — that a decision was delegated |

**So a caller holding `activity:read` and `document:read` but neither `attachment:read` nor `comment:read` learns the names of every file on the document.** A file name is routinely the sensitive part — *2026-redundancy-list.pdf* needs no contents to do damage — and `modules::attachment` states the rule this crosses in its own header: *an attachment is as private as the document it hangs on*. It is as private as the document; its **name** is now more visible than that, because the attachment surface checks a permission the timeline does not.

This is [#263](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/263)'s shape, one sprint later and pointing the other way: that finding was a screen showing *too little* to a caller lacking a permission, and this is a surface showing *too much* to one.

**Not a leak across tenants or documents** — both are in the statement. What is missing is the second permission, and the fix is a decision rather than a line: either the timeline filters entries whose subject the caller cannot read, or `details_json` stops carrying subject detail and the timeline links by id instead. The second is cheaper and probably right; the first is what a reader would expect.

### Finding 2 — a download that failed is recorded as a download that happened ([#293](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/293))

`attachment::service::download` commits the `Attachment.Downloaded` event **and then** reads the object:

```rust
transaction.commit().await?;

let bytes = state.storage.get(&stored.storage_reference).await?;
```

If the read fails — the object gone, storage unreachable — the caller gets a 500 and **the timeline says they downloaded the file**. The comment above it explains why the event comes first, and it argues the case honestly for the failure it anticipated:

> if this product cannot record that somebody took a copy of a file, it should not give them the copy

It does not mention the failure it creates. **Over-recording is the safer direction** for *who has seen this file* — a false positive is a wasted question, a false negative is a copy nobody knows about — so the ordering should probably stand. What should not stand is a record that says `Downloaded a file` when nothing was delivered, and a comment that reads as though the ordering had only one consequence.

### Finding 3 — the scan worker's two statements are not tenant-scoped, and only one of them should be ([#294](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/294))

`repository::pending_scans` selects across every tenant, which is correct: it is a background worker and there is no caller to scope to. It is not documented as deliberate, which is the thing that makes the next reader hesitate.

`repository::record_scan_result` writes by `id` alone. Every id reaching it came from `pending_scans`, so nothing is wrong today — but it is the only write in this sprint's surface with no `tenant_id` in its predicate, and the [#106](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/106)/[#121](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/121) lesson is that a statement's scope should not depend on its caller having chosen correctly. **A defensive `AND tenant_id = $n` costs nothing and removes the question.**

## 3. What was checked and found sound

- **Tenant scope on every read this sprint added** — `list_for_document` and `count_for_document` in all three modules, `find_stored_file`, `find_attachment`, `find_comment`. Each carries `tenant_id` in the statement rather than in the service.
- **The count and the page agree** in all three modules, written out deliberately with the [#279](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/279) drift named above each duplication.
- **The four-record distinction holds by construction.** `activity::record` takes a transaction and `audit::record` takes a pool; the audit-shaped version of the first does not compile. `modules::activity` reads no audit or workflow table.
- **Nothing can produce a false `CLEAN`.** Three independent guards in three places, and the write refuses to move a decided row — which the campaign found untested and which now has a test that reaches the statement rather than the layer in front of it.
- **The address on the audit row is the resolved one.** A caller sending `X-Forwarded-For` does not choose it, asserted on a written row.
- **`attachment_versions`, `comment_mentions`, `comment_attachments`, `parent_comment_id` and `status`** are created and written by nothing, each naming the sprint that fills it.
