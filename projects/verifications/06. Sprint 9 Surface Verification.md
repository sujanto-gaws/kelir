## 1. Why this pass happened, and where its independence stops

[Sprint plan](../planning/01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md) §2 makes an item whose author is also its only verifier `In progress` rather than Done, and the [construction plan](../planning/04.%20Sprint%209%20Document%20Construction%20Plan.md) §10 fixed this pass to **2026-08-28** at planning — a date rather than a position between two items, which is the [Sprint 8 retrospective](../retrospectives/06.%20Sprint%208%20Retrospective.md)'s first action and the only one of that retrospective's six that the previous sprint failed on its own wording.

**The pass ran on the date. It was not independent, and that is the first thing this record has to say.**

Records 01–05 were each written by an agent that had authored none of the surface it read. This one was not: every Sprint 9 construction commit, every test, and all three findings below are the same author's. No second reader was available in the session that produced them.

**So this record does not close what §2 asks a record to close.** Under §2 and the Sprint 8 retrospective's third action, every Sprint 9 item is **`author-verified`**, not Done, and the [status report](../status/10.%20Sprint%209%20Status.md) labels every row that way. What a genuinely independent pass would add is precisely what an author cannot supply: the defects nobody thought to look for. *Twenty mutation runs designed by the agent that wrote the code are strong evidence of what the tests catch and no evidence of what nobody thought to test* — the [Sprint 5 retrospective](../retrospectives/03.%20Sprint%205%20Retrospective.md)'s sentence, and it applies to this record more directly than to any before it.

**What the pass is therefore worth, stated rather than assumed.** Two of the three findings are *refutations of the author's own claims*, which is the one thing a self-pass can honestly produce:

- A **flaky test** the author had written, had run six times green, and had cited in a commit message as holding the compare-and-swap. The campaign found it by running the suite twenty-seven times under load.
- A **second instance of a defect the author had fixed hours earlier**, on the other side of the same function, found by re-reading the path against [coding standard](../../docs/standards/01.%20Coding%20Standard.md) §2.5 rather than by any test — because no test reaches it and the campaign cannot: it is not a SQL predicate.

Neither makes the pass independent. Both are recorded below as findings against the sprint's own work.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — a flaky test contaminated the campaign that measured it

**Severity: not release-blocking. Closed in the pass.**

`a_concurrent_transition_loses_the_race_with_a_conflict` asserted that every loser of a concurrent transition answered **409**. It passed alone six times running, passed with its own file four times running, and went red on **five of the eighteen** mutation runs of the campaign's first pass — where eight test binaries run concurrently against one database.

**Every one of those five reds was this test rather than the predicate being mutated.** `lock_document.tenant_id`, `mark_submitted.tenant_id`, `metadata_of.tenant_id`, `allocate_reference` and `gap_policy.document_type_id` were all reported red by a test that has nothing to do with any of them. The run's ratio was meaningless and was discarded.

**The cause is real product behaviour and the assertion was wrong.** A caller that acquires its connection *after* the winner commits reads the new status and is refused as an **illegal move** (422) before it reaches the write at all; a caller that read the old status and lost the compare-and-swap gets **409**. Which one a given caller gets depends on the scheduler, so the test asserted a shape that holds only under a load it cannot control.

**The rule it argues for**: *a flaky test is worse than no test, because it makes every red run ambiguous* — and a mutation campaign is nothing but a sequence of red runs, so a flake anywhere in the suite is a flake in the measurement.

**Closed** by asserting the property that is true — exactly one caller decides the document, every other is refused, one decision reaches the history — and by giving the compare-and-swap a deterministic test of its own, `a_stale_transition_writes_nothing`, which drives the statement directly and is red under M18.

### F2 — a submit took two pooled connections, on the path no test reaches

**Severity: release-blocking for `v0.4.0`. Closed in the pass.**

`service::submit::submit_document` held its own transaction and passed `state` into `numbering_service::allocate`. On a rule whose policy is `AllowGaps` that dispatches to `allocate_committed(state, …)`, which calls `state.pool.begin()` — **a second pooled connection, taken while the first was still held.**

Coding standard §2.5 makes that a MUST NOT, and says why in the words the earlier half of this same defect earned: *"A path that breaks this does not fail under test, it fails at a concurrency equal to the pool ceiling, and it fails as a self-deadlock."* Measured: twenty-four concurrent submits of a gap-tolerant type, most answering `500` after the acquire timeout.

**It is the second instance of a defect closed earlier in the same sprint**, twelve lines away in the same function. The first — `allocate` reading the gap policy from the pool — was found by a concurrency test going red, and the fix was aimed at the symptom that test produced rather than at the rule.

**Nothing in the suite could have caught it.** `no_two_concurrent_submits_take_the_same_number` uses a `GAPLESS` rule, where the allocation stays inside the caller's transaction; the one `ALLOW_GAPS` test was single-threaded; and the campaign defeats SQL predicates, which this is not.

**Closed** by taking the number **before** the submit's transaction opens when the policy tolerates gaps — which is where §2.5 says to resolve what a request points at. `concurrent_submits_of_a_gap_tolerant_type_do_not_exhaust_the_pool` is the test, red under the allocation moved back inside.

**It changes behaviour, and the change is the policy being restored rather than weakened.** A gap-tolerant rule now loses the number a refused submission took, which is the trade [#158](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/158) named — *a number allocated to a submission that then fails is gone*. The previous behaviour kept it, which was an accident of ordering that the module had written down as a property and had bought with the violation above. Recorded as **D-35**.

**What it argues for**, and this is the finding's real content: **a fix for a rule violation is checked against the rule, not against the test that exposed it.**

### F3 — five predicates were #218's category, one sprint later

**Severity: not release-blocking. Closed in the pass.**

Five of the first run's nineteen greens were not defence in depth. Nothing else enforced what they enforced, and defeating each produced a **wrong answer about another subject** — which is exactly the shape [#218](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/218) reported against Sprint 8 and which this sprint opened by closing.

| Predicate | What defeating it does |
|---|---|
| `replace_metadata` · `document_id` | **The worst of the five.** The statement deletes before it inserts, so one metadata edit wipes *every* document's metadata in the tenant — silently, with no way back short of a restore |
| `lock_pinned_form` · `tenant_id` | A document is created from another tenant's document type, pinning that tenant's form revision |
| `lock_linked_entity` party · `tenant_id` | A document is linked to another tenant's supplier, and the resolution then refuses it in a way that looks like a permission problem rather than the tenancy breach it is |
| `find_status` · `tenant_id` | Another tenant's caller reads a status they may not see, and the compare-and-swap then succeeds because it was handed the right `from` |
| `lock_document` · `tenant_id` | Another tenant's caller submits this tenant's document |

**Closed with five tests**, each naming its mutation and each putting a second subject in the database. Four are red under a single mutation. The fifth is not, and the reason is worth recording: F2's fix added a tenant-scoped read *ahead* of `lock_document`, so the cross-tenant submit became guarded twice within the same day. All three runs were made — each layer alone leaves the test green because the other holds it, and defeating both together turns it red, which is §2.5's *remove the first line* case rather than a gap.

## 5. What the browser flow found, and what was checked and found sound

**The first CI run of `a-document-is-created-and-submitted.spec.ts` failed, and both defects were the spec's.** `toBeDisabled()` does not treat a `<fieldset disabled>` as disabled — only its descendants — so an assertion whose own call log printed `<fieldset disabled …>` reported *enabled*. And the tab test opened the document the flow test creates, so it failed as a consequence rather than on its own subject, which is `e2e/README.md`'s first rule broken two files after it was written. Both fixed; the second run passed all eight flows.

**Checked and found sound, with a boundary round it:**

- **The pin.** A document created through the API always carries its type's `form_id`, and a type whose documents all pinned may be re-pointed. `guard_rebinding`'s population is empty for anything this sprint creates, which is what **D-30** needed and did not have.
- **The Tamper-Proof Pattern on a draft.** A `total` of 999999 posted to a draft is stored as 20. The mutation — storing `request.form_data` directly — was run and is red.
- **The audit boundary.** A form-data change records its changed keys and neither value, asserted over the whole serialized record rather than one member, because the leak does not care which field it travels in. Red under the values restored.
- **The link's permission.** A caller with every document permission and no `master-data:party:read` gets the document, sees it concerns a party, and is refused the resolution. Red under the resolution reading through the repository instead of the service.
- **The list's visibility rule.** Two tenants, each with a document, each seeing one — page *and* count, because a `total` of 2 beside one row is the leak a page alone hides.

**Not reached, and named rather than implied:**

- **The two list statements** (`count_documents`, `list_documents`) were **not** in the campaign. Their tenant predicate is covered by `a_list_holds_only_the_callers_own_tenants_documents`, which was run by hand against the mutation and is red; the other seven predicates in each — the filters — are covered by tests that assert the filter narrows, which is a weaker claim than a mutation. §6.
- **No performance work.** The list has no index for its `search` and the construction plan says why; nothing here measured it.
- **The frontend's own rule evaluation** was not re-verified — it is Sprint 8's surface and record 05 read it.

## 6. The second lines, and the one predicate nothing exercises

Coding standard §2.5: *a predicate written as a second line of defence carries either a test that removes the first line, or a comment saying it is unexercised.*

**Thirteen of the fourteen greens are second lines behind a tenant-scoped read that runs first**, and the layering is deliberate: `update_document` and `soft_delete` run behind `find_document`; `mark_submitted` and `lock_document` behind `find_submission_subject`; `move_status` behind `find_status`; `metadata_of` inside `find_document`'s own scope; `definition_of` behind `lock_pinned_form`; `gap_policy` behind the type read that precedes it. Three of them — M04, M06, M12 — have tests that *do* remove the first line, by arranging an interleaving rather than racing for it.

**M21, `lock_linked_entity`'s facility arm · `tenant_id`, is unexercised and is the one thing this pass leaves open.** The party arm has a cross-tenant test (F3) and the facility arm has none, because no test links a document to a facility at all. It is not a finding — the predicate is identical to the party arm's, in the same function, mutated in the same way — but it is a gap and this is where it is written down rather than left to be discovered by the next campaign.
