**Status:** Final · **Last updated:** 2026-08-23

**Subject:** the role views delivered by #97 (PR #111), and the four fixes the [first verification pass](01.%20Party%20Surface%20Verification.md) produced — #103 (PR #113), #104 (PR #114), #105 (PR #116) and #106 (PR #117) · **Outcome:** six defects, one of them release-blocking for `v0.3.0`

## Why this pass happened

Two reasons, and they are different.

**#97 was authored and verified by one agent.** [Sprint plan](../planning/01.%20Sprint%20Plan.md) §2 makes such an item `In progress` rather than Done, and the Sprint 6 table records "built 2026-08-21, independent verification still owed" against it.

**The four fixes were too.** Each of #113, #114, #116 and #117 was written by the agent that had just read the finding, and each ran its own mutations — five, five, six, and the #117 rework. §2 applies to a fix exactly as it applies to a feature, and the case for applying it here is stronger than usual: three of the four were fixes for *security* findings, and a fix's own new code is not covered by the suite the fix was verified against.

That last sentence is what this pass is mostly about.

## Method

The verifier was independent of the authors of all five changes. It read `repository/role_view.rs`, `domain/role_view.rs` and the role-view half of `service.rs` and `handlers.rs` in full; the source diff of each of the four fixes; and the queries each fix added or moved. It wrote **7 route-level probes** against a real PostgreSQL through the real router, and ran **25 mutations** against the existing suite — each compiled, each executed against all five master-data binaries, each reverted before the next.

Baseline before and after: 203 unit tests and 150 integration tests green across 18 binaries, one ignored (`identity_users`, deferred by **D-7**), working tree byte-identical.

**Four mutations came back STILL GREEN.** Those are findings F4, F5 and F6.

## Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Assigning a role holds a transaction open while taking a second pool connection; ten concurrent profiled assignments deadlock the endpoint | **High** — availability, and a regression introduced by the fix for #105 | [#118](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/118) |
| F2 | Restating a role still hands back its `comments` and `additionalAttributes` to a caller without `master-data:party-role:read` | Medium — #104 one field over | [#119](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/119) |
| F3 | One `PUT` merges a role's `comments` and replaces its `thruDate`, so a restatement silently erases an end date | Medium | [#120](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/120) |
| F4/F5/F6 | Four tenant and soft-delete predicates that no test exercises, including the only cross-tenant guard on the delete path | **High as a coverage defect** | [#121](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/121) |
| F7 | A bad `page` or `pageSize` is refused with a bare 400 and an empty body, outside the error envelope | Low — pre-dates Phase 3, API-wide | [#122](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/122) |
| F8 | `find_live_party_role` lost its doc comment to `find_party_role`, which also joins role types unfiltered | Low | [#123](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/123) |

Every finding was reproduced through the router before being filed. None rests on reading alone.

**F1 is the one that changes what this pass is for.** The fix for #105 moved `state.pool.begin()` above `resolve_profile_references`, which runs on the pool — so the transaction holds one connection while a second is acquired. The production pool is 10 and the acquire timeout 5 seconds, so ten concurrent profiled assignments wait on connections held by each other and all answer 500. It was reproduced deterministically against the harness — four of five connections held, one assignment, **5.06s and a 500** — and the diagnosis confirmed by hoisting the call back above `begin()` and watching the same probe answer 201.

The two concurrency tests #116 added cannot see it: two concurrent requests need four connections against a harness ceiling of five. **A concurrency test written at the concurrency the bug was reported at does not test the endpoint at the concurrency it will meet.**

## What was checked and found sound

Recorded because absence of findings only means something with a boundary around it. Twenty-one of the twenty-five mutations were red.

- **Tenant scoping** — `list_parties`, `count_parties`, `find_party`, `find_party_id_by_code`, `find_role_type_id`, `list_party_roles`, `list_identifications`, `list_contact_mechs`, `lock_party`, and both role-view statements. Each red. #117's rework is real: the reads it was filed about now kill their mutation.
- **The role views' own predicates** — the role type, the role's `deleted_at`, and the agreement between `count_role_view` and `list_role_view` over the search. Each red; the count/page divergence is caught by two tests.
- **Both permissions on the views** — dropping either `master-data:party:read` or `master-data:party-role:read` is red, and the refusal still comes before the filters are parsed.
- **Search escaping** — `%`, `_` and `\` all match themselves; removing the escape is red at the integration level as well as in the unit tests.
- **Page ordering** — reversing `ORDER BY party_code` is red. Paging across a boundary neither loses nor repeats a row.
- **#113's two closes** — removing either the role close or the profile close is red, on two different tests.
- **#116's lock** — removing `FOR UPDATE` is red on three tests, including the mid-assignment delete.
- **#114's gate** — removing `master-data:party-role:read` from `get_party_roles` is red, and the assign route's response no longer carries a `profiles` member.

## What was not reached

- The frontend — #101 has not been built.
- `mdm_facilities`, `mdm_products`, `mdm_services`, `master_data_source_references` — #98 is not built and the rest are unscheduled.
- **Whether F1 has siblings outside master data.** Every transaction in `modules::master_data::service` was checked and `assign_role` is the only one that takes a second connection while holding one; identity and auth were not swept. #118 asks for the rule, which is what would settle it.
- Concurrency on the role views. They are reads and take one connection each; no race was attempted.
- The `mdm_party_relationships` and contact-mechanism paths, except where a mutation crossed them. #107 and #108 remain open over that ground.

## What this changes about how the project verifies

**A fix's own new code arrives unverified.** `find_party_role` and `soft_delete_party_roles` were added by #114 and #113 — fixes for two of the three release-blocking findings — and the tenant predicate on each is untested. Both PRs ran mutations, and both aimed them at the defect being fixed rather than at the code being introduced. The mutation budget went where the author's attention already was, which is the same finding [record 01](01.%20Party%20Surface%20Verification.md) closed with, one level up.

**A fix can be a regression.** #116 closed a real race and opened a worse-behaved failure at higher concurrency. Nothing in the process would have caught it: the PR's mutations were red, CI was green, and the endpoint had been made *more* correct in the property under discussion. The property that broke was one nobody was looking at.

**A test can be masked by a later fix rather than by its own shape.** #106 was about tests aimed at a route that refuses early. F5 is the same result by a different mechanism: `a_soft_deleted_party_leaves_every_view_it_was_in` was a real test when it was written, and #113 — closing the party's roles on delete — made the role predicate absorb the mutation the party predicate used to take. **A green test is evidence about the code as it stood when the test was written.** Re-running it is not re-earning it.
