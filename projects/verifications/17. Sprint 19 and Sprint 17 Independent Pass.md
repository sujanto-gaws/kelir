## 3. Findings

### Finding 1: Registry 1.5.1 and the upgrade note call a pattern subset safe, and it is not. **Blocking for `v0.8.0`, as a claim, not as code. [#482](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/482), `sprint:19`.**

**`^[^,]{1,3}$` rejects two emoji in the browser and accepts them on the server, and it is built only from what the registry recommends.**

#477 corrected 1.5.0's claim of closure, which was record 16 finding 2's charge, and tabulated the divergent constructs. It then added: *"write a `both`-scoped pattern from ASCII literals, written-out classes, `^`, `$` without `m`, and the ordinary quantifiers"*. The `[Unreleased]` upgrade notes send deployments to the registry for *"the subset that is safe"*. P1:

| Pattern | Flags | Value | ECMA-262 | `regex` crate | Saved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `^[^,]{1,3}$` | — | two emoji | false | **true** | **201** |
| `^[^a]$` | — | one emoji | false | **true** | — |
| `abc` | `y` | `xabc` | false | **true** | **201** |
| `abc` | `q` | `abc` | **SyntaxError**, so every value fails | true | **201** |
| `^[a-z]+$` | `i` | U+017F | false | **true** | — |

- **A negated written-out class is inside the subset**, and it counts UTF-16 units in the browser and scalar values on the server. `[^,]` (*anything but a comma*) is about as ordinary as a written-out class gets.
- **The flags are nowhere in the table.** `compile_pattern` reads `i`, `m` and `s` and ignores every other letter. ECMA-262 anchors under `y`, counts differently under `u`, and throws on a letter it does not know.
- **`i` over an ASCII literal** is in the table (*case folding under `i`*) and not excluded from the subset, so the two sentences disagree.

**Why blocking.** These sentences are what `v0.8.0`'s upgrade notes will say to a deployment. [Record 15](15.%20Sprint%2016%20Independent%20Pass.md) finding 2 held `v0.7.0` for a release note that told a deployment something false. Record 16 finding 2 was graded not blocking because it corrected an overstatement no deployment had yet been handed. This one gives a deployment advice to follow. **The discharge is text**: #482's *Done when*. No refusal has to change for the tag.

### 8.3 Findings

Numbered on from §7.3.

#### Finding 11: registry 1.5.3 says the group rule removes the shape that gives out soonest, and a groupless subset pattern gives out at 22 characters. **Blocking for `v0.8.0`, as a claim, not as code. [#499](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/499), `sprint:19`.**

**The registry**, first of *"Three things follow, and none of them is a guarantee"*:

> **The group rule above removes the shape that gives out soonest**: an exponential one, at under thirty characters. Without that shape the backtracking left is polynomial in the value's length, and it still gives out on a long enough value.

The second of the three recommends `validation.maxLength`, and the §6 entry calls it *"the bound on both the split and the wait"*. **The upgrade note** tells a deployment to *"give a patterned field a `validation.maxLength`"* and sends it to the registry *"for what `maxLength` does and does not bound"*.

**S6 is inside the subset, group rule included.** It has no group and no flag, and each `*` sits on its own atom, not stacked. `[a-z]` is not negated, and `1` is printable ASCII. `refuse_pattern` stores it (R4). R2, against `a` × n, then `!1`, which the crate matches:

| Value length | Chromium (V8) | Firefox (SpiderMonkey) | WebKit (JavaScriptCore) | `regex` crate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | match, 1.6 s | match, 2.1 s | match, 0.2 s | match |
| **22** | match, **12.4 s** | **throws after 6.9 s: refused** | match, 1.2 s | match |
| 26 | over 30 s | throws after 7.0 s: refused | match, 7.3 s | match |
| 30 | over 30 s | throws after 6.8 s: refused | over 30 s | match |

**It is polynomial, and it gives out sooner than the exponential shape the rule removed.** That shape splits at 28 characters (R1). S6's degree is the number of stars, and nothing in the subset bounds that number. **The rule does not remove every exponential shape either.** S3, twenty-eight unquantified groups of overlapping alternatives, is inside the subset. Firefox refuses a 58-character value of it that the server matches. **So neither half of the sentence survives a probe**, and a field with `maxLength: 100` bounds neither S6 nor S3.

**What the table and its bullets still say truly.** Every row of 1.5.3's table reproduces (R1). *"Every split and every wait in the table above needed a value longer than 1,000 characters"* is true of that table. The anchoring bullet says a matching value was not measured, and it was not. **The false sentence is the one that tells a reader what the table implies for patterns outside it.**

**Why blocking.** The upgrade note defers to the registry for the bound. The registry tells a deployment that, inside the subset, a browser gives up only on a long value. A probe splits a 22-character one. **This is the fourth claim about the subset that a probe has split**: 1.5.0's *closed*, 1.5.1's *safe*, 1.5.2's *no probe has split*, and 1.5.3's *removes the shape that gives out soonest*. **D-88's stopping rule applies, and this section does not design a fix.** The registry's subset claim is struck. The upgrade note's pattern guidance becomes one sentence: *a pattern the server accepts may still decide differently in the browser; see the registry*. `v0.8.0` tags on that.
