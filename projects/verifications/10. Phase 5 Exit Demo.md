## 2. The three items that had never been driven

`e2e/tests/a-document-is-approved.spec.ts` already carried the exit sentence — a type carrying a workflow, a submit starting an instance, the task reaching somebody else's inbox, a return, a resubmission that keeps its number, an approval that closes it, and the history with its reasons. [#182](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/182) and [#183](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/183) extended it as they went; [#184](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/184), [#185](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/185) and [#186](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/186) added nothing.

All three are now driven, **in one flow**, because they share one branch: the conditioned edge generates the task, that task carries the deadline, and it is assigned to a *person* — the only kind of assignment a delegation window can redirect (JWSS §5.1).

| AC | Item | Observed in a browser |
|---|---|---|
| **AC4** | #186 conditional routing | A request over the threshold is approved and **does not complete**; it lands in `Director approval`, and the history's routing trail reads `DIRECTOR_APPROVAL · condition met` |
| **AC2** | #184 delegation | The task is assigned to the director by the definition; the **delegate** finds it in their own inbox marked `Mine`, badged `On <director>'s behalf`, and the history afterwards carries **both** names |
| **AC3** | #185 due dates | The deadline elapses, the inbox is re-fetched, and the server's flag becomes a `Late` badge — on the row and on the task, which also reads `past due` |

**Nothing was demonstrated by hand**, so [#241](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/241) AC5's escape hatch is not used and this record does not invoke it.

### Three fixture decisions, each of which could have made the flow pass for the wrong reason

- **The branch threshold is above every amount the other flows submit** — they post 45000, 120000, 99000, 64000 and 900; the branch fires above 500000. A threshold between any of those would have rerouted a flow about something else **and still passed**, because the fallback and the branch both end in `COMPLETED`.
- **The delegate holds no approver role.** The target edge is `allowedBy: ROLE:<approver>`, which they do not hold, so if they can decide it at all it is on the director's authority — #184's `permits` checking the actor *and* whoever they stand in for. A delegate who also held the role would have demonstrated the screens and nothing about delegation.
- **`identity:delegation:create` is granted to one role only**, so every flow that is not about delegation is still run by somebody who cannot delegate.

## 3. What the demo found

### [#271](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/271) — the task screen names a destination it cannot know

**The first run failed, and this is why the demo exists.** With two `APPROVE` edges out of `MANAGER_APPROVAL`, a document **under** the threshold — one that will complete — renders:

> Claim it · **Approve → Director approval** · Reject → Rejected * · Send back → Sent back *

The approver is told their approval sends it to the director. It does not.

`TaskDetailPage.vue`'s `offered` collapses by action, rightly: *"two buttons both saying Approve would ask the person to choose something they cannot see"*. What the collapse does not carry through is the label — it keeps the first edge's `toStateName`. **`requiresComment` in the same collapse is combined rather than taken from one edge**, which is the treatment `toStateName` needed and did not get.

**Nothing had caught it because no fixture anywhere had two edges on one action** until this flow added one. `workflow_conditions.rs` covers the *engine* choosing between them; the component spec covers one edge per action. An absent case, not a wrong assertion — the shape record 09's findings share.

**Not blocking the tag.** No control is bypassed, no record is wrong, and the decision taken is the one intended: `APPROVE` is sent and the engine routes it correctly. What is wrong is a sentence on a button — in the surface #186 exists to serve.

**The e2e assertion is narrowed, not corrected**, because there is no correct destination to assert. It keeps AC4's subject — the task says what is being decided and about what — and goes back when #271 settles what a collapsed entry should say.

### A defect in the new flow, found before it ever ran

It asserted the director's **username** on both the task badge and the history entry. The badge renders `delegatedFromDisplayName` and the history renders `onBehalfOfUsername` — different fields holding different strings, so one assertion was wrong and would have failed on first execution. `SeededApprover` now carries both and says why.

## 4. What this does not cover

**#263 was fixed before the demo, and the demo does not exercise it.** Record 09 §6 said the Workflow tab's empty-steps sentence would show if the demo were driven from the requester's side. It was fixed first ([#267](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/267)), and the flows here run the requester as the administrator, who holds every permission — so **no flow covers the requester who cannot read tasks**. That case is covered by component specs and by nothing in a browser.

**One real wait.** `dueAt` is stamped when the task is generated and `isOverdue` is the server's answer against it, so nothing the browser does brings the deadline forward. The deadline is 1.44 seconds because the *indicator* is the subject rather than the duration.

**The rehearsal is [#242](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/242)'s**, and this record does not stand in for it: a demo shows the product works, a rehearsal shows the release of it works.
