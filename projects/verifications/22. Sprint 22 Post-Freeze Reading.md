### Finding 1: `[0.9.0]` says more than the code in three sentences. **Not blocking. [#699](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/699).** Answers subject 3's *claims nothing more*; from the `[0.9.0]` reader's candidates, each re-read here.

1. **The redaction list, without its floor.** *Added*, the integration-log bullet, says the secret is *"redacted as written and in its base64, percent-encoded and `\u`-escaped spellings"*.
   - `redactions` lists no encoded form for a value under `MIN_REDACTED_CHARACTERS`, which is 4 (`integration/domain/secret.rs:270`, `:288-291`).
   - It lists a `BASIC_AUTH` password that short only inside the pair and the header, not on its own.
   - ADR-0043 §R states the floor. #665's corrected screens and OpenAPI text point at *"the spellings ADR-0043 §R lists"*, and they hold.
   - **`[0.9.0]` restates the list and drops the floor.** Record 20 finding 4 and record 21 finding 3 ([#665](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/665)) split the same claim, about what the redaction catches. This is its third text, so by sprint plan §2 (a) it is struck, not narrowed.
2. **Masking by key in a text body.** The same bullet says *"sensitive keys are masked in JSON and in text"*.
   - `mask_text` masks only a double-quoted `"key": "string"` pair (`integration/domain/test_call.rs:392-402`).
   - Its own comment says a number, a boolean, an object or a single-quoted pair under a sensitive key is left alone.
3. **"With nothing written".** Two bullets, on the role delete and on #509, say a refused submit writes nothing.
   - A gap-tolerant rule allocates its number before the transaction, committed (`submit.rs:198-214`). The module's own comment says *"a gap-tolerant rule loses its number to a submission the re-evaluation then refuses"* (`:61`).
   - The document does stay a `DRAFT`, as the #509 bullet also says.

**Why not blocking.** All three are texts. None is in the workflow module or holds a lock.

- Item 3 dates from Sprint 19, and record 18 read its code.
- **The tag's message cannot change.** `CHANGELOG.md` can, and #699 leaves the form of the correction to its fixer.

### Finding 2: ADR-0023 still says creator and submitter coincide on a first submit. **Not blocking. [#700](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/issues/700).** Answers subject 2; from the #676 reader's candidates, re-read here.

## 5. The locks rows 2 and 5 must keep

**This is what each path locks, as found at `e30cb64`. It is not a complete lock order.** ADR-0042 §2 struck that claim after two splits, and this record does not restore it. It states two things. First, the locks each path takes, explicit and through foreign keys, in the order it takes them. Second, the rules that keep the paths that meet from forming a cycle. Record 18 §4 stated the outbox's this way.

### 5.1 What each path locks, in order

| Path | Locks, in the order taken |
|---|---|
| **A submit**, first or resubmit (`submit.rs:215-246`) | 1. The instance `FOR UPDATE`, if the pool read named one (`lock_instance`). 2. The document `FOR UPDATE` (`lock_document`). A document that now names another instance is a 409. 3. The document type `FOR UPDATE` (`lock_pinned_form`). 4. The numbering bucket, for a gapless rule. 5. On a governed change, its record `FOR UPDATE` (`governance::raise`, from `submit.rs:337`). 6. On a first submit, the instance `INSERT`, whose keys take the definition row and its state row `FOR KEY SHARE` (`0025_workflow.sql:209`, `:230`). 7. The roles a new task needs, `FOR KEY SHARE` (`direct`, then `hold_deciding_roles` in `role_code` order). 8. The task and history `INSERT`s, whose keys take the instance and the document, both already held |
| **A decision** (`task.rs` `decide`) | 1. The instance, then the task, both `FOR UPDATE` (`task.rs:922`, `:929`). 2. The task's `UPDATE` and its history row, whose key takes the document `FOR KEY SHARE`. 3. `fire`: the instance's `UPDATE`, its history and outbox rows, then the next task's roles `FOR KEY SHARE` and its `INSERT`. 4. The document's status `UPDATE` (`project_document_status`). 5. On a terminal status of a governed change, the change request, then its record, `FOR UPDATE` (`master_data/service/governance.rs:213`, `:223`) |
| **A claim, a hand-off, a reassign** | 1. The instance, then the task, `FOR UPDATE`. 2. A reassign's target role `FOR KEY SHARE`. 3. The task's `UPDATE`, and the history row, whose keys take the instance and the document |
| **A document's edit or delete** (`document.rs:269`, `:439`) | The document `FOR UPDATE`. An edit then takes its type `FOR UPDATE` and a linked master-data record `FOR SHARE` (`lock_linked_entity`, `document.rs:646`). **Neither locks an instance.** The delete reads `live_instance_of_document` with a plain `SELECT` under the document lock (`document.rs:604`) |
| **The status route** (`status.rs`) | A compare-and-swap `UPDATE` of the document and its history row. The instance is read on the pool, never locked |
| **An attachment, a comment, an external reference** | Their own row `FOR UPDATE`. Their rows' keys take the document `FOR KEY SHARE`. A comment's `INSERT` names no task (`comment/repository.rs:47`) |
| **The outbox delivery** (`outbox/worker.rs:113`) | Plain reads of the instance, the definition and the document. The hook execution `INSERT`, whose key takes the document `FOR KEY SHARE`. Its own leased `outbox_events` row |
| **A type's binding** (`document_type` service) | The document type `FOR UPDATE`, then the definition `FOR SHARE` (`lock_bindable_definition`) |
| **A publish** (row 2, `81b3845`, `service/definition.rs:278-365`) | 1. The definition `FOR UPDATE` (`lock_for_publish`), with nothing held before it. 2. Its roles `FOR KEY SHARE`, by id (`lock_live_roles`). 3. The definition's `UPDATE`, and its own projection rows. **Nothing else.** A revision that is not a draft is refused right after step 1, before it waits on anything |
| **A role's delete** (`identity/service.rs`) | The role `FOR UPDATE`. Then plain reads: the open tasks needing it, and `definitions_naming_role`. Then its `UPDATE`. **It waits on nothing after the role** |
| **A draft's edit, a definition's delete** | One `UPDATE` of the definition row, on the pool |

**Every path that holds both an instance and a document takes the instance first**: the submit, the resubmit, the claim, the hand-off, the reassign and the decision. The edit, the delete, the status route, the outbox and the attachment, comment and reference paths take the document and never the instance. **One order, as #677 claims.**

### 5.2 The rules that keep these paths from meeting in a cycle

1. **An instance before its document.** Kept by every path in §5.1.
2. **A document type after the document, and a definition after the document type.** The submit takes the type, then the definition through the instance's key. The binding takes the type, then the definition.
3. **A path that takes a definition row exclusively holds nothing earlier when it takes it.** That excludes an instance, a task, a document, a document type and a governed record. After it, it takes only roles `FOR KEY SHARE` and that definition's own projection rows. The publish's `FOR UPDATE` and a draft edit's `UPDATE` are such locks. A submit holds the instance, the document and the type while it waits on the definition's key. A binding holds the type while it waits on the definition's `FOR SHARE`. So a definition lock taken after any of those, or followed by one, closes a cycle.
4. **A role is locked exclusively only by `delete_role`, and it waits on nothing after it.** Every other role lock is `FOR KEY SHARE`, and those do not conflict with one another. So their order among themselves (`hold_deciding_roles` by code, `lock_live_roles` by id) closes no cycle.

**Row 2 (`81b3845`) keeps all four.** The publish takes the definition first, with nothing held, and then only roles and its own projection. `delete_role` still waits on nothing after the role, and #693 changed only its comments. The addendum's *"delete_role takes the role FOR UPDATE and never waits on a definition row"* is true: `definitions_naming_role` is a plain read.

**What row 5 must keep.** The route moves an `ACTIVE` revision to `DEPRECATED`.
- **Rule 3 is the one it can break.** It may lock the revision like the publish, `FOR UPDATE`, or by its `UPDATE` alone. Either is safe only while it holds nothing first and takes nothing afterwards but roles.
- Plan 19 §5 row 5 leaves *"what it does to a document type still bound to the revision"* to its criteria. **If the route touches a bound type or its `document_type_workflows` row, it takes that type before the definition**, as the binding and the submit do. Taking the definition and then the type deadlocks with a submit of a document of that type: the submit holds the type and waits on the definition's key.
- **It must not lock an instance, a task or a document.** Running instances pin their revision. If the route counts them, `has_live_instances` is a plain read and should stay one.
- **Not a lock rule, but row 5's to decide.** `engine::start` reads the revision's status with no lock (`engine.rs:258`). A submit that read `ACTIVE` before a deprecate committed still starts its instance on it. With a plain `UPDATE`, the deprecate does not even make it wait, since `FOR KEY SHARE` and `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` do not conflict. If row 5's criteria say that *no instance starts on a revision once it is deprecated*, the start has to read the status under a lock. By rule 2 that lock comes after the type.
