**Finding 2 is the sprint's most valuable single output and it is worth saying why.** Every required context was green while `v0.7.0`'s release notes told a deployment a false thing about its own data, **because nothing in CI reads release notes against the behaviour they describe**. Eight sprints of a rule addressed to somebody's diligence found nothing of this kind. The first release that could not tag without a reader found it in one pass.

**Finding 4 is the sprint's most uncomfortable output** and is recorded here rather than left to `git log`. **§2's criterion is met for items 1, 3, 4 and 5 and #398 — every line of product code the sprint contains — and is not met for item 6 or the release run.** The decision taken is a **narrow re-read from a separate session**, appended to record 15; the exemption the record offered as an alternative was **not** taken. `releases_are_independently_verified.rs` cannot see any of this, because §2's criterion needs `git log` and CI clones at depth 1.

**The re-read ran on 2026-09-11** ([record 15](../verifications/15.%20Sprint%2016%20Independent%20Pass.md) §7), from a session in no commit trailer on `main`, and it widened by one commit because it had to: **[#410](https://github.com/sujanto-gaws/kelir/pull/410), the upgrade-note fix that discharged finding 2, was written by the same session after the pass and read by nobody.** It carried the re-read's one blocking finding — the rewritten note said ECMA-262's classes are ASCII, which is false for `\s` — and that is corrected in the change that adds §7. Three more go to Sprint 17: rule 2 is satisfied by any of **eight** never-cited verification records, so the gate did not force record 15; a release record copied from the template as its first line says is governed by nothing; and `header_status` documents an input no test sends. **`191662e`'s three recorded mutations all reproduce.**
